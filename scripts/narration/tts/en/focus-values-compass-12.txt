# focus-values-compass-12 · Your inner compass (EN)

[warm, calm] There are times when everything speeds up. The days blur together, the lists grow longer, and we run on autopilot. We do a lot, but we're no longer quite sure why.
This session is a pause to find your compass again. Not to add one more goal, but to remind yourself what truly matters to you. What gives you a direction, even when the path is unclear.
[pause 1.5s]
[clear] A quick note before we begin. A goal is something you tick off, like getting a degree or finishing a project. A value is a way of moving forward, like being caring, curious, or honest. You can live it today, in a tiny action, even when your goals are still far away.
[pause 2s]

[calm, unhurried] Take a moment to settle in. Sitting or lying down, in a comfortable position you can hold for a while. You might want a pen and paper nearby, for the end.
Feel the weight of your body, the places where it touches the floor or the seat. Let your shoulders drop, your jaw loosen. You don't have to produce anything in these minutes. Just listen to what comes.
Close your eyes if that feels good.
[pause 2s]
[soft] Let the sounds of the forest settle around you. The leaves, the birds, the air moving between the trees.
For these few minutes, the rest of your list can wait. It will still be there afterward, and you might see it differently.
[pause 2s]
Take a slightly deeper breath. Let the air fill your belly, then your chest...
[pause 3s]
and breathe out slowly, letting your body settle.
[pause 5s]
Once more. Breathe in...
[pause 3s]
and out, more slowly.
[pause 5s]
[calm] Now let your breath find its natural rhythm. Simply notice the movement of breathing, somewhere in your body. The belly, the chest, or the nostrils.
For the next few minutes, this breath will be your place to come back to. If you get lost in thought, or an emotion feels like too much, you can always return to it.
This isn't an exercise in hard thinking. Let the answers come on their own, rather than building them.
[pause 3s]

[warm, gentle] Now let a memory come up. A moment when you felt fully yourself. Alive, in the right place, in tune with who you are.
It doesn't have to be a big moment. It can be very simple. A conversation where you were truly honest. An afternoon spent helping someone. A project you threw yourself into, heart and soul. A walk with a friend. A time you dared to say no. A meal where you made everyone around the table laugh.
Let whatever memory comes, come. No need to look for the best one. The first one is often the right one.
[reassuring] If nothing comes, that's okay. Think of a recent moment when you were proud of yourself, not for a result, but for the way you acted. Or think of someone you admire. What touches you about them? Often, what we admire in others says something about our own values.
[pause 4s]
[soft] Now step into this memory. Where were you? What time of day was it, what season? Who was there?
[pause 3s]
Find the details again. The light, the sounds, the smells. What you were doing with your hands. What you were saying, or not saying.
And notice how you felt. Maybe a warmth in your chest, a sense of lightness, of momentum, of rightness.
Take your time. There's no rush to move on. Let the memory become as vivid as it wants to be.
Let those sensations come back a little, now, in your body.
[pause 5s]

[calm, clear] Now ask yourself what qualities were present in that moment. Not what you achieved, but the way you were.
Was there generosity? Courage? Curiosity? Honesty? Creativity, presence, humor, care for others, freedom, perseverance?
Was this moment about connection with others? About learning? Adventure? Fairness? Beauty?
[pause 2s]
Let two or three words come. The ones that ring true. Keep them in mind for later.
[pause 3s]
These qualities didn't depend only on the circumstances. They were in you that day, and they still are today, even if everyday life sometimes buries them.
You may already know them. But finding them again in a specific memory brings them back into focus, like a photo getting sharp again.
[pause 2s]
[soft] Now let this memory gently fade. Come back to your breath for a few breaths. Listen to the forest. Feel your body resting.
[pause 5s]
If your mind has wandered into the past, into regrets or comparisons, that's normal. Notice it, and come back to the breath, to the forest.
[pause 3s]

[warm, storytelling] Now let's take a little trip through time. It's an exercise of imagination. If at any point it feels uncomfortable, you can simply come back to your breath.
Imagine you're celebrating your eightieth birthday. You've lived a long life. The people who matter to you have gathered for the occasion. Maybe in a garden, a big hall, a family home. Choose the place you like.
See the scene. The tables, the light, the faces. Family, friends, maybe colleagues, people you've crossed paths with who made a difference.
Come closer. Notice the atmosphere. Laughter, glasses clinking, maybe some music. You're there, with a few more wrinkles, surrounded by people. Take the time to look at the faces, one by one.
[pause 4s]
At some point, someone stands up to speak about you. Someone who knows you well. They're going to say a few words about the person you've been.
Not about what you owned, or your titles, or your salary. About who you were for others, and for yourself.
[gentle] What would you like them to say? If you could choose their words, which would you choose?
[pause 2s]
Maybe, we could always count on you. Or, you never stopped learning. Or, with you, we felt free to be ourselves. Or, you did things with heart, even the small ones.
Or, you knew how to listen. You were there when things were hard.
Listen to what comes. Let this person speak, in your imagination.
[pause 4s]
Maybe someone else takes the floor. A child, a lifelong friend, a colleague. What do they say about you?
[pause 4s]
[soft, warm] Notice what it feels like in your body to hear these words. Maybe some emotion, a lump in your throat, a smile. Welcome whatever is here, gently.
[calm, clear] Notice, too, what you don't hear. People rarely wish to hear that they always answered their emails within the hour, or never took a day off. That contrast can be revealing. It shows the gap between what keeps us busy and what truly matters to us.
Day to day, the urgent tends to take up the most room. This moment is about giving the important its place back.
[pause 3s]

[calm] Now bring it all together. The memory from earlier, the qualities that were present. And the words you heard at this celebration.
What keeps coming back? What are the two or three words that sum up what matters most to you?
[pause 3s]
These are your values. Not rules to follow, or goals to tick off. More like directions. Like north on a compass. You never actually reach it, but it tells you which way to walk.
Choose your words. Two or three. Say them to yourself, slowly.
If several words are similar, group them together. If you're torn between two, keep both for now. There's no perfect list, and your values can change over the course of your life.
[pause 4s]
How does it feel to hear them? Do they ring true?
[pause 2s]
If one of them doesn't really speak to you, you can change it.
A value that fits often brings a small echo in the body, a sense of, yes, that's it.
[pause 3s]

[clear, warm] Now look at your current life in the light of these values. Your typical week. Your work, your relationships, your free time, the way you take care of yourself.
Where are you already living in line with them? Even a little. Take a moment to acknowledge that. It matters.
[pause 4s]
[gentle] And where has your life drifted away from them? Where have autopilot, stress, and obligations pulled you off course?
Look at this without judging yourself. With kindness, the way you would for a friend. Everyone drifts. It's human. Life moves fast, and the constraints are real.
If a harsh voice starts up, the one that says you should have, you can thank it, and come back to curiosity.
The gap isn't a failure. It's information. It shows you where you could come back to.
Maybe you care about connection with the people you love, but your evenings disappear in front of a screen, exhausted. Maybe you care about creativity, but your days leave no room to create. Simply notice. One or two places are enough.
[pause 4s]

[clear, encouraging] Now, one last question. What would be one small step, this week, to bring you closer to one of these values?
Not a life overhaul. A tiny, concrete step. Calling someone you love. Saying what you think in a meeting. Taking an hour for a project close to your heart. Turning down a request that doesn't fit you. Walking outside for twenty minutes, without your phone.
The smaller the step, the more likely it is to happen. And a step taken is worth more than a big plan you keep putting off.
What would your step be? Choose just one, something you can do in the next seven days.
[pause 4s]
Imagine yourself doing it. Where, when, with whom. Make it concrete.
And if an obstacle comes up, tiredness, lack of time, the fear of bothering someone, what could you do? Plan an even smaller version, just in case. A message instead of a call. Ten minutes instead of an hour.
And notice how it feels to see yourself acting on what matters to you.
[pause 4s]

[soft, warm] Now come back to your breath. Let everything that came up settle inside you.
[pause 3s]
Notice how you feel now. Maybe calmer, more aligned, or a little stirred up. All of it is welcome. Reconnecting with what matters can be moving.
[warm, grounded] Your compass is here. It has always been here, even when you weren't looking at it. On overloaded days, you can ask yourself, what really matters, here, right now? No need to turn your life upside down. One direction, one step, then another.
These values help you focus, too. When everything seems urgent, they help you choose what to do first, and what to say no to.
