# work-after-meeting-5 · After a tense meeting (EN)
[pause 1.5s]
[warm, calm] The meeting is over. The door is closed, the call has ended. You're here now, and for the next five minutes, nobody needs anything from you.
If you can, step away from your desk for a moment. A hallway, an empty meeting room, even the restroom will do.
[pause 2s]
[grounded] Sit or stand with your feet firmly on the floor. Feel the ground holding you up. Solid. Completely indifferent to whatever was just said.
[pause 1.5s]
[soft, almost a sigh] Now let out a first breath, a little longer than usual... like a sigh you've been holding in for an hour.
[pause 4s]

[calm] Let's take a quick tour of your body, to see where that meeting has settled.
Start with your jaw. Are your teeth clenched right now?
[pause 2.5s]
Maybe your tongue is pressed hard against the roof of your mouth. Let a little space open between your teeth, and let your tongue rest softly, low in your mouth.
[pause 2s]
Move down to your shoulders. They may have crept up during the discussion, as if to protect you. Let them drop half an inch... then a little more.
[pause 2s]
And your belly. In a tense exchange, we often pull the stomach in without noticing. Let it soften, let it round out.
[pause 2s]
Look at your hands, too. Are they gripping a pen, or your phone? Open them wide, fingers spread, then let them rest.
[pause 2s]
[reassuring] Not everything has to relax at once. Simply noticing the tension is already the start of letting it go.
[pause 2.5s]

[clear] What lingers in the body after a hard meeting is energy that had nowhere to go. Your body got ready to react. So let's help it release some of that charge.
Gently shake out your hands, as if you were drying them after washing. Loose wrists, soft fingers.
[pause 2s]
Let the movement travel up into your forearms, then your shoulders. Nothing dramatic. Just enough to get things moving.
[pause 2s]
Keep going a few more seconds. You can shrug a few times too, or march gently in place if there's room.
[pause 4s]
And stop. Let your hands hang, or rest them on your thighs. Notice the tingling, the warmth, the blood moving again.
[pause 3s]

[calm, unhurried] Now, a few long exhales. Breathe in easily through your nose...
[pause 3s]
[slow] Then out through your mouth, lips slightly parted, slowly, all the way to the end.
[pause 6s]
[soft] Once more. The inhale is ordinary...
[pause 3s]
[slow] it's the exhale that takes its time.
[pause 6s]
[soft] One last time. Breathe in...
[pause 3s]
[slow, almost a sigh] and as you breathe out, imagine the air carrying away some of the heat of that discussion.
[pause 6s]
[gentle] If you feel a little dizzy, just go back to your natural breathing.
[pause 2s]

[calm] Your mind may already be replaying the scene. A curt remark, a look, the perfect comeback that shows up too late.
That's normal. Your brain is trying to make sense of what happened, and to protect you next time.
You don't have to stop it. You can simply notice it... there it goes again, the replay.
[pause 2s]
[unhurried] Picture those exchanges on a screen at the far end of a theater. You're sitting in the very back row. You can see them, but you're no longer on stage.
[pause 2.5s]
If one sentence keeps looping, give it a simple label. Replaying. Then come back to the feeling of your feet on the floor.
You don't need to decide right now who was right. That debate can wait. For now, you're just watching from the back row.
[pause 3s]

[clear] Now, out of everything that was said, try to find what actually matters.
Not the tone. Not the way it was said. The substance.
A decision that was made, a fair concern, a deadline to meet, a point you genuinely disagree with.
Once you peel away the emotional wrapping, there are often only one or two concrete things left. What are they, for you?
[pause 3s]
[warm] Good. All that's left is to choose what comes next.
Maybe one action is clear. Send a recap, talk to a colleague, write down a question for later.
Or maybe the best action is not to respond right away... to sleep on it before you write that email.
Both are choices. What matters is that you're the one deciding, not the adrenaline.
[pause 1.5s]
[gentle] If you feel the urge to reply right now, in the heat of the moment, that's often a sign it's wiser to wait a little.
[pause 1.5s]
[clear, grounded] Choose your next step. Just one. Put it clearly into words, in your head.
For example, I'll reply tomorrow morning, with a clear head. Or, I'll stop by Julie's desk before the end of the day.
[pause 3s]
[softer, slowing down] Let your breathing return to normal. Notice whether anything has shifted in your jaw, your shoulders, your belly. Even slightly.
[pause 3s]
[warm, calm] You can head back now, with a little more space between you and that meeting.
[pause 4s]
