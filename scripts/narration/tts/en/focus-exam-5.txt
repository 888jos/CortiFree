# focus-exam-5 · Right before an exam (EN)
[pause 1.5s]
[calm, reassuring] Maybe you're in the hallway, on a bench, or already sitting at your desk in the exam room. Your heart is racing, and your head feels full and empty at the same time.
That's very common before an exam. Let's work with it together.
[pause 2s]
[clear, grounded] You can keep your eyes open. Rest your gaze on one fixed point in front of you. The edge of a table, a wall, your hands.
Feel both feet flat on the floor. Press them down a little, as if to feel the ground holding you up. It's there, and it's solid.
Rest your hands on your thighs or on the table, too. Notice their weight, and the temperature under your palms.
[pause 2s]
[calm] Right here, right now, there's nothing else to do but breathe and feel these points of contact.
[pause 2s]

[calm, clear] Now let's work with the out-breath. No need to breathe in hard. Let the air come in through your nose, normally.
Then breathe out slowly through your mouth, a little longer than the in-breath. Like a long, quiet sigh.
[pause 5s]
Once more. Breathe in easily...
[pause 3s]
[slow] and let the out-breath stretch all the way to the end.
[pause 5s]
[soft] With each out-breath, let your shoulders come down. They may have crept up toward your ears without you noticing. Let them drop.
Unclench your jaw as well. Leave a little space between your teeth.
[pause 2s]
Keep going at your own pace for a few breaths, the out-breath longer than the in-breath.
If it helps, think of a short in-breath and a longer out-breath, or less, whatever feels comfortable. What matters is that the breath out takes a little more time.
[gentle] If you feel a little lightheaded, simply go back to your natural breathing. There's nothing to force here.
[pause 6s]

[warm, clear] Let's talk about that pounding heart. It isn't an alarm bell, and it isn't proof that you're going to fail. It's your body gearing up. It's sending you extra energy, because this moment matters to you.
You don't need to switch that activation off. A bit of drive is actually useful for staying sharp during the exam. We just want to bring it down to a level where you can think clearly.
You can tell yourself, silently, my body is getting ready, and it's on my side.
[pause 3s]

[warm, reassuring] Now, an important reminder. You've done the work. The hours of revision, the flashcards, the practice questions. All of that has settled in somewhere.
Right before an exam, a lot of people feel as if they've forgotten everything. It's a classic stress reaction, and it doesn't reflect what you actually know.
Your knowledge hasn't gone anywhere. It's stored away, and it often comes back once you read the first question and your mind has something concrete to hold on to.
So there's no need to recheck everything now. You've done your part. You can trust yourself.
[pause 2s]

[clear] When you get the paper, don't rush in. Take one slow out-breath first. Read through the whole thing once, calmly, without trying to answer yet. Spot the questions that feel most familiar.
Then start with one of those. Writing something you know well gets things moving, and the rest often comes more easily.
[pause 1.5s]
And what if your mind goes blank during the exam? Let's prepare your backup plan right now.
First, you put your pen down. You feel your feet on the floor, and you take two long out-breaths, just like now.
Next, you move on to the next question, or to a section you know better. You keep going where it's easier.
Finally, you come back to the tricky question later. Often, the answer has come back in the meantime.
[calm, reassuring] A blank is not a disaster. It's a moment, and it passes. Now you know what to do.
Say the plan to yourself in three words. Breathe, skip, return.
[pause 3s]

[warm] Now choose an intention for the exam. Not a result, but a way of being. For example, I read each question calmly. Or, I do my best, one question at a time. Or simply, I stay with the page in front of me.
Find yours. One short sentence you can come back to during the exam.
[pause 4s]

[calm, grounded] Take one last long out-breath. Feel your feet, your hands, your back.
[pause 4s]
Look around you. The others are probably just as nervous as you are. Everyone in this room is sharing this moment with you.
You don't have to be perfectly calm to do well. You just need to be present enough to read, think and write.
[warm, encouraging] Your body is ready, your preparation is there, and you have a plan if you get stuck. The rest will happen on the page.
Go for it.
