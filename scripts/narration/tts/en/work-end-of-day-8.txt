# work-end-of-day-8 · Close the workday (EN)

[warm, calm] Your workday is coming to an end. What if, tonight, you truly closed it, instead of letting it spill over into your evening?
It matters even more when you work from home, when your office and your living room are the same space.
[pause 2s]
[soft, unhurried] Settle in at your workspace, one last time today. Feet on the floor, hands resting on your thighs.

Your head may still be full. Bits of conversations, numbers, things you mustn't forget. That's normal, after a whole day.
We're not going to empty it all at once. We're just going to tidy up, the way you'd clear a desk in the evening.
Think of these minutes as a bridge between your work self and the rest of your life.

[soft] Take an easy breath in through your nose...

Now, for a few minutes, you're turning back toward yourself.
Let your hands grow heavy on your thighs. Let your jaw loosen. These are the first signals to your body that the effort can stop.

[warm, clear] Let's start by reviewing your day. Not to judge it, but to recognize what you did.
Look for three things you got done today. Just three. They can be very small.
A difficult email, finally sent. A problem solved. A colleague you really listened to. A boring task, crossed off the list.
Find the first one. Replay it for a moment. And acknowledge it. That's done.

[warm] What does it feel like to count what got done, rather than what's missing? We do it so rarely.

These three things also say something about you. Your persistence, your attention to detail, your kindness toward others. Those qualities were there all day, even when you couldn't see them.
Take a moment to let them really count. No need for big pride. Just a simple thank-you to yourself for the effort.

[calm, clear] Of course, there are things left. There always are. Tasks half-finished, replies pending, projects that won't budge.
Your mind tends to keep them open, like browser tabs running in the background all evening.
Let's close them, one by one. Picture tomorrow's list. A sheet of paper, a notebook, or a screen, whatever you prefer.
Take the first thing that's left, and write it on that list in your mind. Just a few words.
[pause 3s]
Then the next one. And the one after that. Take your time. You don't need to solve them. Just write them down.
Each time you add one, you can tell yourself... it's noted, I'll deal with it tomorrow.
[pause 3s]
Writing a task down somewhere, even in your mind, gets it out of your head a little. Your mind no longer has to keep repeating it so you won't forget.
Tomorrow, you'll be fresher, and better placed to handle it.
If there are a lot, that's normal. The list can be long. It'll be waiting for you tomorrow morning, and you'll come back to it with a rested mind.
And if one thing is truly urgent for tomorrow, circle it in your mind. You'll know where to start, without having to think about it again tonight.
If you like, you can write the list for real after this session. It often helps the mind let go.

[calm, intimate] Now, there may be something else. Not a task, but something difficult. Tension with someone, a frustration, a worry.
Choose one, not the heaviest. Just the one that's still with you right now.
Acknowledge it. You can tell yourself... yes, today, that was hard.
And maybe ask yourself, did I do my best, with what I had today? Often, the answer is yes. Even if the result wasn't perfect.

[gentle] That difficulty belongs to work. Its place is at the office. Not in your kitchen, not in your bed.

What changes in your shoulders, in your chest, when you don't carry it with you?

[clear, grounded] It's time to mark the end. A concrete gesture, to tell your body the day is over.
When you work from home, this is often what's missing. No door to walk through, no commute, no colleagues saying goodnight. So it's up to you to create that boundary.
If you can, open your eyes. Save whatever needs saving. Then close your laptop. Listen to the soft click of the lid.
