# work-before-presentation-5 · Before you speak up (EN)
[pause 1.5s]
[warm, calm] In a few minutes, you're going to speak. A presentation, an interview, or just that point you need to make in a meeting.
And your body already knows it. Keep your eyes open. We're going to work with it, not against it.
[pause 2s]
[calm, clear] Start by noticing what's happening, without changing anything. Is your heart beating faster, harder?
[pause 2s]
Your hands might be cold, or a little clammy. In your stomach, maybe there's that small knot, that fluttering.
That's stage fright. Almost everyone feels it, including the people who look perfectly at ease up there.
[reassuring] You don't need to make it disappear. That would be hard anyway, just minutes before you go on.
These few minutes are for something else. Turning that energy into an ally instead of an enemy.
[pause 3s]

[warm, clear] Here's another way to see it. That racing heart is sending more oxygen to your brain and your muscles.
Your body isn't betraying you. It's getting ready, because this moment matters to you.
It's the same energy as the excitement before setting off on a trip. The same chemistry, with a different label.
You can say it to yourself. My body is getting ready. This energy is here to help me.
Some studies suggest that simply relabeling nerves as excitement can help people perform better than trying to force themselves to calm down.
[pause 3s]

[grounded] Now, your feet. Place them flat, about hip width apart. If you're standing, spread your weight evenly across both legs, knees soft.
Feel your heels, your toes, the whole sole of each foot. Press very gently into the floor, as if to feel it holding you.
[pause 2s]
Then grow a little taller. Imagine a thread gently drawing the top of your head toward the ceiling.
Open your chest. Let your shoulders drop and widen, away from your ears.
Unclench your hands. Let your arms hang freely, or rest your hands, palms open.
[pause 2s]
If you're somewhere private, you can even raise your arms for a few seconds, like a runner crossing the finish line. Then let them float back down.
[pause 2s]
[warm] This is the posture of someone who has room to be here. You're allowed to take up space.
An open posture helps your breathing too. Your ribcage has room, and the breath drops more easily.
[pause 2s]

[calm, clear] Now the breath, which will steady your voice. Place one hand on your belly, just below your navel.
Breathe in through your nose and let your belly press gently into your hand...
[pause 3s]
[slow] Then breathe out through your mouth, slowly, longer than the in breath. Your belly sinks back.
[pause 6s]
[soft] Again. A calm breath in, about four seconds...
[pause 4s]
[slow] And out, about six, without straining.
[pause 6s]
[soft] A third time. Breathe in...
[pause 3s]
[slow] And let your throat open as you breathe out, like a quiet, hidden yawn.
[pause 6s]
[gentle] If you feel any discomfort or lightheadedness, go back to your natural breathing. That's perfectly fine too.
[clear] If you can, hum softly on the next out breath, lips closed. A gentle mmm.
[pause 5s]
Can you feel the vibration in your lips, your nose, your chest? Your voice is right there, warm and grounded.
[pause 2s]
A steady voice comes from a low breath and a relaxed throat. That's why we take this time, however short.
[pause 2s]

[calm, unhurried] Now picture your entrance. Just the first few seconds.
You walk into the room, or the camera turns on. You see the faces, the screen, the table.
You take your place. Your feet find the floor, just like now.
You let one breath pass before you speak. That short silence isn't empty. It's you setting the pace.
And you say your first sentence. Slowly. Hear it in your head, in your steady voice.
[pause 3s]
[reassuring] Picture a small hiccup, too. A word that slips away, a slide that won't load.
You pause, you breathe, you pick it back up. It happens to everyone, and it's easy to recover from.
[pause 2s]
[calm] Run through that entrance one more time, even more calmly.
This time, notice your easy breath and your low shoulders as you speak.
[pause 5s]

[warm, clear] Finally, choose an intention. One thing you want people to remember.
Not all of your content. The heart of your message, in one simple sentence.
Say it to yourself now, as if you were explaining it to a friend over coffee.
[pause 4s]
If you lose your thread while you're speaking, that's where you'll come back to. It's your anchor.
And remember, the people listening aren't looking for perfection. They want to understand what you have to say.
[pause 2s]
[warm, encouraging] Your heart may still be racing, and that's fine. Your body is ready, your feet are grounded, your voice is there, and so is your message.
One last long out breath.
[pause 5s]
[warm, grounded] This is your moment. Go for it.
[pause 4s]
