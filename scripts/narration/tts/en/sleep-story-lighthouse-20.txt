# sleep-story-lighthouse-20 · The lighthouse keeper (EN)
[pause 1.5s]
[warm, soft, unhurried] Settle in comfortably, in the position you like to fall asleep in. Pull up the duvet, and let your head sink into the pillow.
Tonight, I'm going to tell you a story. A very simple story, where almost nothing happens.
[pause 2s]
You don't need to follow it to the end. You don't need to remember any of it.
If your attention drifts away, let it go. If you fall asleep in the middle, that's just as it should be.
Simply let my voice keep you company, like the sound of the sea heard from far off, through an open window.
[pause 4s]
[storytelling, calm] It's the end of a September day, on the coast of Brittany.
The sky is clear. It rained a little in the morning, and now the air is rinsed clean, very bright, with that smell of salt and seaweed you only find by the ocean.
On the slipway of the little harbour, a man is waiting, a canvas bag at his feet. His name is Loïc. He's a lighthouse keeper.
Tonight, he's going back to his island, for a week on watch.
[pause 2s]
The ferryman arrives in his boat, an old wooden launch painted blue, the paint flaking a little at the edges. The engine makes a steady, reassuring sound. Put, put, put.
The two men greet each other with a nod. They've known each other a long time. They don't need to say much.
Loïc steps aboard. He sits at the back, his bag between his knees, and the boat slips gently out of the harbour.
[pause 2s]
The sea is calm. Just a long, lazy swell that lifts the boat, lets it down, and lifts it again.
The wooden bench is warm beneath him, heated by the afternoon sun. Through the soles of his boots, he can feel the slow, steady hum of the engine.
Behind them, the village grows smaller. White houses with blue shutters, the grey church tower, small boats resting on the sand at low tide. Everything becomes small, then distant, then blurred.
The gulls circle above the boat, then drift away toward the rocks. Their cries grow further and further off.
[pause 2s]
Loïc trails his hand over the side. The water is cool, almost cold.
It slips between his fingers, lively and salty. He watches the little drops sparkle in the sun as he lifts his hand out.
The ferryman holds the tiller without a word, eyes narrowed against the light. He knows every rock, every current. He could make the crossing with his eyes closed.
[pause 2s]
Ahead of them, the island draws near.
It's a tiny island, hardly bigger than a field. A lump of pink granite, covered in short grass and heather. And right at the far end, on the point, the lighthouse.
It's tall and white, with a red band at the top. At this hour, the low sun lights it from the side, and it looks almost golden.
The sun sinks slowly toward the horizon. The light turns warm and thick, the colour of honey. It settles on the water in millions of tiny dancing sparkles.
[pause 2s]
The boat draws up against a small stone jetty. Loïc steps onto the damp steps, slippery with kelp. The ferryman hands him his bag.
See you next week, says the ferryman.
See you next week, says Loïc.
And the boat heads off again, put, put, put, drawing a white wake across the golden sea. Loïc watches it go for a while. Then he turns toward the island.
[pause 3s]
A little dirt path climbs between the grasses. It's edged with flat stones, laid there long ago by other keepers.
Loïc walks slowly. He's in no hurry. He has the whole evening ahead of him.
The wind has dropped. All there is to hear is his footsteps on the path, the rustle of the grass, and the sea, all around, breathing against the rocks.
The air smells of sun-warmed heather, and of salt. Now and then a rabbit darts between the tufts of grass, then stops, and watches him go by.
[pause 2s]
At the foot of the lighthouse stands the keeper's cottage. A small, low house of granite, with a slate roof and a door painted green.
Loïc takes a big key from his pocket. The lock is a little stiff.
It has to be turned twice, with the handle lifted slightly. He knows the movement by heart.
The door opens with a small creak.
[pause 2s]
Inside, it smells of wood, of wax, and a little of a house left shut. Loïc opens the kitchen shutters. The evening light pours in, all orange, and settles on the wooden table.
He puts his bag down on a chair. He takes off his jacket and hangs it on the hook behind the door.
Then he kneels in front of the cast-iron stove, in the corner of the room.
[pause 2s]
He opens the little black door. Inside, there's still some grey ash from last week. He pats it down with the poker.
He takes a few sheets of newspaper from the basket, crumples them into balls, one by one, and lays them at the bottom. On top he places dry twigs, criss-crossed, like a little hut. Then two logs of oak, not too big.
He strikes a match. The small yellow flame trembles for a moment, then catches the paper.
The newspaper flares, curls, turns orange. The twigs crackle. And soon, the logs begin to sing softly.
[pause 2s]
Loïc closes the stove door, leaving the vent a little open. He stays crouched for a moment, watching the flames through the small glass window. He can already feel the warmth on his face.
Next, he fills the kettle at the tap. The water comes in fits and starts, a little cloudy at first, then clear and cold. It's rainwater from the cistern.
He sets the kettle on the stove.
[pause 2s]
While he waits, he takes out what he's brought for the week. A loaf of bread. A piece of salted butter wrapped in paper.
Some apples. A packet of tea. A few books.
He puts each thing in its place, unhurried. The bread in the bread bin. The butter in the larder, where it's cool.
The apples in a bowl on the table. The books on the shelf, beside the others.
[pause 2s]
The kettle begins to murmur. Then to whistle softly.
Loïc takes his mug, a big earthenware mug, chipped at the rim, that has always lived here. He drops in a pinch of black tea. He pours the hot water.
A fragrant steam rises to his face.
He sits at the table by the window, and holds the mug in both hands to warm them.
[pause 3s]
Outside, the sun is now touching the horizon. It turns red, then a deep orange. The sea beneath it takes on shades of pink and violet.
Loïc drinks his tea in small sips. It's hot, a little strong. He cuts a slice of bread, butters it generously, and eats it slowly while he watches the sky change colour.
The stove purrs. A log settles with a small, soft thud. The kitchen is warm now.
[pause 2s]
The sun disappears. There's only a strip of orange light left along the water, growing thinner, and thinner still.
Soon it will be time.
Loïc rinses his mug and sets it upside down on the draining board. He takes his torch from the shelf. He puts his jacket back on.
And he goes out, to climb the lighthouse.
[pause 5s]
[storytelling, unhurried] The lighthouse is right next door, a few steps away. Its door is made of thick wood, strengthened with black iron.
Loïc pushes it. It opens slowly, heavily, and cool mineral air comes to meet him. The smell of stone, slightly damp, and of machine oil.
Inside, it's almost dark. Only a small window, very high up, lets in a last trace of blue light.
Loïc switches on his torch. The circle of light lands on the first steps.
[pause 2s]
The staircase spirals upward, around a central pillar. The steps are granite, worn down in the middle by the feet of all the keepers who came before him. A little hollowed, a little polished, like pebbles.
A brass handrail follows the curve of the wall. It's cool and smooth under his hand.
Loïc sets his foot on the first step. Then on the second.
[pause 2s]
He climbs slowly. One step, then another.
He knows the number by heart, a hundred and twenty-eight steps. But tonight he doesn't count. He simply climbs.
The sound of his footsteps echoes softly off the walls. A small, round echo that rises with him.
One step. Then another.
[pause 2s]
The pillar turns beside him, always on his left. The handrail slides under his right hand.
Every so often he passes a small square window, set deep into the thickness of the wall. Through the first, he sees the keeper's cottage, its slate roof, the smoke from the stove rising straight up into the still air.
He carries on. One step, then another.
[pause 2s]
Through the second window, he sees the whole island. The heather, the little path, the jetty. And the sea all around, a deeper and deeper blue.
He carries on. His breathing is slow and steady.
His legs know the way. He doesn't need to think.
One step, then another. The spiral turns, and turns again. The torchlight dances on the stone.
[pause 2s]
Through the third window, there's almost nothing but sky. A midnight-blue sky, where the first star appears. Just one, all alone, above the horizon.
He carries on. The steps grow a little narrower as he nears the top.
The wall comes closer. The air grows cooler.
One step. Then another. Then the last.
[pause 2s]
Loïc pushes open a small wooden hatch above his head. And he climbs into the lantern room.
It's a round room, glass on every side. All around, there's nothing but sky and sea.
Nothing else. As far as the eye can see.
[pause 2s]
In the centre stands the great lens of the lighthouse. It's enormous, as tall as a man, made of dozens of rings of cut glass, layered like the petals of a flower. Even unlit, it catches the last glints of the day and makes them shimmer softly.
Loïc sets down his torch. He takes off his jacket and folds it on the little bench.
Then he begins the evening routine. The same tasks every evening, in the same order.
[pause 2s]
He takes a soft cloth from the drawer. He wipes the windows of the lantern, one by one, working his way around the room. He removes the salt the wind has left during the day.
One pane. Then the next. Then the next.
Then he wipes the lens. Delicately, from top to bottom, following the curve of each glass ring. The cloth glides without a sound.
He checks the oil level in the mechanism. He winds up the counterweight a little. He listens to the faint ticking of the gears.
Everything is in order. Everything is ready.
[pause 3s]
Outside, the sky is now a deep blue, almost black in the east. In the west there's still a pale glow, green and gold, just above the sea.
Loïc looks at his watch. It's time.
He turns the little copper switch.
[pause 2s]
At first, nothing happens. Then comes a faint hum. And at the heart of the lens, the lamp comes on.
The light grows, white and warm. It passes through the rings of glass, which gather it, focus it, and send it far away, in a great beam reaching out over the sea.
Then the mechanism starts up with a small click. And the lens begins to turn.
Slowly. Very slowly. Without a sound, or almost.
[pause 3s]
[soft, storytelling] The beam sweeps across the sea. It passes over the dark water, slides toward the horizon, then swings back toward the coast, lights up the island's rocks for a moment, and sets off out to sea again.
One turn. Then another. Then another still.
Loïc sits on the little bench, against the glass. He watches.
The beam turns above his head. It passes, it goes, it comes back.
Always at the same pace. Steady as a breath.
[pause 3s]
Night has fallen now. A real night, deep and full of stars. There are hundreds of them.
Thousands. The longer you look, the more appear.
The sea is black and calm. Its surface shows only in the small glints when the lighthouse beam passes over it.
[pause 2s]
Far away, very far, there's a ship. All there is to see are its lights.
A small white light at the top of the mast, a green light on the side. It moves gently, unhurried, toward the south.
Loïc watches it for a long time. The ship can see the lighthouse too. It knows where it is.
It knows where the rocks are. It can carry on its way, untroubled.
[pause 3s]
Further still, along the coast, there's the light of another lighthouse. A small white flash, returning every ten seconds. Like an old friend waving from the other side of the bay.
The beam turns. It passes, it goes, it comes back.
A little later, a small trawler heads home to the harbour, across the bay. Its lights move slowly over the black water. Carried on the still air, there's almost the hum of its engine.
The fishermen are going home. Soon they'll be in bed too, in the houses with the blue shutters.
[pause 3s]
Loïc writes in the big logbook. Clear sky, calm sea, no wind, one vessel offshore, heading south. His handwriting is slow and careful. The pen scratches the paper a little.
Then he closes the logbook, and stays a while longer, looking at the night.
[pause 3s]
[slower, soothing] In the east, behind the coast, the moon is rising. It's nearly full, a little orange at first, huge just above the hills. Then, as it climbs, it pales, turns white, then silver.
Its light settles on the sea. A long silver path stretches across the water, from the horizon right up to the island's rocks. It trembles a little, in time with the swell.
The lighthouse beam crosses this moon path on every turn. A white light that turns, a silver light that stays still. And between the two, the sea, vast and calm.
[pause 3s]
There's nothing else to do. The lighthouse does its work.
The light turns by itself. The sea breathes below, against the rocks.
[pause 3s]
After a while, Loïc stands. He shrugs his jacket back on. He takes one last look at the lens, turning, bright and calm.
Then he opens the hatch, and goes back down.
One step, then another. The spiral turns the other way this time. The brass rail slides under his left hand.
[pause 2s]
He goes down slowly. The small windows pass one by one, all dark now. In each, now and then, the glow of the beam passes, and is gone.
His footsteps are softer now. As if the staircase itself had fallen asleep.
One step, then another. At the bottom, the door.
The path. The cool night air, smelling of salt and heather.
Above him, the beam sweeps the sky, slowly, silently.
[pause 3s]
Loïc goes back into the cottage. It's cosy.
The stove has warmed the kitchen through. He adds one last log, and closes the vent, so the fire will burn gently until morning.
He turns off the kitchen lamp.
He climbs the little wooden staircase to his bedroom, under the roof.
[pause 2s]
It's a small, simple room. A wooden bed. A thick, checked wool blanket.
A bedside table with a candle and a book. A round window, like a porthole, looking out over the sea.
Loïc undresses and folds his clothes on the chair. He pulls on an old wool jumper that smells of wood smoke.
[pause 2s]
He slips under the sheets. They're cool at first. Then, very quickly, they warm up.
The wool of the blanket is a little rough under his fingers. It smells of soap, and of the sea wind that dried it on the line last summer.
He blows out the candle.
[pause 3s]
[soft, slow, quieter] In the dark, he listens.
The waves. Down below, against the rocks.
They come in, they draw back. They come in, they draw back.
[pause 3s]
Through the round window, the glow of the lighthouse passes over the ceiling. A long streak of pale light that glides... and fades. Then comes back.
The waves come in... they draw back.
[pause 3s]
Down on the little shingle beach, the pebbles roll softly as the water pulls away. A gentle sound, like a string of beads slipping slowly through someone's fingers.
The blanket is heavy and warm.
The stove creaks softly, downstairs.
The light passes over the ceiling... and goes.
[pause 4s]
[slower, soothing] Outside, the whole island is asleep.
The little path edged with flat stones, silvered by the moon.
The tufts of heather, perfectly still. The rabbits, curled up deep in their burrows, ears folded along their backs.
The stone jetty, where the water rises gently with the tide, step by step, without a sound.
[pause 3s]
On the rocks, the seaweed lifts and settles, slowly, in time with the swell. It gleams for a moment whenever the lighthouse beam passes over it.
Across the bay, the village is asleep too. The blue shutters are closed.
The church tower stands in the night, grey on black. In the harbour, the small boats rock at the end of their moorings.
[pause 3s]
The ferryman is asleep in his house by the water. His old blue launch is tied to the quay, and the swell rocks it, very slowly.
And in the middle of it all, the lighthouse keeps watch. Its light turns, and turns again, above the houses, above the boats, above the sea. So that everyone can sleep in peace.
[pause 4s]
[almost whispering, slower] Loïc closes his eyes.
[pause 3s]
The waves... against the rocks...
[pause 3s]
The light turning... up above...
[pause 3s]
The ship, far away... going on its way...
[pause 3s]
The stars... thousands of them...
[pause 4s]
The sea, breathing...
[pause 4s]
Softly...
[pause 4s]
Slowly...
