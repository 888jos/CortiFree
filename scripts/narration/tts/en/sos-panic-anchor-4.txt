[calm, grounded, reassuring] I'm here with you. Keep your eyes open, and stay with my voice.
What you're feeling is very unpleasant. It isn't dangerous, and it will pass.

[clear, grounded] Let's start with your feet. Place them flat on the floor. If you're sitting, feel the seat under you too. If you're standing, let your knees bend very slightly.
Now press down through your heels. The floor is solid. It isn't moving. It holds you, whatever your body is doing right now.
[pause 2s]
Bring your hands together, palm against palm, in front of your chest or resting on your knees.
Push them firmly into each other. Feel the strength in your arms, and the warmth building between your palms.
[pause 3s]
[calm] And release. Keep your hands together, just more gently now. Your feet and your hands are your anchors.
[pause 1.5s]

[clear] Now look around you. Let your eyes move slowly around the room, or the street.
Pick one object and name it, under your breath if you can. Just its name. A chair. A window. A bag.
[pause 2s]
Now a second object. Notice its color as you name it.
[pause 2s]
And a third one. Take your time, there's no right answer.
[pause 2s]
[reassuring] Naming what you see helps your attention come back here, to this place, and away from the alarm.
[pause 2s]

[calm, unhurried] Now, the breath. You don't need a big breath in. Let the air come in however it comes.
Then blow out through your mouth, slowly, as if onto a candle you don't want to put out...
[pause 5s]
Once more. The air comes in on its own... and you let it flow out through your mouth, for a long time.
[pause 6s]
[reassuring] It's the exhale that matters. The inhale takes care of itself, you don't have to reach for it.
Feeling short of air is very common in moments like this. You are breathing. The air is moving.
[pause 1.5s]
[soft] One more exhale, soft and long, like a thin stream of air over the flame...
[pause 6s]

[calm, storytelling] Now picture a wave. It rises, it swells, it can look very high.
Then it reaches its peak. And like every wave, it starts to come back down.
What you're going through follows the same movement. You may be right at the top, or already on the way down.
[gentle, reassuring] You don't have to stop the wave. You only have to stay on your feet while it passes.
[pause 3s]

[calm] Some sensations may still be there. A racing heart. Heat, tingling, a tight throat.
These are your body's alarm signals. They're loud, and they aren't dangerous. They rise, and then they settle on their own.
You don't need to fight them. Let them sit beside you while you keep breathing out, long and slow.
[pause 3s]

[grounded, clear] Come back to your feet. Press down into the floor a little.
Your hands are still together. Press a bit harder if it helps you come back here.
Find one object in front of you and name it once more. And one long exhale, through your mouth...
[pause 5s]
[warm] You're still here. You're getting through this, one exhale at a time.
Is anything different, even slightly? Your breath a little less short, your shoulders a little lower?
[pause 3s]
Or maybe not yet, and that's okay too.
[pause 2s]

[warm, gentle] You've made it through this wave, and that takes a lot of energy. You can stay seated a while longer, drink a glass of water, take things slowly.
Feeling tired afterward, with legs a bit wobbly, is common. Your body gave a lot, and now it's recovering.
[calm, clear] If these attacks come back often, talk to a doctor or a health professional. Real help exists, and no one should have to handle this alone.
[pause 2s]
[soft, warm, slowing down] For now, one last long exhale. Then go on at your own pace, with your feet firmly on the ground.
