# focus-single-point-6 · Single-point focus (EN)
[pause 1.5s]
[calm, clear] This is one of the simplest attention exercises there is. No visualization, no images to build. Just breaths, and numbers.
[pause 1.5s]
Sit in a steady position. Your back upright without being stiff, as if a thread were gently drawing the top of your head toward the ceiling. Your hands resting on your thighs.
Close your eyes if you like, or keep a soft, lowered gaze, resting about three feet in front of you.
Let the background sound wrap around you. It works like a curtain between you and everything else.
[pause 2s]
[soft] Take two slightly deeper breaths, to tell your body it's time to stop for a moment. Let the air come in fully...
[pause 3s]
and leave without hurrying.
[pause 5s]
[calm] Then let your breathing find its natural rhythm again. You're not going to control it. You're only going to count it.
[pause 1.5s]
First, find the place where you feel it most clearly. The tip of your nose, your chest, or your belly. Choose just one, and stay with it for the whole session.
[pause 2s]

[clear] Here's the instruction. With each out-breath, you silently count one number. The first out-breath is one. The next is two. And so on, up to ten.
When you reach ten, you start again at one.
The in-breath gets no number. It's a small neutral space, where you simply feel the air coming in.
The number lands on the out-breath, gently, like a pebble settling at the bottom of a pond.
[pause 1.5s]
[clear, reassuring] And here's the most important rule. If you lose count, if you find yourself at fourteen or twenty-three, or you have no idea where you are, you go back to one. No commentary, no sigh.
Losing count isn't a failure. It's information. Your mind wandered off, and you've just noticed. That moment is exactly what we're training.
[pause 2s]

[warm] Let's do a first round together. I'll count with you, and you keep your own pace.
Breathe in... and on the out-breath, one.
[pause 3s]
Two.
[pause 3s]
Three.
[pause 3s]
Four. Really feel the end of the out-breath, the moment the air comes to rest.
[pause 3s]
Five. Let the in-breath come on its own before six.
[pause 3s]
[calm] I'll leave six, seven and eight to you. Your rhythm, not mine.
Keep going at your own pace up to ten, then come back to one.
[pause 4s]
Notice the quality of the counting. It can be very light, almost whispered inside. There's no need to say the numbers with force.
Some people like to see the number, others to hear it, others to feel it. Go with whatever comes naturally.
If your breathing speeds up or slows down, let it. The counting follows the breath, never the other way around.
[pause 4s]
Keep going. One out-breath, one number.
If you feel you're counting too fast, you're probably setting the number down before the breath has finished. Wait until the out-breath is complete, then count.
[pause 4s]
[gentle] Where are you now?
[pause 2s]
The number doesn't matter. If you know where you are, carry on. If you don't, go back to one.
Maybe you've already lost count several times. That's true for almost everyone. It doesn't mean meditation isn't for you. It means you have a lively mind.
Every time you come back to one, that's a repetition, like working a muscle. Notice, let go, come back.
[pause 3s]
Sometimes a thought keeps running in the background while you count. You can count anyway. It may quiet down, or it may not. Your only job is the next number.
[quieter] Now I'll speak less, and you count on your own. Just you, the breath, and the numbers from one to ten.
[pause 8s]
One number per out-breath. Nothing else to do.
[pause 5s]
You're still here. Whatever the number, or if there's no number anymore, simply start again at one on the next out-breath.
If you caught yourself planning, remembering, or commenting on the exercise, that's perfect. You noticed. Back to one.
And if you feel a flicker of annoyance with yourself, note that too, with a bit of humor. It's part of the landscape. Then pick up the count again.
[pause 5s]

[clear] Now let's switch to a variation. Instead of counting to ten, count only up to four, then go back to one.
This shorter cycle asks for a different kind of presence. There's less time to drift, and the return to one comes more often. On days when your mind is very busy, this can be the most helpful version.
It works at work too. One thing at a time, and when you drift, you go back to the start of the paragraph, no drama.

two...

[pause 6s]
[soft] Feel how each number is a small appointment. You arrive, you set the number down, you move on.
Notice the space, too, between the out-breath and the next in-breath. A small quiet moment, where nothing happens.
[pause 5s]

[softer, slower] Now let the numbers fade away. Stop counting. Just stay with the breath for a few moments, in the spot you chose.
[pause 4s]
Notice what's here. Maybe a little more calm, maybe not. Maybe restlessness, or tiredness. All of it is valid. You don't have to get anything out of these minutes.
[pause 2s]
[warm, clear] You can take this counting anywhere. In a waiting room, before a meeting, in the middle of an overloaded day. A few rounds of ten are enough to come back to yourself.
Take a deeper breath. Gently move your fingers, your shoulders.
[pause 2s]
[warm, grounded] When it feels right for you, open your eyes. Take this simplicity with you. One breath, then the next.
