# sos-overwhelm-triage-4 · Too much at once (EN)
[pause 1.5s]
[warm, calm] It's a lot right now. Too many things at once, and all of them feel urgent.
Feeling overwhelmed like this is normal. It isn't a lack of ability. It's simply too much for one head to hold at once.
For the next four minutes, set it all down. None of it is going to fly away.
[pause 3s]

[calm, clear] Let's start with three long exhales.
Breathe in through your nose, without forcing...
[pause 3s]
Then blow out through your mouth, slowly, emptying all the way...
[pause 6s]
[soft] A second one. The air comes in...
[pause 3s]
and goes out, more slowly than usual...
[pause 6s]
The third one. Breathe in...
[pause 3s]
[slow, almost a sigh] and let the exhale carry away a little of the tension in your forehead and shoulders, right to the end.
[pause 5s]
[calm] When everything speeds up, slowing your breath is one of the few things you can do straight away.
[pause 1.5s]

[grounded] Now, your hands. Rest them flat on a table, on your thighs, or one inside the other.
Notice their weight and their temperature. Are your fingers cold, warm, a little numb?
[pause 2s]
Now choose a fixed point in front of you. A corner of the wall, a door handle, something on your desk.
Let your gaze rest there. It doesn't need to jump from screen to screen anymore.
[pause 3s]
[calm, reassuring] Hands resting, eyes resting. You have something steady to lean on.
Things can move forward without your body rushing off in every direction. For now, it can stay right here.
[pause 2s]

[clear] Now let's empty your head. Not to organize anything, just to let out what's spinning.
Silently, name everything that's on your mind, in any order, without sorting. A word or two for each thing.
The report that's due. The message you haven't answered. The groceries. That appointment. That conversation that keeps nagging at you.
Let your own things come up, big ones and tiny ones alike. Each one gets its name, just once.
If you like, you can count them quietly on your fingers.
Work. Home. The people waiting to hear from you. The worries about later on. Anything can come up.
[pause 3s]
[calm] If more keep coming, keep going. You don't have to do anything with these thoughts right now. Just acknowledge them.
[gentle] Notice that once they're named, they often take up a little less room. They're still there, but they're not all shouting at once anymore.
Your mind was trying to hold everything in memory at the same time. Now that it's all been named, it can loosen its grip a little.
[pause 1.5s]
[slow] Take one long exhale...
[pause 6s]

[clear, calm] Now, just one question. Out of all of that, what's the one thing that really matters in the next hour?
Not necessarily the biggest one, or the one others find most urgent. The one that would bring you the most relief once it's done. Or the one that truly can't wait.
Let the answer come. If you're torn between two, pick the simpler one. You can always change it later.
[pause 3s]

[encouraging] You have your one thing. Now picture the very first small step to get going on it.
Really small. Opening the document. Writing the first line of the email. Finding the number in your phone.
See yourself doing it. Where are your hands? What's on the screen, or in front of you?
[pause 2s]
Notice how doable that one step is, even now, even tired.
One small, clear step is often what gets everything else moving again.
[pause 1.5s]
[calm, reassuring] The rest of the list hasn't disappeared. It's waiting its turn, and it will get it, one thing after another.
You don't have to carry it all at once. Just this one step. When it's done, you'll choose the next one the same way.
[pause 1.5s]
[soft] One last long exhale, hands resting, eyes on your fixed point...
[pause 5s]
[warm, grounded] And now, go and take that first step. Everything else can wait its turn.
[pause 4s]
