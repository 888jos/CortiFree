# sos-anger-cool-down-4 · When anger rises (EN)
[pause 1.5s]
[calm, grounded] Anger has just surged up in you. A word, an email, a situation that crossed a line.
It's allowed to be here. Often, it's a sign that something important to you has been touched.
We're not going to make it disappear. We'll only take care of what you do with it in the next few minutes.
[pause 2s]

[clear, calm] First, find where it's burning in your body.
Maybe in your jaw, clenched, teeth pressed together.
Maybe in your fists, or in your forearms, ready to spring.
Or in your chest. Heat, pressure, a pounding heart.
Sometimes it's a knot in the stomach, or a face that's burning.
[pause 2s]
You don't need to change anything yet. Just notice the hottest spot. Simply locating it already gives you back a little control.
[pause 2s]
Now, your hands. If they're clenched, open them slowly, one finger at a time.
Rest them on your thighs, palms facing up, or let them hang loosely by your sides.
[pause 2s]
Feel the air on the inside of your hands. Open hands are not the same as clenched fists. Your body notices the difference.
[pause 2s]

[calm] Let's cool things down a little with the breath.
Breathe in through your nose, normally.
[pause 3s]
[slow] Then blow out slowly through your mouth, lips slightly rounded, as if you were cooling a spoonful of soup that's far too hot.
Not hard. Just a thin stream of air, long and steady...
[pause 6s]
[calm] Again. Breathe in easily...
[pause 3s]
[slow] and blow on the soup, nice and long...
[pause 7s]
[calm] A third time. Breathe in...
[pause 3s]
and out. Notice the end of the exhale, that little moment when the air is out and nothing is urgent.
[pause 4s]
[clear] The long exhale is like your body's brake pedal. Every slow breath out presses on it a little more.
Keep this rhythm going while we take a closer look at the anger.
[pause 1.5s]

[gentle] Behind it, there's almost always a need. Something that matters to you and wasn't respected.
Maybe respect. Someone spoke to you in a way that isn't okay with you.
Maybe fairness. What happened feels unjust.
Maybe rest. You're running on empty, and this was the last straw.
Or something else. Being heard, being recognized, feeling safe.
Which word feels right to you? Say it silently, once or twice.
[pause 3s]
Naming that need doesn't mean your anger is wrong. It helps you see what you actually want to protect.
[pause 1.5s]
[slow] Take another long exhale, like blowing on the soup. Feel your shoulders come down a notch, and your jaw loosen a little.
[pause 5s]

[clear, calm] Now you have a choice. And that choice belongs to you.
You can respond now, if you feel calm enough to say what matters without hurting anyone.
You can respond later. In an hour, tomorrow, after a walk or a night's sleep. Nothing forces you to react in the heat of the moment.
If it's an email, you can even write your reply without sending it, and reread it once you've cooled down.
You can also decide not to respond at all, if it isn't worth it.
[pause 1.5s]
All three options are valid. Let the one that feels most right, here and now, come to you.
Notice what your body says about it. Often, the right option is the one that lets you breathe a little more freely.
[pause 3s]

[soft] Before you carry on, one last breath. In through the nose...
[pause 4s]
[slow] and blow out, long and slow, keeping your hands open...
[pause 7s]
[warm, reassuring] The anger may still be there, a little less fierce. That's normal. It tends to come down in stages.
You know what you need, and you know you get to choose your response.
[pause 1.5s]
[warm, calm] You can go back to your day now, in your own way.
[pause 4s]
