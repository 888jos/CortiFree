# work-impostor-8 · Impostor feelings (EN)

[warm, calm] Maybe you've just started a new role, earned a promotion, or taken on a project that feels too big for you.
And somewhere, a small voice whispers that you don't belong here. That one day, someone is going to figure it out.
If so, this session is for you. We're not going to silence that voice. We're going to learn to live alongside it.
[pause 2s]
[calm] Get comfortable, with your back supported and your feet on the floor. Close your eyes if you like, or keep a soft, lowered gaze.

and breathe out gently, without forcing.

Feel where you touch the chair, and the floor. Your body is here, right now. It has nothing to prove.
[pause 2s]
[warm] Simply being here, willing to look this thought in the eye, already takes a bit of courage.
You don't need to feel confident to do this. You just need to be curious for a few minutes.

[clear] Now let's do something a little unusual. Instead of pushing this thought away, we're going to invite it in.
Let the sentence come that your mind tells you in those moments. The one that shows up most often.
Maybe it's, I'm not good enough. Or, I just got lucky. Or, sooner or later they'll find out I have no idea what I'm doing.
Find your own version, in your own words.
[gentle] Don't pick the harshest one you can imagine. Just the familiar one, the one you actually hear.
[pause 3s]
Listen to it. What's the tone of that voice? Urgent, mocking, cold, anxious?
[pause 2s]
How long have you known it? Maybe since school, or your first jobs. It's not a newcomer. It's an old habit of your mind.
Notice what it does in your body, too. Maybe a tightening in your stomach, heat in your chest, a weight on your shoulders.
You don't have to do anything about it. Just observe it, with curiosity, like a scientist studying something interesting.
[calm] Often we try to shut this voice up, or prove it wrong. And the more we fight it, the more room it takes up. Here, we do the opposite.
[pause 2s]

[clear] Here's a first step toward a little distance. Instead of thinking, I'm not good enough, you're going to say to yourself, I notice I'm having the thought that I'm not good enough.
Try it with your own sentence. I notice I'm having the thought that... and then your thought.
[pause 3s]
[calm] Feel the difference. The thought is still there. But now there's a little space between it and you. You're the one noticing it.
You are not the thought. You're the space it appears in, like the sky a cloud drifts across.
You can go a step further. Tell yourself, there's my mind, telling me the impostor story again.
Like an old song the radio keeps playing. You know every word. You don't have to sing along.
Each time you name the thought this way, you're practicing watching it pass, instead of getting swept away by it.
[pause 2s]

[warm] Now let's play a little. Take your sentence again, but this time, hear it in a cartoon voice.
A tiny, squeaky, nasal voice, like a very small character. Or a very deep, very slow voice, like a record winding down.
Let that voice say your sentence. They're going to find me out...
You can also picture it written in big, colorful letters on a balloon, drifting slowly off into the sky.
[pause 3s]
What happens? Maybe a slight smile. Maybe the sentence feels a little less threatening.
[pause 2s]
The words are the same. But you hear them for what they are. Words. Not facts. Not the truth about you.
[gentle] If this exercise doesn't do much for you today, that's fine. Simply observing is already enough.
[pause 2s]

[clear, reassuring] Here's something important to know. This feeling is extremely common, especially among capable, conscientious people.
Brilliant students, doctors, researchers, celebrated artists have all described this same voice.
It often shows up precisely when you're growing, when you're taking on new responsibilities. It's not a sign that you don't belong. More likely a sign that you're stepping outside familiar ground.
It's often the people who think hardest about their work who doubt themselves the most. Your doubt may mostly be a sign that you take things seriously.
Feeling like an impostor and being one are two very different things.
[pause 3s]

[clear] Now let's gather some evidence. Not feelings. Concrete facts.
Think of a first piece of evidence that you belong. A skill you've mastered. A problem you solved. A specific piece of positive feedback someone gave you.
Let it come with details. The moment, the people, what you did.
[pause 3s]
A second piece of evidence. Maybe the reason you were chosen. A degree, an experience, a quality others have noticed in you.
Someone decided to trust you with this role or this project. They surely had their reasons.
[pause 3s]
And a third. Something difficult you've already been through. A situation where you didn't know how, and you learned along the way.
[warm] You've never known everything in advance. Nobody does. We learn by doing.
Every capable person you admire was once a beginner, figuring it out as they went.
Keep these three pieces of evidence close. They're just as real as that little voice. More real, in fact, because they actually happened.
If your mind answers yes, but, to each one, notice that too. It's the same voice again. You can let it talk, and keep the evidence anyway.
[pause 2s]

[calm, intimate] Now, one last question. Why does this work matter to you?
Not for your title, or for what other people think. For you.
[pause 2s]
Maybe because you love to learn. Because you want to help people. Because you enjoy creating, solving, teaching, building.
Let what's true for you come forward. A value, a single word.
It doesn't need to be grand. Something simple and honest is enough.
Feel what it's like to connect with that. Maybe a kind of calm, a steadiness, deeper than the doubt.
[pause 4s]
[warm, grounded] That's your compass. Not the doubting voice. When it comes back, and it probably will, you can turn toward what matters to you.
[pause 2s]

[soft] Come back to your breath. Feel your body in the chair, your feet on the floor.
Notice how you feel now. The thought may still be there, a little in the background. That's okay.

[encouraging] If you like, choose one small step for tomorrow. A question you dare to ask, an idea you share in a meeting, even an imperfect one.
You don't need the voice to disappear in order to move forward. It can ride along in the car. But you're the one driving.
Next time it speaks up, you can simply tell yourself, ah, there's the impostor story. Thanks, mind. And carry on with what you were doing.
Doubt and competence can live side by side. Many people do excellent work while still hearing that voice now and then.
