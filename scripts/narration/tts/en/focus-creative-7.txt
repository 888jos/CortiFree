# focus-creative-7 · Unlock creativity (EN)
[pause 1.5s]
[warm, calm] Stuck on a project? An idea that won't come, a problem that won't budge?
[pause 2s]
You may have noticed that the best ideas rarely show up when you force them. They tend to arrive in the shower, on a walk, while you're doing the dishes.
That's no accident. When we push hard, the mind tends to narrow. When it loosens up, it makes unexpected connections more easily. So that's what you're going to do now. Loosen up.
[pause 2s]
[clear, unhurried] Get comfortable, sitting or lying down, whichever you prefer. Keep a pen and paper within reach, for the end.
Close your eyes, if that feels okay. Let the sound of the water settle around you. It flows, with no goal and no effort.
[pause 2s]
[soft] Take a big breath in through your nose...
[pause 3s]
[almost a sigh] and let it out through your mouth, with a gentle sigh.
[pause 5s]
[soft] One more. Breathe in...
[pause 3s]
and let go. Feel your shoulders drop.
[pause 4s]
[gentle] If part of you is still in that file, in front of the screen, leave it there for now. It'll still be there in seven minutes.
Now let your breath come and go at its own pace. Like the stream. Nothing to steer.
[pause 3s]
Let's take a quick tour of your face. Your forehead, often furrowed when you're searching for something. Let it widen. Your eyebrows, your jaw. Let all of that soften a little.
A tense head tends to produce tense ideas. A relaxed face is already a step toward more flexibility.
[pause 3s]

[calm, clear] Now put your question, or your project, into a single sentence. It doesn't need to be perfect. For example, how can I make this presentation come alive? Or, what's the right title for this piece? Or, how do we get unstuck on this problem with the team?
An open question often works better than a statement. Start with how, or what if, or what would.
Find your sentence, and say it to yourself, slowly.
[pause 4s]
Say it once more. Feel it, as if you were setting it down somewhere. On a table, or at the water's edge.
[pause 2s]
[soft, unhurried] And now... let it go. Completely.
You don't have to think about it anymore. It's been set down. Your mind can keep working on it in the background, without you.
If it comes back, that's fine. Notice it, and gently set it down again.
Like letting go of a balloon. You watch it rise, without holding on.
[pause 4s]

[calm] Now open your attention to everything that's here. Without choosing anything in particular.
The sounds. The flowing water, the noises in the room, maybe a car in the distance.
The sensations. The weight of your body, the temperature of the air on your skin, your hands.
The breath, carrying on by itself.
[pause 2s]
There's nothing you need to do with any of it. Just let things come, and let them pass. As if you were sitting on a riverbank, watching leaves drift along the surface.
[pause 5s]
Notice how there's no effort here. No answer to find. Only this moment, as it is.
If you feel the urge to get back to work right away, to search, just notice it. Wanting to search. And come back to the sounds, to the water.
[pause 3s]

[warm, clear] Now you're going to do something we usually try not to do. Let your mind wander. On purpose.
Usually in meditation, we come back to the breath. This time, let your mind go wherever it wants. A memory, an image, a color, a place, a conversation, a piece of music.
[soft] Follow it, the way you'd follow a cat through a garden, with no idea where it's heading. One thought leads to another, then another. Let the associations happen, even the strangest ones, even the ones that seem completely unrelated.
[pause 4s]
You can also wonder, without looking for an answer. How would a child see this? Or someone in a completely different line of work, a chef, a gardener, an architect? Let the images come, however far fetched.
Nothing is silly here, and nothing is off topic. You're simply daydreaming.
[pause 2s]
[gentle] If you find yourself worrying, about a problem or an overdue task, that's no longer daydreaming. Come back to the water for a moment, then let your mind drift somewhere lighter.
[pause 6s]
[soft] Keep drifting. If you notice you've started thinking in a tense, analytical way, let yourself slip back into the daydream. Lighter. Looser.
If an image amuses you, play with it. Make it bigger, change its color, turn it upside down.
[pause 6s]
And if your mind becomes very calm, almost empty, that's fine too. Emptiness is often the space ideas need.
[pause 4s]

[soft, intimate] Now, very gently, without leaving this relaxed state, bring your question back. Just brush against it. As if you were glancing at it sideways, without looking at it head on.
Say it once, silently. And notice what shows up.
[pause 4s]
Maybe a word, an image, a feeling. A memory that seems totally unrelated. A half formed idea. Even another question.
Don't sort. Don't judge. Don't tell yourself that's rubbish, or that'll never work. Welcome whatever comes, the way you'd gather shells on a beach, without knowing yet which ones you'll keep.
[warm] If your inner critic wakes up, the one that says been done, too simple, ridiculous, thank it politely. It'll get its turn later, when it's time to sort. For now, it's playtime.
[pause 4s]
[reassuring] If nothing comes, that's normal too. The idea may arrive later, in the shower, on the bus, on a walk. You've planted something, and it will grow at its own pace.
[soft] Ask the question one more time, differently. What if... And let things come.
[pause 5s]
Maybe an idea appears from a completely different angle. Something that seems absurd sometimes holds a lead. Welcome it with curiosity. You'll evaluate later, not now.
[pause 2s]
How do you feel? Maybe lighter, more open, with a little pleasure in playing with ideas. Or maybe nothing special. That's fine too.
[pause 3s]

[calm, clear] We'll come back soon. Take a deeper breath. Feel the weight of your body, the contact with the floor or the seat.
Start to move your fingers and toes a little.
[pause 2s]
[clear, encouraging] And here's the most important part. As soon as you open your eyes, before doing anything else, write down everything that came up. A word, an image, a phrase. Even what seems pointless. Daydream ideas evaporate quickly, like dreams when you wake up.
Don't write it up, don't organize it. Just get it down, uncensored.
[pause 2s]
[warm, slowing down] Open your eyes when it feels right for you. Pick up your pen. And let it flow, like the water.
[pause 4s]
