[calm, clear] Sit or stand tall, feet flat, shoulders settled. Let's go.
Box breathing has four sides, and each side lasts about four seconds. Firefighters, nurses and pilots use it to keep a clear head under pressure.
[clear, unhurried] You breathe in, you hold, you breathe out, and then you rest with empty lungs. All through the nose, without straining, as if you were tracing a square in your mind. One side goes up, one runs across, one comes down, one runs back.
I'll give you one word for each side, and the silence will do the counting. Start by gently letting your air out.

[gentle, reassuring] If the hold feels uncomfortable, make it shorter. A smaller square is still a square. And if you feel dizzy or uneasy, simply go back to your natural breath.
While you trace the square, your mind has a simple job to do. There's less room left for whatever was worrying you.
Notice the four corners. The top of the breath, the bottom of the breath, and those small plateaus in between, where everything is still.
