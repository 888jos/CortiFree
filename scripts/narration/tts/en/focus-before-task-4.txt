# focus-before-task-4 · Before an important task (EN)
[pause 1.5s]
[warm, calm] You have something important to do, and you chose to take four minutes before diving in. That's a good instinct.
[pause 1.5s]
[soft, unhurried] Settle into your chair, both feet on the floor, your back supported. You can close your eyes, or simply lower your gaze toward your desk.
[pause 2s]
Let's take three breaths together, a little slower than usual.
Breathe in through your nose, easily...
[pause 3s]
[slow] And out through your mouth, slowly, as if you were blowing on a hot drink.
[pause 4s]
[soft] Once more. The air comes in...
[pause 3s]
and flows back out, a little more slowly.
[pause 4s]
One last time, in...
[pause 3s]
[almost a sigh] and as you breathe out, let your shoulders drop, just a little.
[pause 4s]
[gentle] Good. Now let your breathing find its own rhythm again. It knows how.
If your mind is still on the last meeting, or on what's waiting for you tonight, that's normal. For these few minutes, bring it back here, to your chair, to this breath.
[pause 2s]
[clear] Before going further, something very practical. Your phone.
If you can, switch it to silent, or turn it face down. Better still, put it out of reach.
Think about the open tabs too, the inbox, the notifications blinking in the corner of your screen. Right after this session, you can close them, and keep only what this task really needs.
Every little alert pulls your attention somewhere else, and it takes a while to find its way back. Turning them off simply makes things easier for you.
[pause 2s]
[calm, clear] Now, the task itself. Often, what makes the mind restless is vagueness. You think about that big project, about everything you have to do, and it feels enormous.
So try to describe your task in a single sentence. Simple, precise, starting with an action word.
For example... Write the introduction to the report. Or, prepare the three slides for Thursday's meeting.
Take a few seconds to find your sentence, and say it silently to yourself.
[pause 4s]
[gentle] If it still feels too big, narrow it down. Something you could clearly move forward in the next half hour.
[pause 1.5s]
Good. Now go even smaller. What's the very first step? Not the second one, not the whole plan. Just the first move.
Opening the right file. Typing a heading. Reading the brief once through. Jotting down three rough ideas.
[pause 2s]
[soft, intimate] See that move in detail. Where are your hands? What's on the screen? What's the first word you type?
[pause 2s]
Picture yourself doing it, without hesitating, almost casually. The way you'd open a door you've walked through a hundred times.
[pause 3s]
[warm] Notice how doable that first step is. It doesn't need inspiration, or being in top form. It only asks you to begin.
[pause 1.5s]
[clear, grounded] And now, set an intention. Something like this. For twenty-five minutes, just this.
Twenty-five minutes is long enough to make real progress, and short enough for your mind to agree to it. You can set a timer if that helps.
During that time, if another thought pops up, an email to send, an errand to run, jot it down on a piece of paper next to you, and come back. Nothing is lost. It's simply parked for later.
[pause 1.5s]
[reassuring] And if your mind wanders off, that's normal. You'll come back to your sentence, to the next small move. No need to scold yourself.
No need to start over either. One breath, your sentence, the next small move. That's all.
[pause 2s]
[calm] Repeat your intention one last time, silently. For twenty-five minutes, just this.
[pause 3s]
One more breath. Feel your feet on the floor, your hands ready.
Open your eyes, if they were closed, and look at your workspace.
[pause 1.5s]
[warm, encouraging] Your sentence is clear. Your first step is ready. You don't need to feel like it to get started.
Begin now.
[pause 4s]
