# sos-after-conflict-6 · After an argument (EN)
[pause 1.5s]
[warm, calm] An argument has just happened. With someone who matters to you, and that's exactly why it's so unsettling.
You don't have to sort anything out in the next few minutes. Just come back down, and see things a little more clearly.
[pause 2s]
[soft] Settle in wherever you are. On a chair, standing against a wall, it doesn't matter. If you need to change rooms, now is a good time.
[pause 3s]
[calm, clear] Let's start by noticing what's happening inside you, without changing anything.
Your heart may be beating faster, or harder. You can feel it in your chest, in your throat, sometimes even in your temples.
There may be some heat. In your face, at the back of your neck, in your hands.
And in your head, the scene is probably replaying. The words that were said, the tone, that look.
Your hands may be trembling a little too. A lump in your throat, an urge to cry or to shout.
[pause 2s]
[reassuring] All of this is normal. It's your body, still on alert, ready to defend itself. It needs a little time to realize the tense moment is over.
Let's help it along with a few long exhales. I'll guide you.
[pause 1.5s]
[soft, unhurried] Breathe in through your nose, simply...
[pause 4s]
[slow] Then blow out through your mouth, slowly, a little longer than the inhale...
[pause 6s]
[soft] Again. Breathe in...
[pause 4s]
[almost a sigh] And as the air leaves, let your shoulders drop. They may have crept up toward your ears during the argument.
[pause 6s]
A third time. In...
[pause 4s]
The air goes out, your shoulders come down another notch, your hands unclench.
[pause 6s]
[slower] A fourth one, even slower. In...
[pause 4s]
And out... feel your belly soften at the very end of the exhale.
[pause 6s]
[gentle] Good. Keep breathing like this, at your own pace, with an exhale that takes its time.
[pause 2s]
[calm] Comebacks will probably keep popping up. I should have told them... Next time, I'll say...
That's what the mind does after a conflict. It hunts for the perfect line. It replays the match.
You don't need to follow these comebacks, or push them away. Just notice them, the way you'd watch cars go by from the sidewalk.
[pause 2s]
Oh, there's one. It passes. And you come back to your exhale.
You can even label it with one word... replaying. And come back to your breath.
[pause 3s]
[reassuring] Every time you return to the breath, you give your body a little more time to settle.
If one line keeps coming back, again and again, you can answer it silently. I hear you. Not now.
[pause 3s]
[warm, intimate] Now turn toward yourself. Not toward what the other person did, but toward what's happening inside you.
What are you feeling, right now, underneath the agitation?
[pause 2s]
Maybe sadness. Hurt. Fear. Exhaustion. Sometimes several at once.
Put a word to it, the one that feels true.
[pause 2s]
Notice where it sits in your body. Your throat, your chest, your belly. You can rest a hand there, if that helps.
[pause 2s]
And behind that feeling, what do you need? To be heard? Respected? Some calm? Recognition for everything you do?
[pause 3s]
[warm] That need is legitimate. It doesn't make you difficult. It simply tells you what matters to you.
[pause 2s]
Take a long exhale, and let that need stay where it is, acknowledged.
[pause 3s]
[gentle] Now, if you feel up to it, take a small step to the side. Picture the scene from the other person's place.
Not to say they were right. Just to see a slightly bigger picture.
What might they have been feeling during the argument? Frustration? Worry? Feeling like they didn't matter?
[pause 2s]
And what do they need, on their side?
[pause 2s]
Often, the two needs turn out to be less opposed than they seemed. Two people who each wanted, in their own way, to feel respected or reassured.
You don't have to guess right. A best guess is enough.
[reassuring] And if it's too soon to put yourself in their shoes, that's okay. You can come back to it later.
[pause 3s]
[soft, slow] Breathe. An easy breath in...
[pause 3s]
and a long breath out, gently emptying.
[pause 5s]
[calm, clear] Now picture the moment when you reconnect. Later on, when the time is right.
What could your first sentence be? A calm one, that opens things up instead of starting the fight again.
Maybe... I don't like how things went earlier. Or, I'd like us to talk about it again, more calmly. Or simply, I care about you, even when we disagree.
[pause 2s]
Choose your own, in your own words. One sentence is enough to begin.
A sentence about you, about what you feel and what you need, is often easier to hear than a reproach.
Say it to yourself silently, slowly, and notice what it does in your body.
[pause 3s]
[gentle, reassuring] And nothing says you have to fix everything right away.
Sometimes it takes a few hours, or a night, for both of you to come back down. That delay isn't a failure. It's often what makes a real conversation possible.
[pause 2s]
[soft] Take one more long exhale. Feel your feet on the floor, the weight of your body, your back straightening a little.
[pause 3s]
Notice where you are now, compared to the beginning. Maybe a slightly slower heart, a slightly less crowded head. Or still some agitation, and that's okay too.
[pause 3s]
[warm, grounded] You've made some space between the argument and whatever comes next. That space is what will let you choose your words.
[soft, warm, slowing down] Go back to your day, gently, with your sentence in your pocket for the right moment, whenever it comes.
[pause 4s]
