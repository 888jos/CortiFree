# anxiety-rain-method-7 · Meet your emotion (EN)
[pause 1.5s]
[warm, calm] Welcome to this moment for yourself. There may be an emotion keeping you company today, and you've chosen to stop and look at it, rather than run from it. That takes courage.
[pause 2s]
[soft, unhurried] Get comfortable, your back supported, your feet on the floor. You can close your eyes, or rest your gaze on a spot on the floor.
Take two or three breaths a little fuller than usual, and let each out-breath set you down a little more into your seat.
[pause 6s]
[gentle] Now feel your body breathing on its own. Your belly rising slightly. The air a little cooler as it enters your nose.
Stay here for a few moments, just with this movement.
[pause 4s]
If your mind is already wandering, that's normal. Just bring it back to the feeling of the air moving in and out.
[pause 2s]
[calm, clear] Today we're going to practice RAIN. It's a four-step way of moving through a hard emotion without drowning in it.
R for recognize. A for allow. I for investigate. N for nurture.
This approach was popularized by the American psychologist Tara Brach. The idea is simple. Instead of pushing away what you feel, you make room for it, one step at a time. And often, it becomes easier to bear.
[pause 2s]
[gentle, clear] To practice, choose a situation that stirs you up a little, but not the heaviest thing in your life.
Tension with a coworker, a message that went unanswered, a worry about someone. Something in the middle, a five out of ten.
Let the scene come back to you. Where were you, who was there, what was said? Just enough for the feeling to wake up.
[pause 3s]
[calm] First step, recognize. Simply ask yourself... what's happening inside me, right now?
[pause 2s]
Let a word come. Worry. Disappointment. Anger. Shame. Sadness. There may be several, tangled together like threads.
You can name it silently, in a calm voice, like a friend noticing. Ah, there's anger here. Or, oh, that's fear.
If no word comes, just describe what you notice. Something tight. Restlessness. A heaviness. That's just as accurate.
Naming isn't solving yet. It's simply turning on the light in the room.
[pause 3s]
[calm, clear] Second step, allow. This doesn't mean you approve of the situation, or that you like what you're feeling.
It means stopping, just for a moment, the fight against the fact that it's here. A bit like rain. You don't have to love the rain, but there's no point shouting at the sky.
You can whisper inside, yes, this is here. Or, okay, I see you.
[pause 2s]
[gentle] Notice if there's resistance. An urge to think about something else, to justify yourself, to blame yourself for feeling this way. You can let that be, too. Yes, that too.
Often, it's the fight against an emotion that wears you out the most. When you stop pushing against the door, it sometimes weighs a little less.
[pause 3s]
[calm, clear] Third step, investigate, with kindness. Now let's leave the story behind, and move toward the body.
Where does this emotion live? Take a little tour. Your throat, maybe tight. Your chest, heavy or pressed. Your stomach, knotted. Your shoulders, your jaw, your hands.
[pause 2s]
[soft, intimate] Choose the place where it's clearest, and rest your attention there, gently, the way you'd rest a hand on someone's shoulder.
Explore it with curiosity. Is it warm or cool? Hard like a stone, or shifting? Does it have a size, a shape, edges?
[pause 3s]
Notice, too, what the emotion is pushing you to do. Withdraw? Snap at someone? Grab your phone so you don't have to think about it?
You don't have to follow that push. Just see it.
[reassuring] If it gets too intense, you can come back to your breath or your feet on the floor at any moment. You're the one in charge.
[pause 3s]
[gentle] Then ask this part of you, kindly... what are you afraid of? Or, what do you need me to understand?
[pause 3s]
Don't look for the right answer. Listen to whatever comes, a sentence, an image, or nothing at all. Simply asking is already a form of care.
Sometimes the answer turns up later, during the day or on a walk. You've already done your part by asking.
[pause 2s]
[warm] Fourth step, nurture. Now ask yourself... what do I need, right here, right now?
To be reassured? To be understood? To know it's not my fault? A bit of rest?
[pause 2s]
When you have an idea, even a vague one, try to offer it to yourself.
You can place a hand on your heart, or on the spot where the emotion is. Feel the warmth of your palm, the light pressure.
[pause 2s]
[warm, intimate] And offer yourself a few words, the ones you'd say to a friend going through the same thing. Of course this is hard. I'm here. You're doing your best.
If the words ring false, imagine instead someone who loves you, or once did, saying them to you, in their own voice.
And if what you need is more practical, a break, a phone call, a glass of water, a boundary to set, make a note of it somewhere inside. You can give it to yourself later.
[pause 3s]
[soft, slower] Let these words, or this gesture, do their quiet work. There's nothing to force.
Notice even the smallest change. A shoulder softening, a slightly longer breath, warmth under your hand.
[pause 3s]
Now let go of all the steps. Nothing left to do, nothing left to explore.
Simply rest in being aware. There's the emotion, maybe softer, maybe the same. There are the sounds, the breath, the seat beneath you.
[pause 3s]
[calm, intimate] And there's you, noticing all of it. A bit like the sky, which holds the clouds without becoming the clouds.
You are not your anger, or your fear. You're the space they move through.
This emotion is a visitor. It arrived, it's shifting, and sooner or later it will leave, like every one that came before it.
[pause 4s]
[warm, gentle] Notice how you feel now, without looking for a result. Sometimes RAIN brings real relief. Sometimes, just a little more gentleness. Both count.
[pause 2s]
[clear, grounded] Gently, come back to the room. Move your fingers, your shoulders. Let the sounds around you come in.
Open your eyes when the moment feels right for you.
[pause 2s]
[warm] The next time an emotion floods you during the day, you don't need all the steps. Sometimes, pausing and saying yes, this is here, is enough to shift something.
