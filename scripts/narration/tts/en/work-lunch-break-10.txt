# work-lunch-break-10 · Lunch break (EN)
[pause 1.5s]
[warm, calm] It's time to eat. And for once, you're really going to eat. Not in front of a screen, not reading emails, not thinking about this afternoon's meeting.
For these ten minutes, there's only you, your meal, and this moment.
It doesn't have to be a special meal. Something homemade, a salad in a plastic tub, a sandwich grabbed on the run... it all works. What matters is how you eat it.
[pause 2s]
[calm] Start by settling in. If you can, step away from your desk. A break room, a bench outside, a quiet table.
Put your phone face down, or slip it into your pocket. Close your laptop if you're at your desk.
Set your meal in front of you. And sit comfortably, feet on the floor, back upright without being stiff.
If you're eating with colleagues, you can listen with one earbud, or simply keep the idea in mind. Eat a little slower, and notice the flavors.
[pause 2s]

[soft] Before the first bite, let's take three breaths together. To shift from work mode to meal mode.
Breathe in slowly through your nose. Maybe you can already smell what's in front of you...
[pause 4s]
[slow] and breathe out gently through your mouth.
[pause 5s]
[soft] Second breath. Let your shoulders drop as you exhale.
[pause 7s]
And a third. Notice your stomach as you breathe.
[pause 6s]
[calm] Are you hungry? A little, a lot?
[pause 2.5s]
Is it your stomach that's hungry, or is it more tiredness, stress, habit? There's no right or wrong answer. Just an observation.
This quick check on your hunger is useful. Later on, it'll help you notice the moment you've had enough.
[pause 2s]

[calm, unhurried] Now look at your food. Really look. As if you were seeing it for the first time.
What colors are there? The green of lettuce, the golden crust of bread, the red of a tomato, the white of rice.
Count the different colors, if you like. Even a simple sandwich often holds more than you'd think.
Notice the textures. What's smooth, what's crunchy, what's glossy, what's matte.
Look at the way the light falls on the food. The small shadows, the highlights.
[pause 3s]
[soft] Now bring your face a little closer, or lift your plate slightly. Breathe in the smells.
Close your eyes for a moment, if you can. Your sense of smell sharpens when your eyes rest.
Maybe spices, herbs, something toasted, a sweet or tangy note.
Notice if your mouth starts to water. Your body is already getting ready to eat. A smell is all it needs.
[pause 3s]

[gentle] Take your first bite. But before you put it in your mouth, look at it for a moment, on your fork or between your fingers.
Then bring it to your lips, slowly. And place it on your tongue, without chewing right away.
[pause 2s]
Notice the first taste. Is it salty, sweet, sour, bitter? A mix of all of them?
Notice the temperature. Hot, warm, cool.
Notice, too, where on your tongue the flavor is strongest. The tip, the sides, the back.
Every mouth is different. There's no right place, only what you notice.
[pause 3s]
[slow] Now begin to chew. Very slowly.
Notice how the texture changes. What was firm turns tender. The flavors open up, shift.
Maybe a taste you hadn't noticed appears. A spice, an herb, a small hidden note.
Keep chewing, without hurrying. Longer than usual.
How many times do you usually chew before swallowing? This time, go a little further. No need to keep track. Just enough to feel the difference.
[pause 3s]
[soft] And when it feels right, swallow. Feel the food travel down your throat, and then nothing.
Notice the taste that lingers in your mouth afterward.
[warm] That was a single bite. But maybe more flavor than in an entire meal eaten at your screen.
[pause 2s]

[clear] Now put down your fork. Or your sandwich, if that's what you're eating. Really set it down, on the plate or the table.
Between bites, your hands are free. It's a small gesture, but it changes everything. It breaks the habit of eating on autopilot.
[soft] Take a breath, hands resting.
[pause 4s]
[gentle] You might notice a little impatience, an urge to grab the next bite quickly. That's the pace of work still running inside you. Let it slow down, bite by bite.
[pause 3s]
[calm] Then take another bite, at your own pace, keeping that same attention.
With each bite, you can focus on a different sense. Taste first. Then texture. Then sound, the crunch, the softness.
For this bite, choose taste. Let it unfold, from the first contact to the last trace on your tongue. For the next one, choose texture. The melt, the crunch, the grain.
[pause 3s]
[reassuring] If your mind drifts back to work, to an email, to what's waiting for you later, that's normal. It happens to everyone.
Just notice that you've wandered off. Then come back to the taste of what's in your mouth. That's your anchor.
And remember to set your fork down between bites. No need to be perfect. Just do it more often than usual.
[pause 3s]

[warm, unhurried] Keep eating calmly. You might think about where this meal came from. The hands that grew it, transported it, cooked it.
All that journey, just to end up on your plate, this lunchtime.
No need to dwell on it. Just a moment of appreciation, and then back to the taste.
Between bites, look around you, too. The daylight, the people, a patch of sky. For a few minutes, you're somewhere other than your desk.
[pause 3s]
[calm] As the meal goes on, start noticing your stomach. Is it filling up? Has the hunger from the beginning changed?
Fullness arrives gradually. We often notice it too late, because we eat too fast.
Take a moment between bites to ask yourself, am I still hungry? Or am I eating out of habit?
[pause 2.5s]
On a scale of one to ten, where is your hunger now? A rough guess is enough. It's a way to get used to listening to that signal.
Five might mean comfortably hungry, eight pleasantly full. There's no target, only listening.
If you're still hungry, keep going, and enjoy it. If you're satisfied, you're allowed to stop. Even if there's food left on the plate.
[pause 3s]
Notice, too, whether the taste changes. Often, the first bites are the most intense. Then the pleasure fades a little, as your body is satisfied.
That's a valuable signal. Your body is telling you it has what it needs.
Eating more slowly gives those signals time to arrive. That's often why we feel lighter after a calm meal.
[pause 2s]

[soft, slower] When you're finished, or nearly, put your fork down one last time. Push your plate away a little.
And take a few minutes for yourself, before you go back to work.
Let your back lean against the chair. Let your hands rest on your thighs, or on your belly.
Feel the warmth of the meal in your body. The feeling of being nourished.
[pause 3s]
Let your belly be free. Loosen your belt if you need to. Digestion can begin quietly, with nothing for you to do.
[warm] Maybe a silent thank you. For this meal, for this moment, for your body, which will turn it into energy.
Notice your energy, too. Maybe a little drowsiness, which is normal after eating. Or a fresh sense of calm.
[pause 2s]
[calm] Now lift your eyes. Find the farthest point you can see. Through a window, down a street, at the far end of the room.
Let your gaze rest there, soft and relaxed. Your eyes have spent all morning inches from a screen. Let them see far.
[pause 4s]
[soft] Breathe easily. Let your breath be slow and steady, the way it is after a good meal.
Notice the sounds around you. Voices, cutlery, the street, the wind. You don't need to respond to any of them.
[pause 4s]

[clear, warm] If you have a little time left, you might take a short walk after eating. Outside, or just to the window. Many people find it softens that early afternoon slump.
Before you go back to work, choose an intention for the afternoon. Not a task, more a way of being.
Maybe, take things one at a time. Or, stay calm when the unexpected happens. Or, take a real break around four.
Choose a simple intention, in just a few words.
[pause 3s]
You can even link it to a gesture. Placing your feet flat on the floor, for example, every time you sit down at your desk. A small reminder, to find this calm again in an instant.
That link between gesture and intention grows stronger every time you use it. After a few days, it can become almost automatic.
Keep it with you. And when you get back to your desk, bring a little of this rhythm, this slowness, with you too.
[pause 2s]
[calm] You don't need ten minutes to find this calm again. One slow breath before you reopen your inbox can already be enough.
When the afternoon gets busy, remember this moment. A slow bite, a faraway gaze. You know now that you can slow down, even in the middle of a full day.
[warm, slowing down] Your body is nourished, your eyes have seen far, your mind has had a break.
Enjoy your afternoon.
