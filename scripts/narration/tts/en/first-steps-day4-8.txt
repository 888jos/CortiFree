[clear] Which brings us to the next step. What you've just noticed with sounds is also true of thoughts.
A thought arrives. An image, a memory, a sentence in your head, a list of things to do.
It stays for a while. Then, if you don't hold on to it, it leaves. And another one arrives.
You don't really choose which thoughts show up, any more than you choose the sounds from the street. But you can choose not to follow every one of them.
[pause 1.5s]
[warm] Thoughts are like a radio playing in another room. You don't need to switch it off. And you don't need to listen to every program either.
You can let them chatter in the background, and stay quietly where you are.
A thought isn't an order. It isn't necessarily the truth, either. It's just something happening in the mind, passing through, the way a sound passes through the air.
[pause 2s]
[clear] To help with this, I'm going to offer you a little tool. It's called mental noting.
When something catches your attention, you note it with a single word, very softly, inside.
If it's a sound, you note sound. If it's a thought, you note thinking. If it's something in your body, an itch, some tension, warmth, you note feeling.
Just one word, placed lightly, like a feather. Not an analysis, not a judgment.
And right after the label, you let the thing carry on its way.
[pause 1.5s]
For example. A car goes by... sound. You wonder whether you locked the door... thinking. Your knee tingles... feeling.
You don't need to note everything. Just whatever calls you most strongly, every few seconds, at your own pace.
And if you're not sure which word to use, that's fine. You can just note, this.
[gentle] Many people find that this small word creates a welcome bit of distance. You're no longer swept along by the thought. You're seeing it.
