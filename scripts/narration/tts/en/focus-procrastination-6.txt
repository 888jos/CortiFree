# focus-procrastination-6 · When you're procrastinating (EN)
[pause 1.5s]
[warm, calm] If you're listening to this, there's probably a task you keep putting off. Maybe for an hour, maybe for weeks.
[pause 1.5s]
Let's start with something simple. This isn't laziness, and it isn't a character flaw. Everybody procrastinates. Students, managers, artists, and very organized people too.
[clear] Often, procrastination has little to do with time or willpower. You put a task off because it stirs up something unpleasant. So you do something else instead, and for a while, that feels like relief.
Today, instead of forcing yourself or beating yourself up, let's look at this with curiosity.
[pause 2s]
[soft, unhurried] Get comfortable. You can close your eyes, or let your gaze rest on the floor.
Take a slightly fuller breath...
[pause 3s]
And let it go, without effort.
[pause 4s]
One more. Feel your body on the chair, or the couch, wherever you are.
[pause 4s]
[gentle] You don't need to feel motivated for this. You only need to be here, with whatever is going on inside you.
[pause 2s]
[calm] Now think of the task you've been avoiding. Bring it clearly to mind. The file on your desk, the email you haven't written, the form to fill in, the pile of laundry, the chapter to review.
See it as if it were right in front of you. Imagine you're just about to start it, right here, right now.
You don't have to actually start. Just imagining it is enough.
[pause 2s]
[soft, intimate] And notice what happens in your body. Not in your head. In your body.
Maybe a small tightening in your stomach. A heaviness in your chest. A sudden urge to pick up your phone. Your shoulders tensing, a sigh rising.
There's no right answer. Just notice what's there, even if it's subtle.
If you barely feel anything, notice the urge to move on to something else. That's useful information too.
[pause 3s]
[calm, clear] Now try to put a word to what you're feeling. What emotion is hiding behind that reaction?
[pause 2s]
Maybe it's boredom. The task is dull and repetitive, and everything in you is asking for something more alive.
Maybe it's fear of failing, or of doing it badly. Fear of what others will think, or of your own judgment. As long as you don't start, you can't fail.
Maybe it's fog. You don't really know where to begin, or what done even looks like. And the mind hates vagueness.
Or maybe it's something else. Tiredness, irritation, a sense that it's unfair.
Choose the word that feels most accurate, and say it gently to yourself. Boredom. Fear. Fog. Or your own word.
[pause 3s]
[gentle] Good. Simply naming an emotion often loosens its grip a little. It's no longer a vague cloud. It's something specific, something you can look at.
[pause 1.5s]
[calm] Now let's do the exact opposite of what procrastination does. Instead of running away from this feeling, you're going to stay with it for a few seconds.
Find the place in your body where it's strongest. And breathe with it. Imagine your breath reaching that spot, surrounding it, making a little room around it.
It doesn't need to disappear. You can let it be there, just for a moment.
[pause 4s]
[reassuring] Yes, it's unpleasant. But it's bearable. Maybe it shifts a little, or changes in intensity.
[soft, slow] Breathe in...
[pause 3s]
and as you breathe out, let a bit of space open up around it.
[pause 5s]
[gentle] If it gets too intense, come back to your feet on the floor, or open your eyes. You're always in charge.
[warm] You've just done something important. You felt what you were avoiding, and you're still here. Discomfort isn't dangerous. It's just uncomfortable.
[pause 2s]
[clear] Now, back to the task. We're going to make it ridiculously small. So small that your mind can hardly argue with it.
Not, write the report. Instead, open the document and type the title.
Not, clean the apartment. Instead, pick up the three things lying on the table.
Not, review the chapter. Instead, read the first page and highlight one sentence.
Not, reply to that difficult email. Instead, write the greeting and one first sentence, even a clumsy one.
[pause 1.5s]
Find the tiny version of your task. The one that fits into two minutes. The one so small it almost makes you smile.
[pause 3s]
[soft] Got it? See yourself doing it. Your hands, the exact movement, the place where you are.
[pause 2s]
[clear, grounded] Here's the rule. Two minutes. No more commitment than that. If you want to stop after two minutes, you're allowed to. You'll have kept your promise.
Very often, the hardest part is the first minute. Once you're moving, momentum does the rest. And if that doesn't happen today, those two minutes still count. They're done.
Starting is often the part the mind dreads most. Once the first step is behind you, the task usually looks smaller than it did from the outside.
[pause 1.5s]
Make one simple commitment. Right after this session, with no transition, you'll do those two minutes. Not after a coffee, not after checking your messages. Straight away.
Say it to yourself. Right after this, two minutes.
[pause 3s]
[warm, reassuring] And if the feeling comes back when it's time to start, which it probably will, you now know what to do. You recognize it, you make room for it for one breath, and you take the first step anyway.
[pause 1.5s]
[soft] Take one last breath. Feel your feet, your back, your hands.
[pause 3s]
Notice that you have nothing to prove. You just have one small thing to do.
[warm, encouraging] Open your eyes, if they were closed. Your tiny task is waiting. Two minutes, now.
Go.
[pause 4s]
