# compassion-kind-pause-5 · Self-compassion break (EN)
[pause 1.5s]
[warm, gentle] If you've opened this pause, maybe something is weighing on you right now. You did well to stop for a moment.
[pause 2s]
Over the next few minutes, I'll guide you through three simple steps, inspired by the work of the psychologist Kristin Neff. Three inner gestures you can come back to anywhere. At your desk, in the street, or lying awake in bed.
[pause 2s]
[calm, soft] Settle in just as you are. Sitting or standing, it doesn't matter. If you like, close your eyes, or simply let your gaze rest on the floor in front of you.
Take a breath in through your nose, without forcing it...
[pause 3s]
[almost a sigh] And let the air flow out slowly, like a long sigh.
[pause 4s]
[soft] One more time. Breathing in...
[pause 3s]
and letting go.
[pause 4s]
Now feel where your body is supported. Your weight on the chair or the floor. Your feet. Your hands, resting somewhere. Your body is being held up. You don't have to hold it.
[pause 2s]

[calm, clear] Now choose something that's difficult for you at the moment. Something medium sized, not the heaviest thing in your life. A worry at work, some tension with someone close to you, a concern that's been going round in your head since this morning.
Let the situation come to mind. Who's involved, what was said, what worries you.
[pause 2s]
[gentle] Then, gently, turn your attention to your body. Where do you feel this difficulty?
[pause 2s]
A tight throat, a weight on your chest, a knot in your stomach, a clenched jaw.
You don't need to change anything. You're simply noticing. Ah, there it is.
You can even rest a hand on that spot, as a way of telling your body, I feel you. If it gets too intense, come back to your feet on the floor.
[pause 3s]

[warm, clear] Step one is acknowledging. Say to yourself, in simple words, this is a moment of difficulty.
Or, this is hard right now. Or, this hurts. Choose the words that feel true for you.
[pause 2s]
This isn't complaining, and it isn't being dramatic. It's the opposite of pretending everything's fine. You're looking honestly at what's here, without adding to it.
Naming what hurts often helps you step back a little.
[pause 2s]

[warm] Step two is remembering that you're part of something bigger. Difficulty is part of life. It isn't proof that you're getting things wrong.
Say to yourself, other people go through this too. I'm not the only one who feels this way.
[pause 2s]
[soft] Right now, somewhere, someone feels overwhelmed in front of a full inbox. Someone is waiting for a reply that doesn't come. Someone is being hard on themselves, alone in a kitchen, late at night.
You don't know these people, and yet you share something with them. What you're feeling is deeply human.
This difficulty doesn't set you apart. If anything, it connects you to everyone who has ever found life heavy.
[pause 3s]

[warm, intimate] Step three is kindness. If it feels comfortable, place a hand on your heart. Or both hands. Or a hand on your cheek, on your belly, wherever that touch feels good.
Feel the warmth of your palm. The gentle pressure. Your chest rising and falling beneath it.
[pause 2s]
This gesture isn't trivial. Warm, gentle touch can help your body feel safe, a little like a friend's hand resting on your shoulder.
[soft, slow] And now say to yourself, softly, may I be kind to myself. May I give myself the patience I need.
Repeat it slowly, once or twice, in time with your breath. Breathing in, may I be kind to myself. Breathing out, the patience I need.
[pause 4s]
[gentle] These words are only an example. What do you need, right here, right now? Comfort, courage, rest, a little understanding?
[pause 3s]
Imagine a friend going through exactly what you're going through. What would you say to them? Maybe, you're doing your best. Or, of course this is hard. Or simply, I'm here.
Take those same words, and offer them to yourself.
[pause 4s]

[reassuring] If nothing comes, that's fine. The intention is enough. And if an emotion rises, tears, a lump in your throat, that's normal too. You can slow down, open your eyes, or stop here. You're the one who decides.
[pause 2s]
[soft] Stay a moment longer with your hand where it is. Notice what's here now. Perhaps a little more space. Perhaps nothing has changed, and that's completely fine too.
[pause 4s]
[calm, clear] Let your hand come down. Move your fingers a little, and your shoulders. Come back to the sounds around you.
When the moment feels right for you, open your eyes.
[pause 2s]
[warm, slowing down] These three steps fit into three short sentences. This is hard. I'm not alone in this. May I be kind to myself. You can take them with you wherever you go.
