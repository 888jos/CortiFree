[soft, slow] Now try following one whole breath. Not just a moment of it, but its entire journey.

[soft] If you like, you can also note the breath itself, very lightly. The word in, on the in breath. The word out, on the out breath. An inner whisper, almost silent. It sometimes helps when the mind is busy. As soon as you no longer need it, let the words drop, and stay with the pure sensation.

[calm] If you find you're often carried away, you can make your attention a little finer. Don't just try to feel the breath in general. Look for the details.
If your anchor is at the nostrils, notice the temperature. The in breath is often slightly cooler. The out breath, slightly warmer. Notice too which side the air flows through more, the left nostril or the right.
If your anchor is the chest or the belly, notice the texture of the movement. Is it smooth, or jerky? Are there small waves within a single breath?
Maybe the air feels more humid on the out breath. Or the movement of the belly extends into your lower back. Notice, too, the moment the air touches the back of your throat. Tiny details you may never have noticed before.

[soft, slow] And notice the pauses. Between the end of the in breath and the start of the out breath, there's often a tiny stillness. And another, often a little longer, after the out breath, before the air returns.
Don't create these pauses, and don't lengthen them. Just notice them when they're there. A small moment of calm, where the body quietly waits for the next breath.
