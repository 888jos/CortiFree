# sos-safe-place-7 · Your inner refuge (EN)
[pause 1.5s]
[warm, calm] For the next seven minutes, we're going to build something you can keep. An inner refuge, a place where your body feels safe, and where you can return in a few seconds, even in the middle of a busy day.
[pause 1.5s]
[soft, unhurried] Get comfortable, sitting or lying on your back. Let your back be supported, and let your hands rest wherever they feel good.
You can close your eyes, or lower your gaze to a point that isn't moving.
[pause 2.5s]

[calm] Let's arrive with a few slow breaths. Breathe in through your nose...
[pause 4s]
[slow] and let the air leave even more slowly, through your mouth...
[pause 6s]
Once more. The air comes in, cool... and goes out, warm, carrying a little of the day away with it.
[pause 5s]
One last time. And at the end of the exhale, let your body grow a little heavier, as if it were settling onto whatever is holding it.
[pause 4s]

[warm, gentle] Now, let a place come to mind where you feel safe.
It can be a real one. Your grandparents' kitchen, a little cove in Brittany, a bench in a park you know by heart. Or it can be completely imaginary, a cabin, a room that exists nowhere else.
[pause 1.5s]
Don't look for the perfect place. Take the first one that brings a sense of calm, even a faint one.
If several come up, choose just one for today. And if nothing comes, you can simply imagine a warm, quiet room, with a door that only opens for you.
[gentle, reassuring] If a place brings up difficult memories, set it aside and pick another, more neutral one. This refuge should feel simple and gentle.
[pause 3s]

[soft, intimate] You're there now. Start by looking around.
Which colors stand out? Greens, blues, the warm tones of wood or stone?
[pause 2s]
And the light. Is it morning, late afternoon, the soft glow of a lamp? Where does it come from, and how does it fall on things?
[pause 2s]
Let your inner gaze wander. Notice a detail close to you, a texture, a shape, an object. Then a detail farther away, on the horizon or at the far end of the room.
You can look up, too. A sky, a ceiling with wooden beams, branches swaying.
[pause 2s]
Every detail you add makes this place a little more solid. Take your time. And if the picture stays blurry, that's fine. Some people see very little, and feel a great deal.
[pause 3s]

[calm] Now, listen. What can you hear in this place?
[pause 2s]
Maybe running water, wind in the leaves, waves rolling in. The crackle of a fire, a clock ticking, birds in the distance.
Or a deep, reassuring silence, which has a texture of its own.
Notice whether these sounds are near or far, steady or coming and going. Let them wrap around you, with no effort at all.
[pause 2.5s]
Breathe in the air of this place. Is there a smell? Cut grass, sea salt, wood, warm bread, clean laundry.
If no smell comes, that's alright. Some places are felt more than they're smelled.
[pause 2s]
Feel the temperature on your skin. Is the air mild, or cool, just the way you like it? Maybe a ray of sunlight on your face, or the warmth of a blanket.
And under your feet, what's there? Sand that gives a little, soft grass, smooth floorboards, a stone warmed by the sun.
Take a few steps, if you like, and feel how the ground answers each one.
If you sit down in your refuge, feel what's holding you. An armchair, a rock, a soft cushion.
[pause 3s]

[soft, warm] Here, nothing is rushing you. No one expects anything from you. No notifications, no appointments, no judgment.
Notice how your body responds to this safety. Where do you feel it?
[pause 2.5s]
Maybe in your shoulders, letting go. In your belly, unknotting. In your breath, fuller and slower. In your face, which no longer has to put on a brave front.
Let this feeling spread, like warmth slowly filling a room. You don't have to do anything to keep it. Just notice it.
[pause 2s]
Take the time to really savor it. The more clearly you feel it now, the easier it will be to find it again later.
[gentle] If part of you stays a little on guard, that's normal. Welcome that part into this place as well. It's allowed to be here.
And if a thought from your day comes knocking at the door, that's normal too. You see it, you leave it outside, and you come back to one detail of your refuge. A color, or a sound.
[pause 3s]

[clear, calm] Now I'm going to help you create a shortcut to this place.
Gently bring the tip of your thumb and your index finger together, on one hand or both. A small, discreet circle that no one will notice.
[pause 1.5s]
While your fingers are touching, breathe in the calm of your refuge. Its colors. Its sounds. Its temperature. The ease in your body.
As if you were recording all of it into this small gesture.
The gesture and the feeling, together, at the same moment.
[soft] Breathe in the calm...
[pause 4s]
[slow] and let it settle into your fingers...
[pause 6s]
Release your fingers. Breathe normally.
[pause 2s]
And make the gesture again. Thumb against index finger. Notice how the refuge comes back, maybe a little faster this time.
A color. A sound. The feeling under your feet. It's all there, in this small circle.
[pause 3s]

[warm, clear] The more often you repeat this pairing, the more reliable the gesture becomes as a key. At first, it brings back a hint of calm. Over time, it can bring you back here within a few breaths.
Later on, before a meeting, in a waiting room, as you fall asleep, you'll be able to bring these two fingers together and find part of this place again.
You can practice a little each day, when things are going well. That way, the shortcut is ready on the day you need it.
[pause 2s]
[gentle] Now release the gesture. Your refuge stays where it is. It doesn't disappear when you leave it.
You can also return to it simply by closing your eyes, without the gesture, whenever you have a little more time.
[pause 2s]
[calm, grounded] Slowly, begin to come back. Feel the support beneath your body, the floor, the chair or the bed.
Hear the sounds of the room you're actually in. The nearest ones first, then the ones farther away.
[pause 2s]
Take a slightly fuller breath. Move your fingers and your feet, and stretch if you feel like it.
[pause 2s]
[warm, slowing down] And whenever it feels right, open your eyes. Your shortcut comes with you. Thumb to index finger, and your refuge is never far away.
