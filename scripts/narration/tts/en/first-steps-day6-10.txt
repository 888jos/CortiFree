# first-steps-day6-10 · Day 6 · Simply being here (EN)
[pause 1.5s]
[warm, calm] Welcome to day six.
[pause 1.5s]
So far, you've learned to place your attention somewhere. On the breath, on the body, on sounds. You've also learned to watch thoughts and emotions pass by.
Today, you're going to let go of the anchor, just for a little while. Not to get lost, but to discover what it's like to simply be here, welcoming whatever shows up.
[pause 2s]
[clear] In meditation traditions, this is sometimes called open awareness. You'll see, it's much simpler than it sounds.
We'll take it step by step. First the breath, then the body, then sounds and thoughts. At each step, we open a little wider, without letting go of what was already there.
[pause 2s]

[soft, unhurried] Take your time to settle in. Feel your seat, your back lengthening without effort, your shoulders settling down.
Close your eyes, or let your gaze rest ahead of you, toward the floor, not focusing on anything.
[pause 2s]
And give yourself a big breath in...
[pause 3s]
[almost a sigh] and let it out like the sigh you let out when you get home after a long day.
[pause 5s]
[soft] One more. Breathe in...
[pause 3s]
and let your upper body soften as you breathe out.
[pause 5s]

[calm] Begin with your most familiar anchor. The breath.
Find it wherever it's clearest. Follow one in breath from beginning to end. Then one out breath, from beginning to end.
[pause 5s]
Notice that each breath is a little different from the last. Shorter, longer, deeper.
[pause 3s]
Let your breath bring you back here, one breath after another. No need to control it. Just watch it do its thing.
If your mind is already wandering, you know the way back.
[pause 4s]

[calm, clear] Now gently widen your attention to include the whole body.
As if the beam of a flashlight were opening up until it lit the entire room.
[pause 2s]
You feel the breath, and around it, the whole body. The weight on your seat. Your feet. Your hands. The temperature of the air on your skin.
You don't need to move from one area to the next, as in the body scan. You're feeling the whole body at once, a little blurry, like a landscape seen from a distance.
A landscape with calm areas and livelier ones, warm spots and cool spots.
[pause 4s]
[gentle] If a stronger sensation catches your attention, an itch, some tension in your back, let it be there, in the landscape, without locking onto it.
It's part of the picture. It isn't the whole picture.
Notice too that this landscape keeps changing. One area warms up, another fades, the breath gently moves the sensations around. Nothing stays fixed.
[pause 4s]

[calm] And now, let's add sounds.
The body breathing, and around it, the sounds of the room, the street, the world. All of it can live side by side in your attention.
There's nothing to choose. The body is here. The sounds are here. They come and go.
[pause 3s]
Notice that you don't need to reach out for sounds. They come to you, all on their own, just as the body makes itself felt on its own.
The body and the sounds aren't two things to monitor one after the other. They're here together, in the same space. Like sitting outside a café. You feel the sun on your skin, and at the same time you hear the street, without any effort.
[pause 4s]

[calm, clear] And then, thoughts. They're part of the landscape too. A thought appears, like a sound. It stays for a while, then drifts away.
You don't need to follow it, or stop it. You can let it pass, the way you let the sound of a car pass.
If you like, you can still use the labels from day four. Thinking. Sound. Feeling. Or simply let everything pass, without words.
[pause 3s]
You may notice that some thoughts are light and pass quickly. Others are sticky. They come back, they insist. Those can stay in the background too, without you having to obey them.
Emotions, if there are any, have their place as well. You now know how to welcome them in the body, give them a name, and make a little room for them.
[pause 3s]

[warm, storytelling] An image might help here. Picture the sky.
Clouds drift across it, small or large, light or dark. Birds, planes, sometimes a storm.
The sky doesn't choose what passes through it. It doesn't hold on to anything. It doesn't push anything away.
[pause 2s]
And even in the biggest storm, the sky is still the sky. Vast, open, untouched behind the clouds.
Have you ever flown on a rainy day? Above the gray layer, the blue was there all along.
[pause 2s]
Your awareness can be like that sky. Thoughts, sounds, sensations, emotions are the clouds. They pass. The sky remains.
You don't have to become the sky through effort. You just have to stop chasing every cloud.
[pause 2s]
[soft, intimate] And notice something, very gently. Something in you is watching all of this go by. The sounds, the thoughts, the sensations. That quiet awareness doesn't leave with them. It's simply here, like the sky.
[pause 4s]

[soft, slower] For the next few minutes, I'll say very little. Simply rest in this openness, like the sky. Let everything come, let everything go.
There's nothing to do. Nothing to get right. Just be here.
[pause 8s]
Let your attention rest, without holding on to anything. Like an open hand, rather than a clenched fist.
Breath, body, sounds, thoughts. Everything passes through the sky of your awareness.
[pause 6s]
You can feel the breath coming and going, in the middle of everything else. It's no longer at the center. It's part of the landscape too.
[pause 5s]
Maybe a distant sound appears, then fades. You let it pass through, without following it to the end.
A memory, a plan for tonight, a small worry. Just another cloud. It comes, it goes.
[pause 8s]
Maybe a sensation shows up in the body. Some warmth, a tingling, a spot of tension. You let it appear, linger, and change.
Notice that you don't need to decide where to look. Whatever is here shows up, and whatever leaves, leaves.
[pause 6s]
[gentle] If you notice that a thought has taken over, that's normal. A cloud doesn't damage the sky.
You can come back to the breath first, for a few breaths, to steady yourself. Then, without hurrying, open your attention back up to everything else.
[pause 6s]
[soft] Maybe notice the quality of this moment. A bit of space. A bit of calm. Or restlessness instead. The sky welcomes it all.
If you catch yourself waiting for something, a special kind of calm, a feeling of spaciousness, notice that waiting too. It's just another cloud.
[pause 6s]
Sounds nearby, sounds far away. The silence between them. All of it held together, without effort.
[pause 5s]
Between two thoughts, there's sometimes a little gap. A moment where nothing in particular is happening. You can enjoy it, without trying to stretch it out.
[pause 8s]
[softer, slower] Your body breathes on its own. Sounds arrive on their own. Thoughts pass on their own. And all you have to do is be here.
Nothing to hold on to. Nothing to push away. Only this moment, and then the next.
[pause 8s]
[gentle] If you notice yourself straining to keep your attention open, ease off the effort. Openness comes more easily when you stop chasing it.
There's no special experience to reach. Just the one that's here, now.
[pause 5s]
[reassuring] If the openness starts to feel too vague, or your mind gets restless, return to the breath for as long as you need. The anchor is always there. And when you feel steadier, you can open up again.
[pause 6s]

[calm, clear] Gently, bring your attention back to the breath. One breath in, one breath out.
[pause 4s]
Then feel your body again, resting here. The weight of your hands, the contact of your feet.
[pause 3s]
How do you feel, right now? Don't demand anything.
[pause 3s]
Maybe a little lighter, maybe tired, maybe exactly the same. All of it is fine.
[warm] If this openness felt pleasant, good. If it felt strange or hard to grasp, that's very common at first. It's a quality you discover little by little, often in small glimpses.
As with everything else this week, it isn't a question of talent. It's a question of getting familiar with it.
[pause 3s]

[warm, clear] Today, you used everything you've learned this week.
The breath to anchor you. Coming back, when your mind wanders. The body as a place to rest. Sounds and labels to watch things pass. Making room for what you feel.
And you got a taste, maybe for just a few seconds, of that wider space that's always there, behind the busyness.
Those few seconds count. With practice, they become easier to find again.
[pause 2s]
You can find them outside meditation, too. On a park bench, on a moving train, looking out of a window. A few moments when you let everything in, without sorting any of it.
During your day, if you think of it, look up at the sky, the real one. And remember that your thoughts pass, and you remain.
[pause 3s]
[warm, gentle] Whenever you're ready, move a little, stretch if you feel like it, and open your eyes.
[warm, slowing down] Tomorrow, on the last day of this course, we'll look at how to take meditation with you.
