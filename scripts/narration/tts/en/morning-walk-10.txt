# morning-walk-10 · Walking meditation (EN)
[pause 1.5s]
[warm, calm] This meditation is done while walking. Outdoors, on your way to work, in a park, or simply at home, pacing back and forth across your living room or along a hallway.
[pause 1.5s]
[clear] First, a safety note. Keep your eyes open the whole time. If you're outside, stay aware of what's around you. Sidewalks, bikes, cars.
At crossings, look, and cross as you normally would, with your full attention on the road. And of course, never while driving.
Your inner chatter can quiet down. The world around you stays switched on.
If you're outside, listen with one earbud, or at low volume, so you can still hear the street.
[pause 2s]
[soft, unhurried] To begin, stand still for a moment. Feet hip-width apart, arms by your sides.
Notice your posture too. Knees soft, pelvis settled, shoulders dropping naturally. Your head light, as if balanced on top of your spine.
[pause 2s]
Feel the weight of your body sinking down into your feet. How is it spread? More on your heels? More toward the front? More on one foot than the other?
[pause 3s]
Sway gently, forward and back, side to side. Then find the center, the point where your weight is spread roughly evenly over both feet.
Feel the ground beneath you. Hard, soft, flat, uneven. The contact of your sole, your sock, or bare skin.
Take a moment to really feel it. This contact with the ground will be your anchor throughout.
[pause 4s]
[calm] Take a breath or two here. Your gaze resting a few yards ahead, not fixed on anything.
[pause 3s]
This short pause helps your mind change gears. You shift from I need to get there, to I'm walking right now.
[pause 2s]
[clear, warm] Now, let's start walking. Very slowly at first. Much more slowly than usual. If you're on the street and this pace feels awkward, just walk a little slower than normal, and keep your attention in your feet.
[slow] Shift your weight onto your left leg. Feel your right leg becoming light.
Your right heel lifts. Then the sole. Then your toes leave the ground.
Your foot moves forward through the air, a few inches.
And comes down. Heel first. Then the sole rolls down. Then the toes.
Your weight glides forward, onto that right foot. And your left leg, in turn, becomes light.
[pause 2s]
[reassuring] Breaking it down like this may feel mechanical at first, a bit like learning to walk all over again. That's normal. You're discovering a movement you've made all your life, without ever really watching it.
[pause 3s]
[soft] Keep going like this, step by step. Heel, sole, toes. Lift, move, place.
You can say it silently, if it helps. Lift... move... place.
[pause 3s]
Notice all the tiny adjustments your body makes to keep its balance. Ankles, calves, hips. An incredible feat of coordination you perform thousands of times a day without a thought.
Notice, too, the moment when both feet are on the ground at once, just before your weight tips forward. A tiny instant of double support, and then the movement carries on.
[pause 4s]
[calm] If you're at home and you reach the end of the room, stop for a moment. Feel your feet, still. Then turn around slowly, staying present through the whole turn. And set off again.
The turn is part of the walk too. Your weight shifting, your feet pivoting, your eyes discovering the other side of the room.
[pause 2s]
[clear] Now, let's link your breath to your steps. Not rigidly. Just noticing the connection between them.
For example, you might breathe in over two steps, and out over two steps. Or in over a few, and out over a few more. Find whatever feels natural.
[soft] Breathe in, as your feet carry you forward...
[pause 3s]
and breathe out, over the next steps.
[pause 3s]
If your breath needs more or less, let it decide. Your steps adapt to your breath, not the other way around.
Does your breathing slow a little as your steps become steady? Or does it stay the same?
[pause 3s]
Either is fine.
[pause 2s]
Keep walking with this rhythm. Breath and feet moving together, like two musicians playing the same tune.
If counting steps feels too much like school, drop the numbers. Just feel the in-breath carrying a few steps, and the out-breath carrying a few more.
[pause 4s]
[gentle] You may have noticed that your mind has already wandered off. Toward your day, a memory, a conversation, a project.
That's perfectly normal. It's what minds do. There's nothing to fix.
The moment you realize it, that's already a moment of presence. Simply come back to your feet. To the next heel touching the ground.
You can even note, in one word, where your mind went. Work. Memory. Worry. Then pick up your stride again, no commentary needed.
[pause 4s]
[calm, warm] Now widen your attention a little. Your feet stay as a reference point, in the background. But you open up your other senses too.
Sounds first. What's close by, your own footsteps, the rustle of your clothes. Then further away. Voices, a door, wind in the trees, traffic, a bird, a fridge humming.
Let them come, without holding on to them.
Notice how they change as you move. Some come closer, others fade and disappear behind you.
[pause 3s]
[soft] The air on your skin. On your face, on your hands. Cool, mild, damp, dry. Maybe a bit of a breeze. Maybe sunshine on one cheek.
And smells, if you notice any. Coffee from a café, damp earth, cut grass, warm bread from a bakery, or the familiar smell of your hallway.
No need to stop to take them in. They come to you, step by step.
[pause 3s]
[warm] And colors. Look around as if you were discovering this place for the first time. The green of the leaves, the gray of the pavement, the red of a mailbox, the blue or white of the sky. At home, the color of a book, a cushion, a plant.
Let one color draw you in. Really look at it for a few seconds, while you keep walking.
[pause 3s]
If you're outside, notice the other people passing by. Each with their own day, their own thoughts, their own pace. You're part of this great morning movement, simply by walking.
[pause 3s]
All of this is part of your walk. You walk, and the world flows in through your senses.
[gentle] If at any point it feels like too much at once, come back to your feet. Heel, sole, toes. That's your anchor, always available.
[pause 4s]
[calm, reassuring] Thoughts will almost certainly come back, again and again. Each time, it's the same. You notice, and you come back to your feet. No frustration, no judgment. The way you'd gently bring back a puppy that's wandered off.
This coming back is the real heart of the practice. It's not a failure. It's the exercise itself.
Maybe your mind is calmer now, or maybe it's still very busy. Either way, your walk is exactly what it needs to be.
[pause 3s]
[clear, encouraging] Now you can let your pace pick up. Gradually return to your natural walking speed. Your everyday pace.
This is where the real challenge begins. At this speed, the mind is used to slipping into autopilot.
Let's see if you can keep a little presence. Not as much as when you were walking slowly. Just a thread.
Keep feeling your foot meet the ground with each step, even at this faster pace. The natural swing of your arms. Your breath adjusting to the new rhythm.
[pause 2s]
You could choose a cue to bring you back to the present. For example, every time you pass a tree, a red light or a doorway. That cue becomes your bell. One mindful step, then you carry on.
[pause 3s]
[calm] How are you holding yourself right now? Is your head pushed forward, as if it wanted to arrive before you?
[pause 2s]
Let it come back over your shoulders. Your gaze opens out toward the horizon.
Let your arms swing freely too, rather than burying them in your pockets or clenching them around your bag.
An open posture often changes how you meet what comes next. Your body straightens, and your mind follows.
[pause 2s]
A few more steps, at your own pace. Feet, breath, sounds, light. Everything is here, all at once.
[clear] If you reach a street to cross, set the meditation aside while you cross. Look, wait, cross. Then pick up the thread again.
[pause 4s]
[warm, calm] This session is coming to an end, but your walk can carry on.
If you're outside, you can keep this attention all the way to where you're going. The station, the office, the bakery. At least now and then, come back to a step, a sound, a color.
If you're at home, come to a gentle stop. Stand for a few seconds. Feel your weight settle back into both feet, still.
[pause 3s]
Notice your body after this walk. What feels calmer, more awake, more present.
[pause 2s]
[warm] This morning, you walked without rushing toward what's next. You lived in the journey, instead of just getting through it.
From today, every journey can become a small meditation. Even two minutes, between your front door and the end of the street. Or along an office corridor, between two meetings.
[warm, grounded, slowing down] All you need is to remember your feet. They're always there, with every step, to bring you back here.
[pause 4s]
