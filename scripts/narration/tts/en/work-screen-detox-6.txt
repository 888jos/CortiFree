# work-screen-detox-6 · Screen detox (EN)
[pause 1.5s]
[warm, calm] How many hours have your eyes spent on a screen today? A laptop, a phone, maybe both at the same time.
[pause 2s]
You may already feel it. Eyes that are a little dry and sting. A heavy forehead. A sort of buzzing, as if your mind keeps scrolling even after you've stopped.
For the next six minutes, we're going to give your eyes something else.
[pause 1.5s]
[clear, unhurried] Let's start with your phone. Turn it face down on the table. Even better, put it out of reach, in a drawer or on another shelf.
If you're at a computer, switch off the monitor, or tilt the laptop lid partway down.
[pause 2s]
[gentle] You might feel a little resistance. What if someone messages me? That's normal. It can wait six minutes.
That small tug is the habit of always being reachable. Every notification pulls at your attention, even the ones you never answer.
[pause 3s]

[warm] Now rub your palms together. Briskly, the way you would on a cold winter morning.
Keep going until you feel real warmth in the hollows of your hands.
[pause 2s]
Close your eyes. Then gently cup your palms over them. Your fingers rest on your forehead, the heels of your hands on your cheekbones.
No pressure on the eyes themselves. Just a pocket of warmth and darkness.
If you can, rest your elbows on the table, so your arms don't get tired.
If you wear glasses, you can take them off and set them aside. If you wear contacts, simply keep your eyelids relaxed.
[pause 3s]

[calm, soft] This is called palming. It's a very simple way to rest your eyes, by giving them complete darkness.
Let the warmth of your hands spread over your eyelids. Then your brows, your temples, the bridge of your nose.
[pause 2s]
Do you still see spots of light, shapes, colors?
[pause 2s]
Let them fade on their own. You don't need to look for them.
The harder your eyes have worked, the longer they may need to really rest. Give them that time, without expecting anything in particular.
[pause 2s]
[slow, soothing] In this darkness, let your breathing slow down. You don't have to control it. Let it find a slightly calmer rhythm by itself.
Breathe in through your nose... and feel the cool air come in.
[pause 4s]
Breathe out slowly... and feel your shoulders drop a little.
[pause 6s]
If it helps, make the out breath a little longer than the in breath. Or don't measure anything at all.
[pause 3s]
[soft] Each exhale can soften the tiny muscles around your eyes a bit more. The ones that squint, stare and refocus all day long.
They can rest now too.
[pause 2s]
[gentle] If a work thought shows up, an email to send, a file to finish, let it pass. It has no place here for now.
You can even say to it, silently, later. Then come back to the warmth of your hands, and the rhythm of your breath.
[pause 3s]

[calm, clear] Keep your hands over your eyes, and now open your ears.
Listen to the sounds in the room. There are probably more than you thought.
The hum of a vent. A fridge or a computer whirring. Footsteps in a hallway, a voice in the distance, a car outside.
[pause 2s]
You don't need to name them, or to like them. Just let them come to you.
Notice the closest ones, then the farthest ones. And then the small silence between two sounds.
[pause 3s]
Some sounds are steady, like a backdrop. Others are brief, they arrive and disappear. For once, your ears are doing the work, and your eyes get to rest.
[pause 3s]

[soft, unhurried] Slowly, let your hands come down and rest on your thighs, or on the table. Keep your eyes closed a little longer.
Now feel your body. Your weight in the chair. Your feet on the floor. Your back against the backrest.
And your hands. Empty hands, for once. No mouse, no phone, no pen.
[pause 2s]
Feel the air on your palms. The warmth that's still there. A faint tingling in your fingertips.
Maybe your hands want to do something. Let them do nothing. It's rare enough to enjoy.
[pause 2s]
Notice the smell of the room as well. Coffee, paper, the air from a window left open. A sense no screen ever reaches.
Touch, hearing, smell. All of them were on standby while your eyes did the work. You've just given them a little room again.
[pause 2s]

[clear, gentle] Now get ready to open your eyes. Before you do, turn your head slightly toward a window, or toward the far end of the room.
Open your eyelids slowly. Let the light come back little by little.
[pause 2s]
And find the farthest point you can see. A building, the sky, a tree, the back wall.
Rest your gaze there. Keep it soft, without searching for detail. Notice the space between you and that point.
[pause 3s]
Then bring your gaze to something at mid distance. A plant, a door. And back out, far away. Your eyes loosen up, like a muscle being stretched.
[pause 3s]
Blink several times. It helps keep your eyes moist, and in front of a screen we often forget to do it.
[pause 2s]

[warm, grounded] Before you go back to your screens, choose one simple rule for the next hour.
For example, every twenty minutes, look at something far away for twenty seconds. Or leave your phone in the drawer until your next break.
Just one rule, easy to keep. Which one is yours?
[pause 3s]
It may seem tiny. But repeated day after day, a small rule often changes more than a big resolution.
[warm, calm, slowing down] Your eyes now remember what it's like to look far away. You can come back to it whenever they ask.
[pause 4s]
