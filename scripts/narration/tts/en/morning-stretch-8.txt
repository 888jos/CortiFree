# morning-stretch-8 · Mindful morning stretch (EN)
[pause 1.5s]
[warm, clear] Welcome to these eight minutes for loosening up your body. If you feel stiff this morning, or if sitting still to meditate feels like a struggle, this session is for you.
Here, we meditate by moving. Movement becomes the anchor, and the breath sets the pace.
[calm, reassuring] One rule, and only one, before we start. Never into pain. Always stay within what's comfortable. If a movement doesn't suit you, make it smaller, or just do it in your imagination.
And if standing isn't possible for you, you can follow the whole session on a chair, sitting slightly forward, away from the backrest.
[pause 2s]

[calm] Stand with your feet hip width apart. Toes pointing forward. Knees soft, not locked.
Let your arms hang by your sides. Feel the weight of your hands at the ends of your arms.
[pause 2s]
Bring your attention to your feet. Heels, soles, toes. Where is most of your weight resting?
[pause 2s]
Maybe more on one foot than the other, more on your heels than on the balls of your feet.
Sway very slightly, forward and back, then side to side, and find the center. The place where you can stand without effort.
This gentle swaying already wakes up your ankles and the muscles of your legs. They're the ones carrying you through this session.
[pause 2s]
[soft] Take a breath. Just notice what it's like this morning. Short, long, high in the chest, low in the belly.
[pause 3s]
[clear] From here on, let the breath guide every movement. The in breath opens and lifts. The out breath releases and lowers.
If you slept in a slightly awkward position, you may notice a particular stiffness, in your neck or lower back. Keep it in mind, and take extra care of that area.
[pause 1.5s]

[clear, calm] First movement. As you breathe in, slowly raise your arms out to the sides, then up over your head.
Your palms can meet, or stay shoulder width apart.
[pause 2s]
As you breathe out, let your arms come back down the same way, like a big wave.
[pause 3s]
Again. Breathe in, arms rising. Feel the space opening between your ribs, the air filling the top of your chest.
[pause 2s]
Breathe out, arms lowering. Your shoulders stay well away from your ears.
[pause 2s]
Do two or three more of these waves at your own pace. Match the speed of your arms to the length of your breath. If the in breath is short, the movement is short.
There's no need to fill your lungs to the max. We're looking for a smooth movement, not a feat.
[pause 8s]

[calm] Now, side bends. Breathe in, and raise your right arm over your head. Your left hand slides down the outside of your left thigh.
As you breathe out, lean gently to the left. Like a tree bending in the wind.
Feel the stretch all along your right side, from your hip to your fingertips.
Keep both feet firmly grounded. Don't try to go far. A small arc is plenty.
[soft] Breathe here. With each in breath, imagine the air flowing into the ribs on the stretched side.
[pause 5s]
Breathe in, and come back to center. Breathe out, arm lowering.
[pause 2s]
Now the other side. Breathe in, left arm rising. Breathe out, leaning to the right.
[pause 2s]
Does this side feel different? Looser, or stiffer?
[pause 2.5s]
The body is never perfectly symmetrical, and that's absolutely fine.
[pause 3s]
Breathe in, back to center. Breathe out, arm lowering.
[pause 2s]

[clear, calm] Now, a forward roll down. Bend your knees a little more. This matters, to protect your lower back.
As you breathe out, let your chin drop toward your chest. Then your shoulders round, and your back rolls down, one vertebra at a time.
Your arms hang heavy toward the floor. No need to touch your toes. Stop wherever feels comfortable, even halfway.
[pause 3s]
[soft] Let your head hang, neck relaxed. You can nod a small yes, then shake a small no, to release the back of your neck.
Breathe into your back. Feel it lengthen and widen with every in breath.
If you like, bend one knee and then the other, alternating, to gently stretch the backs of your legs. No bouncing.
[pause 5s]
[calm] To come up, press firmly into your feet. Over several breaths, roll your back up, one vertebra at a time. Your head comes up last.
Take your time. If you feel a little dizzy as you come up, stay still for a moment, knees soft, and breathe normally.
[pause 5s]

[warm, clear] Now, some gentle twists. Let your arms hang loose, relaxed like scarves.
Turn your upper body gently to the right, then to the left. Your arms follow, lightly tapping against your body as they swing.
Your feet stay put. Your hips can follow a little. Your gaze goes along with the movement.
Let the rhythm find itself. Like a child playing at swinging their arms.
Your breath follows freely. There's nothing to count.
[pause 3s]
Notice the feeling in your lower back, around your waist. That's often where the night has left some stiffness, and the movement eases it, little by little.
[pause 4s]
[slower] Slow down gradually. Smaller and smaller twists, until you come to rest at the center.
Take a moment to feel your lower back, still now. It often feels warmer and looser than at the start.
[pause 3s]

[calm, clear] Now, a chest opener. Bring your hands behind your back. Interlace your fingers if you can. If not, hold one wrist, or grip both ends of a towel.
As you breathe in, gently draw your hands down and away from your back. Your shoulder blades move toward each other. Your chest opens forward, as if you were turning it toward the sun.
Keep your neck long, chin level with the floor.
[soft] Breathe here for a few breaths. Feel the fronts of your shoulders stretching, right where all those hours hunched over a screen tend to settle.
[pause 6s]
As you breathe out, release your hands. Let your arms come back to your sides.
Roll your shoulders once or twice, to finish.
[pause 3s]

[soft, unhurried] Now, stay still. Remain standing, eyes closed or gaze resting on the floor.
Feel what's happening in your body after moving. Tingling in your hands. Warmth in your back. Your heartbeat, a little more noticeable.
Maybe there's more space in your chest, more length along your sides.
[pause 3s]
Feel your feet again. Is your weight spread differently than it was at the start?
[pause 2.5s]
Notice your breath too. It may have found a fuller rhythm, without you even trying.
[warm] This body that felt stiff a few minutes ago is now a little more alive. You didn't force anything. You simply listened to it.
Your body has its own wisdom. It knows what does it good, when you give it time to tell you.
[pause 3s]

[calm] Take one last full breath, raising your arms one more time, if you feel like it.
[pause 3s]
And as you breathe out, let them float back down, slowly.
[pause 4s]
Feel your feet on the ground one last time, before picking up the thread of your morning.
[warm, clear] Carry this looser body with you into your day. And when the stiffness creeps back, at your computer or on your commute, remember that a single side bend, a single roll down, a single breath can bring back a little of this ease.
Even two minutes of stretching tomorrow morning can change the way you start your day.
[pause 1.5s]
[warm, slowing down] Have a good day. Take it gently.
[pause 4s]
