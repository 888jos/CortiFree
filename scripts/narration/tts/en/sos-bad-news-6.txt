# sos-bad-news-6 · After bad news (EN)
[pause 1.5s]
[warm, calm, gentle] Something hard has just happened. A call, a message, an announcement.
You don't have to understand or decide anything right now. Just take this time for yourself.
[pause 2s]
[soft] If you can, sit down. Or lean your back against a wall.
If you're outside, find a bench, a low wall, a quieter corner. Put your phone down if you can, screen facing the table, or tucked in your pocket.
[pause 2.5s]

[calm, unhurried] Let's start by looking around you. Slowly, let your eyes travel around the place you're in.
The walls, their color. A window, the light coming in. A piece of furniture, a familiar object.
[pause 2s]
Let your eyes rest on three things, one after another, without hurrying.
Name them silently, simply. The lamp. The door. My mug.
[pause 3s]
[reassuring] It helps your mind remember where you are. After a shock, everything can feel unreal. Looking at what surrounds you is a gentle way back into the present.
[pause 1.5s]
[grounded] Now feel the floor beneath your feet. It's there, steady. Feel the seat holding you, the backrest behind you.
You're here, in this room. What just happened is real. And you are here too.
Both are true at the same time. The news, and this floor holding you up.
[pause 2s]
Notice the temperature of the air on your hands, on your face. The touch of your clothes. These small, ordinary things are still here.
[pause 3s]

[soft, slow] Let your breathing slow down a little, if it's willing. There's no goal here.
Just a breath in, whenever it comes...
[pause 3s]
and a breath out, a little longer, if that's possible.
[pause 5s]
[soft] Another one. The air comes in however it can...
[pause 3s]
and goes out, without effort, without trying to do it right.
[pause 5s]
[gentle, reassuring] If your breath stays short or ragged, that's okay too. Your body is reacting to something big. You don't have to correct it.
If you like, press your feet a little more firmly into the floor, and let the exhale leave through your mouth, like a sigh.
[pause 5s]

[warm, gentle] Whatever you're feeling right now is normal.
Maybe a kind of numbness, as if everything were wrapped in cotton wool, with no clear thoughts. That's a very common reaction to shock.
Maybe tears are rising. You can let them come. They belong here.
Maybe anger, disbelief, an urge to run away.
Or thoughts racing in every direction. What if, what now, how will I cope. You don't have to answer them yet.
Or maybe nothing at all, for now. A big blank. That happens too, and it doesn't mean this doesn't matter.
[pause 2s]
[calm] There's no right way to react. All of these are ways for your body to absorb the blow.
And they may shift through the day, coming and going in waves. That's normal too.
You don't have to choose how to feel. Just let whatever is here exist, for the length of this pause.
[pause 2.5s]

[soft, intimate] If you'd like, place a hand on your heart. Or on your belly. Wherever feels most right.
Feel the warmth of your hand, its gentle weight. Like a friend's hand resting on you.
[pause 2s]
And say to yourself, softly... This is hard, what's happening to me. I'm here for myself.
Or another phrase that speaks to you more. I'll take this one step at a time. It's okay for this to hurt.
[pause 4s]
[soft] Keep your hand there a little longer, if it feels good. Breathe beneath it.
Feel your chest or your belly moving softly under your palm. You're keeping yourself company.
This gesture may seem very small. Yet many people find it soothes a little, like having someone there.
[pause 3s]

[calm, clear] Now, just one question. Not for tomorrow, not for the week. Only for the next hour.
What do I need, in the next hour?
[pause 3s]
Maybe a glass of water. Something to eat. Some fresh air. Lying down. Letting someone know you won't be able to do what was planned.
Choose just one thing. Simple, concrete, doable. One, not a list. The rest will come later.
[warm] This isn't a small detail. Looking after your body now is what will help you face what comes next.
[pause 3s]

[gentle] Think of a person, too. Someone you could call or text, if you need to share some of this weight.
Someone close, a friend, a neighbor. You don't have to explain everything. Something hard is happening, can you call me... That's enough.
Choose that person now, even if you only reach out later. See their face, hear their voice, just for a moment.
[pause 3s]
[reassuring] And if no one comes to mind, or if it feels too heavy, support lines exist, and your doctor can be a source of support too.
You don't have to carry this alone. Asking for help isn't a weakness. It's one way of taking care of yourself.
[pause 3s]

[soft, unhurried] Come back to your breath, just as it is now. Feel your feet on the floor again, and the seat beneath you.
You don't need to feel better right away. What happened will take time, and that's normal.
The next few hours may have their ups and downs. You can come back to this pause as many times as you need.
[pause 2s]
[warm, calm] For now, you've done something important. You stopped, and you took care of yourself for a few minutes.
[soft, slowing down] When the moment comes, move toward what you need in the next hour. One thing at a time.
[pause 4s]
