[warm, soft, unhurried] Tonight, let's play a little game. A very quiet game, played with your eyes closed, lying in bed.
[pause 2s]
[soft] First, settle in as if you were going to sleep. Turn onto whichever side you like. Let your head find its place in the pillow.
Pull the covers up to wherever feels right. Let your arms rest wherever they want to, without arranging them.
[pause 3s]
[calm] If your mind is reasoning tonight, arguing, planning tomorrow, this game is for you.
As you fall asleep, your thoughts change shape. They come loose. An image of a cat, then a staircase, then a colour, with no link between them.
Some researchers think you can imitate that moment. By picturing random objects, unrelated to one another, you give the brain the impression that sleep is already on its way.
And above all, you keep it just busy enough to let go of its ruminating. It's hard to replay an argument while you're picturing a giraffe.
[pause 2s]
[clear, calm] The rule is simple. I'll give you a word, and we'll take it one letter at a time.
For each letter, I'll suggest a few objects beginning with that letter. You picture one for a few seconds, then move on to the next.
You don't need to see them clearly. A blurry image is enough. Even just the idea of an image is enough.
[reassuring] This isn't a memory test, or an imagination contest. If an object doesn't come, move on to the next one. If a different object turns up instead, take it. It'll do just fine.
And above all, don't try to connect them. No story, no logic, no reason. An apple, then a piano. That's all there is to it.
[pause 2s]
[soft] Before we start, two easy breaths.
Breathe in through your nose, without forcing...
[pause 3s]
[slow] and breathe out gently, letting your body sink a little further into the mattress.
[pause 5s]
[soft] Once more. The air comes in, cool in your nostrils...
[pause 3s]
and flows out, a little warmer, a little slower.
[pause 5s]
Then let your breath find its own rhythm again. You don't need to look after it anymore.
