# work-after-criticism-6 · After criticism (EN)
[pause 1.5s]
[warm, calm] You've just heard something that hurts. Negative feedback, a tough performance review, a passing comment that stuck with you.
And it stings. That's human. Nobody likes hearing that their work isn't good enough, even when the criticism is fair.
[pause 2s]
[soft, unhurried] Find a place where you can have a little quiet. Sit down, with your back supported and your feet on the floor.
You can close your eyes, or lower your gaze to a spot in front of you.
Take one breath that's a little slower than the others. Nothing more for now.
[pause 3s]
Let the air come in and go out at its own pace. Feel the chair beneath you, your feet against the floor.
These are simple, steady things. They were here before that feedback, and they'll be here after.
If your mind drifts back to the scene, that's normal. Just come back to the feel of your feet, one more time.
[pause 2s]
[calm, intimate] Now let's look for where that criticism has landed in your body.
For some people, it's heat in the face, in the cheeks or the ears. For others, a lump in the throat, a weight on the chest, a hollow feeling in the stomach.
Where is it for you, right now?
[pause 3s]
It doesn't have to be clear. A vague impression is enough.
When you find a spot, stay next to it. You don't have to dive in.
Imagine your breath flowing toward that area. As if each inhale gave it a little more room, and each exhale softened it.
You don't have to make the feeling go away. It can stay as long as it needs to. You just breathe alongside it, the way you'd sit beside someone who's hurting.
[pause 3s]
[gentle, warm] If you like, rest a hand on that spot. Or over your heart, or on your arm. A warm, simple touch.
It's the gesture you'd offer a friend coming out of a hard meeting.
Now say to yourself, in the kindest voice you have... this is hard to hear.
Let the words land. You're not complaining. You're simply naming what's true.
[pause 3s]
You can add... I'm not the only one who goes through this. Everyone, at some point, gets criticism that hurts.
And maybe... what do I need, right now? A little patience with myself. A little time.
[pause 2s]
[warm, clear] This isn't letting yourself off the hook. It's what allows you to look at the criticism without getting defensive, and without crumbling.
When we talk to ourselves harshly, we tend to shut down. When we're gentle, we can actually face what happened.
[pause 3s]
[clear, calm] Now that the sting has eased a little, let's sort things out together.
Think back to what was actually said. Not everything you felt. Just the words, the facts.
For example, the report came in two days late. Or, the presentation needed more data. Those are facts.
And then there's what your mind made of them. I'm useless. I'll never get this right. They're going to realize I don't belong here.
That's not what was said. It's an interpretation. A sweeping judgment added on top of specific feedback.
In a matter of seconds, a comment about one report becomes a verdict on who you are. It's a very common shortcut when we feel under attack.
Try to separate the two, like sorting two piles of paper on a desk. On one side, what was really said. On the other, the story you're telling yourself.
[pause 3s]
[calm] Look at the pile of facts. If you could keep just one useful thing, what would it be?
[pause 2s]
A piece of information that can help you grow, even a little. A concrete point, something you can act on.
Choose it. Just one. The rest can be set aside for today.
You can even turn it into a small action. Next time, I'll flag it as soon as I sense the deadline is tight.
And if part of the criticism feels unfair, you're allowed to think so. You can bring it up later, once things have cooled down.
As for the pile of judgments about yourself, you don't need to keep it. It teaches you nothing new. It just hurts.
[pause 3s]
[warm] To rebalance the scales, bring to mind a recent success. Something that went well in the last few weeks.
A project wrapped up, a happy client, a colleague you helped, a problem you solved.
Replay that moment in a little detail. Where you were, what you did, how it felt.
[pause 3s]
That success is just as real as today's criticism. Both are part of your work.
You don't have to choose between them. You can see something to improve and, at the same time, know what you do well.
[pause 2s]
[soft, unhurried] Come back to your breath. Notice the place in your body you visited earlier. Has anything shifted, even a tiny bit?
[pause 3s]
If the sting is still there, that's normal. It will fade in its own time.
You might also offer yourself something kind in the next hour. A glass of water, a short walk, a message to someone you trust.
[pause 2s]
[warm, calm] Wiggle your fingers, roll your shoulders, and come back to the room around you.
That feedback was about a piece of work, at one moment in time. It doesn't define who you are. You're much bigger than a few sentences.
[soft, warm, slowing down] And you leave with the one useful thing you chose to keep.
