For this exercise, keep your eyes open. You can do it sitting or standing, in your kitchen or on a train platform.
[pause 1.5s]
[grounded] Place both feet flat on the floor. Feel the ground holding you, under your heels, under your toes.
Now let the air out through your mouth, slowly, as if you were blowing on soup that's too hot.
[pause 5s]
Once more. A long, easy out breath, without forcing the in breath at all.

[calm, clear] Anxiety likes to live in tomorrow. What might happen, what might go wrong. Your senses only know about now.
So we'll use them like a rope, to pull you back here. Five things, then four, then three, then two, and finally one. Take your time with each step. There's no rush.
If a thought pulls you toward tomorrow, that's okay. Notice it, and come back to the step you were on.
[pause 2s]

[clear] Start with your eyes. Look for five things you can see.
They don't need to be special. The corner of a table, a power outlet, a mug, the sky through the window.
Rest your gaze on the first one. Look at it as if you had to describe it to someone who has never seen it.
What is its exact color?
[pause 2s]
Not just white, but off white, a slightly grey white, a white that shines where the light hits it.
Notice its edges. Are they sharp, rounded, a little worn?
[pause 3s]
Move on to the second thing. Take in its shape, its shadow, the small details you've never noticed. A scratch, a reflection, a speck of dust.
[pause 2s]
Then the third. And the fourth, maybe a little further away. Let your eyes travel slowly, without hurrying.
[pause 2s]
And a fifth. If your eyes want to go back to one of them, that's allowed.
[pause 3s]

[calm] Now, touch. Find four things you can feel against your skin.
The first may already be there. The weight of your body on the chair, or your feet inside your shoes.
Notice the pressure. Is it stronger on one side than the other? On your heels, or on the balls of your feet?
[pause 2s]
The second is the fabric of your clothes. On your shoulders, on your thighs. Is it soft, scratchy, snug, light?
[pause 1.5s]
For the third, you can place a hand on something. A table, your knee, the armrest. Feel whether it's cool or warm, smooth or rough.
[pause 1.5s]
And the fourth is more subtle. The air on your face, on the backs of your hands. Maybe a slight draft, maybe a little warmth.
[pause 3s]

[soft, calm] Now, sounds. Listen, and find three of them.
Start with the most obvious one. A car, a voice, the hum of a fridge or a computer.
[pause 2s]
Then look for a more distant sound, right at the edge of what you can hear. Let your listening widen, as if your ears were opening a little more.
[pause 2s]
And finally, a sound very close to you. The rustle of your clothes when you move, or the quiet sound of your own breathing.
[pause 2s]
You don't need to name each sound precisely. Just notice that it's there, and then that it changes, or stops.
If a thought slips in among the sounds, let it drift into the background, like a radio playing in the next room.
[pause 2s]

[gentle] Now, two smells. This is often the hardest step, so be patient with yourself.
Breathe in gently through your nose, without forcing it. Maybe there's a hint of coffee, laundry soap on your sweater, hand soap on your fingers.
You can bring your wrist or your sleeve up to your nose.
[pause 2s]
If you can't smell anything in particular, that's completely fine too. Then imagine two smells you love. Warm bread from a bakery in the morning. Freshly cut grass. The peel of an orange you've just opened.
Let the memory of those smells come back, as vividly as you can.
[pause 3s]

[soft] And finally, one taste. Notice what's in your mouth right now.
Maybe the trace of your last drink, toothpaste, or just a neutral taste.
Run your tongue over your teeth, along the roof of your mouth. Feel the saliva, the temperature inside your mouth.
If you have a glass of water nearby, you can take a slow sip, and follow its coolness until it fades away.
[pause 3s]
[warm, clear] Each time you explore something with your senses, your attention steps out of the worried story. It's simple, and that's why it helps.
[pause 1.5s]

[grounded, calm] Now come back to your whole body. Your feet on the floor. Your hands resting. Your eyes taking in the room.
Let your out breath lengthen one more time, easily.
[pause 5s]
Take a moment to notice how you feel, without expecting anything. Maybe your anxiety has dropped a notch, or several. Maybe it's still here, but a little less overwhelming.
If you had to give it a score out of ten right now, what would it be?
[pause 3s]
Just compare it with earlier, without judging the number.
[reassuring] If nothing has changed, that isn't a failure. You spent a few minutes here, instead of in tomorrow. That's exactly what the practice is.
[pause 2s]
[warm] Keep this method in your pocket. In a waiting room, before a phone call, in the middle of a restless night, it's always there. It asks for nothing more than what's already around you.
Five things seen, four touched. Three heard, two smelled, and one tasted.
[pause 2s]
[calm, slowing down] Before you go back to your day, choose one of the things you saw earlier, and look at it one last time. Let it be your little reminder. Here and now is where you actually live.
