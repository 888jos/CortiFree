# anxiety-social-event-6 · Before a party or a date (EN)
[pause 1.5s]
[warm, calm] In a few hours, or maybe a few minutes, you'll be meeting people. A party, a dinner, a first date. And something inside you is tightening.
Maybe your throat, your stomach, slightly clammy hands. Maybe that small voice suggesting you cancel.
[pause 2s]
[reassuring] All of this is deeply human. Feeling nervous before seeing people doesn't mean you're strange, or that the evening will go badly. It just means it matters to you.
So to begin, there's nothing to fix. Only a little room for what you're feeling.
[pause 2s]
[calm] Settle in wherever you are, sitting or standing. You can keep your eyes open, or let them half close.
If your heart is beating fast, let it beat. That's your body getting ready, like before a game or an exam. There's nothing dangerous about it.
[pause 2s]

[calm, clear] First, let's talk to the body, because the body is what sounded the alarm.
Breathe in calmly through your nose, no bigger than usual...
[pause 3s]
[slow] Then breathe out through your mouth, slowly, as if you were fogging up a window. Let the out breath last longer than the in breath.
[pause 6s]
[soft] Once more. Breathe in, normally...
[pause 3s]
[slow] And breathe out, long and soft, all the way to the end.
[pause 6s]
[gentle] Now look at your hands. Are they clenched?
[pause 2s]
If they are, open them. Gently spread your fingers, then let them fall back, loose, onto your thighs.
Notice the warmth coming back into your palms. Let your shoulders drop half an inch. Unclench your jaw, let your tongue rest.
[slow] One last long out breath, and let your body sink a little into the seat.
[pause 5s]
[calm, clear] Breathing out slowly is a simple way of telling your nervous system it can slow down. You don't have to believe it. You just have to do it.
[pause 3s]

[calm] Now let's see what your mind is doing. There's a good chance it's already playing a little movie.
The moment you walk in and nobody talks to you. The awkward silence. The joke that falls flat. The other person looking bored.
These images feel real, and the body reacts to them as if they were.
[pause 2s]
And yet they all have one thing in common. None of them has happened yet. They're predictions.
[clear] So let's try something. Each time one of these scenes plays, simply say to yourself, silently, prediction.
They'll think I'm boring. Prediction. I'm going to blush. Prediction. I won't know what to say. Prediction.
You're not trying to prove it's false. You're just noticing that it's a guess your brain is making, not a fact.
[pause 3s]
[warm] And remember the times you dreaded an evening, and it actually went well, or at least better than you expected. Your brain tends to forget those.
Tonight too, there's a good chance reality will be simpler than the movie.
[pause 2s]

[warm, clear] Here's something many people find reassuring. Tonight, most of the people you meet will mostly be thinking about... themselves.
About their outfit, about what they're going to say, about that person they don't know. Some of them will have the same knot in their stomach that you have.
Psychologists even have a name for it. The spotlight effect. We feel as if we're under the stage lights, when in fact other people notice us far less than we think.
[pause 2s]
You're not on stage. You're one person among others, all of whom would like to have a nice time.
And if you blush, or search for your words, most people won't even notice. The ones who do often find it rather endearing.
[pause 2s]

[calm, encouraging] So let's change the goal. Instead of trying to come across well, to be funny, interesting, perfect, try a lighter intention. Curiosity.
Being curious about at least one person. Who they are, what lights them up, how they see things.
When your attention goes out toward someone else, it steps away from that spotlight pointed at you. And awkwardness often loosens its grip.
Put this intention into your own words. For example, tonight, I want to get to know someone. Or, tonight, I'm listening.
[pause 3s]
To make it easier, prepare one simple question you can pull out of your pocket if a silence settles in.
An open question, one that invites a story. What's been keeping you busy lately? How do you know everyone here? What was the best thing you did this summer?
Pick one. Say it once in your head, in your own voice, calmly.
[pause 2s]
Now picture the person answering, and you listening, really listening, nodding along. Nothing else to get right.
[pause 3s]

[clear, grounded] One small gesture for the moment you arrive. Just before you ring the bell or push the door open, pause for a couple of seconds. Feel your feet in your shoes, open your hands, and let out a long breath.
Then walk in and look for a first face to smile at. You don't need to shine. A hello, a name, your question, and you're on your way.
And if a silence happens anyway, it's not a disaster. You can take a sip, smile, or ask your question. Silences are part of every conversation.
[pause 2s]
[soft] Come back to your breath. A long out breath. Hands open. Feet grounded.
[pause 4s]
[warm, reassuring] And keep one last thing in mind. You're allowed to leave whenever you want. If after an hour you've had enough, you can say goodbye and go home. That's allowed, and it doesn't make you any less likeable.
Knowing the door is open often makes it easier to walk through it.
[pause 2s]
[warm, slowing down] You're going with your nerves, your question, and your curiosity. That's more than enough.
[pause 4s]
