[warm, calm] Something went wrong. You forgot something, sent an email too fast, said something that hurt, missed an appointment. And ever since, it's been going round and round in your head.
[pause 1.5s]
[gentle] Before anything else, notice this. If you're upset with yourself, it's because it matters to you. People, good work, the trust others place in you. This discomfort comes from something precious.
Over these six minutes, we won't erase what happened. Instead, you'll look at it clearly, without beating yourself up, and see what you can do with it.

[pause 6s]
[grounded] Feel your feet in contact with the floor. It's solid. Whatever happened, the ground is still there beneath you.

[calm] Now let the mistake come back to mind. Not replaying it on a loop. Just placing it in front of you, like a photo on a table.
And notice what it does in your body. Shame and guilt often have a precise address. Heat rising in your face. A hollow feeling in your stomach. Shoulders curling forward, as if you wanted to hide.
Where is it, for you?
[pause 2.5s]
You can simply name what you find. Heat. Tightness. Heaviness. Without trying to push it away.
[pause 3s]

[clear] Now listen to what you've been telling yourself since the mistake. Maybe, I'm useless. I'm hopeless. I always ruin everything.
Look closely at the difference between two sentences. The first is, I made a mistake. The second is, I am a mistake.
The first is about an action. One specific moment, which can be understood and repaired. The second is about the whole of you, and it leaves no way out.
[pause 1.5s]
Guilt says, what I did wasn't right. It moves you to make amends. Shame says, I'm worthless. It makes you want to hide, or freeze.
[pause 1.5s]
[gentle] If you hear that second sentence in your head, try rephrasing it. Bring it back to its true size. I forgot that file. I spoke too harshly. I made a mistake, on that day, in those circumstances.
Notice what changes in your body when the sentence shrinks back to its real size. Often, your chest breathes a little easier.
[pause 3s]

[warm, intimate] Now imagine a friend who knows you well and cares about you, no matter what. They've heard the whole story. They sit down beside you.
They don't tell you it's nothing. They don't pretend. But they speak to you warmly.
Maybe they say, you were running on no sleep that day, you had a thousand things on your mind. Or, everyone gets things wrong, it doesn't take anything away from who you are. Or, I can see you're upset with yourself, and that shows how much this matters to you.
[pause 2s]
Listen to what they would say, in their own words. And let those words actually reach you.
Let them land the way a warm hand would on your shoulder. You don't have to agree with every word. Just let yourself hear them.
[pause 5s]
[soft] Notice if something loosens a little. Your shoulders, your belly, your breathing. Even a slight shift counts.
And notice that you can hold two things at once. Regret for what happened, and kindness toward the person who did it. One doesn't cancel out the other.
[pause 3s]

[calm, clear] Now that you're a bit calmer, you can look at the situation with clearer eyes. What can be repaired?
[pause 2.5s]
Sometimes it's a sincere apology. Sometimes a message to correct some information, a phone call, an offer to catch up on what was delayed. And sometimes nothing can be repaired, and the only possible action is to do things differently next time.
Choose one single action. Small, concrete, doable. Picture yourself doing it. The time of day, the first words you'll say or write.
If it helps, decide when you'll do it. Today, tomorrow morning, right after lunch. Writing it down can help, too.
[pause 3s]
[gentle] Then ask yourself, gently, what is this mistake teaching me? Maybe that you need to write important things down. To say no more often. To slow down before replying when you're running on empty.
One lesson, just one, is enough. You don't need to put yourself on trial.
Mistakes are how every human being learns. Including the people you admire most.

[soft, warm] If you like, place a hand on your heart or your arm, and tell yourself, I made a mistake, and I still deserve kindness. Or, in your own words, I'm learning, like everyone else.

[reassuring] If strong emotions come up during this practice, that's normal. You can open your eyes, come back to your feet on the floor, or take a break. There's no rush.
[soft] Stay for a few more breaths with whatever you're feeling now. Without forcing yourself to feel better. Just being here, on your own side.
