# focus-visual-anchor-6 · Visual anchor (EN)
[pause 1.5s]
[warm, calm] This time, you're going to meditate with your eyes open. If closing your eyes makes you restless, or puts you to sleep, this practice is for you. It's also a way of meditating you can use at work, without anyone noticing.
[pause 1.5s]
[clear] Start by choosing a simple object in front of you. A mug, a plant, a pen, a stone, the flame of a candle if you have one. Ideally something still, at a comfortable distance, a little below eye level.
Take your time choosing it, and moving it into place if you need to.
[pause 3s]

[calm, unhurried] Settle in so your head is upright and your neck relaxed. Your eyes don't need to stare hard. Your gaze is soft and resting, the way you'd watch the countryside from a train window.
You can blink as much as you need. We're not trying to stare without blinking, just to keep the object as a point of reference.
[soft] Take an easy breath...
[pause 3s]
and as you breathe out, let the small muscles around your eyes soften. Your forehead, your eyebrows, your eyelids.
[pause 3s]
[reassuring] There's nothing to get right here. Just looking, with a little more attention than usual.
[pause 2s]

[calm] Now look at your object as if you were seeing it for the very first time. As if you didn't know what it was for, or what it was called.
Start with its overall shape. Its outline. Is it rounded, angular, irregular?
[pause 2.5s]
Where does it end, and where does everything around it begin? Slowly trace the outline with your eyes, as if you were drawing it in one continuous line.
[pause 3s]
Then its colors. Not just white or green, but every shade. Where the color is lighter, where it deepens. Maybe tones you'd never noticed before.
If a word pops up, blue, beige, let it pass, and come back to what you actually see, beyond the word.
[pause 2s]
Now move in closer to the details, with your eyes only. A scratch, a small mark, the texture of the surface. Does it look smooth, grainy, matte, glossy?
[pause 2s]
Notice the shadows. Which side is the light coming from? How does the object's shadow fall across the table? Maybe its edges are sharp, or maybe they're soft and blurry.
And the reflections. A small point of light on the rim of a mug, the sheen on a leaf, the flicker of a flame that never stops moving.
[soft] Let your gaze explore slowly, unhurried, like a hand discovering the object with its fingertips.
[pause 4s]

[gentle] At some point, you'll notice you're not really looking anymore. Your eyes are still open, but your mind is somewhere else. A shopping list, a conversation, a worry.
Or the mind starts commenting. It's a mug, I know what it is, I get it. That's very common too.
As soon as you notice, come back to the object. Pick one specific detail, and rest your gaze on it, like returning to the page where you left off reading.
[reassuring] No need to judge yourself. The mind wanders, you come back. That's the very movement of the practice.
You can even note where you went. Planning, remembering, worrying. One word, then back to the detail.
[pause 4s]

[calm] Keep exploring. If you feel you've seen everything, look again. There's always another shade, another angle, a speck of dust.
You might notice the image shifting slightly as you look at it for a long time. The edges blur, the colors seem to shimmer. That's normal, it's just your vision at work. Simply blink and come back.
[pause 3s]
[unhurried] Now, without moving your eyes, let your field of vision widen. Your gaze stays on the object, but your attention opens to everything around it.
The table, what's to the left, to the right. The top of your visual field, the bottom. All the way to the edges, where shapes go soft and blurry.
[pause 2s]
You see everything at once, without lingering on anything. A panoramic view, like a wide angle lens.
You can even feel your breathing and your hands resting at the same time, without your gaze leaving the object. Seeing and feeling, together.
[pause 2s]
Many people find this wide gaze soothing, and that it brings a sense of space. Is that true for you?
[pause 3s]
If a movement catches your attention, someone walking by, a screen lighting up, let it be part of the scene, without following it.
Stay in this open view for a few more moments.
[pause 5s]
[clear] Notice how this wide view resembles the attention a long task needs. Centered, but not tense.
[pause 1.5s]
[calm] And now, gently bring your attention back to the object. Like a zoom lens closing in. The edges become simple background again, and the object returns to the center.
Look at it again. Does it seem different from before? More present, sharper, more familiar? Or exactly the same? Whatever you see is fine.
[pause 2s]
[soft] Rest your gaze on a single detail, the one you like best. And stay with it for a few breaths.
[pause 6s]

[warm] This object, or any other, can become an anchor in your day. Between two emails, in the middle of a meeting, you can rest your gaze on one detail for thirty seconds, and come back to yourself.
[pause 2s]
[soft] Now close your eyes for a moment. Notice any image that may linger behind your eyelids. Notice your eyes resting.
[pause 4s]
Take a slightly fuller breath.
[pause 4s]
[warm, clear] Then open your eyes again, slowly. Let the light come back in. Look around the room, and notice what you see now.
[pause 4s]
