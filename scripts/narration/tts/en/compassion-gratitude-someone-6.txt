# compassion-gratitude-someone-6 · Someone who matters (EN)

[warm, calm] Hello. This practice is a little different from the others. There's no problem to solve here, and no tension to undo. You're simply going to think about someone who matters to you.
[pause 2s]
When stress builds up, we tend to close in on ourselves. Remembering the connections that hold us up is a very simple way to feel supported, even when things are hard.
[pause 2s]
[soft, unhurried] Settle in comfortably, with your back supported. Your eyes can close, or rest softly on a point in front of you.
Breathe in through your nose, and let your belly swell a little...
[pause 4s]
[almost a sigh] And breathe out slowly, as if you were blowing across a cup of hot tea.

[soft] Once more. Breathe in...

and let it go.

Now let your breathing find its own rhythm. You don't need to guide it anymore. Just feel the air coming in, cool, and going out, a little warmer.
Notice where your body is resting, too. Your weight on the seat, your hands in your lap. There's nothing to get right here. Only a memory to let in.
[pause 2s]

[warm, gentle] Bring to mind someone who helped you at some point, in one way or another. Not necessarily the person closest to you. Maybe a grandmother, a childhood friend, a colleague, a teacher, a neighbor.
Don't look for the perfect person. Take the first one who comes, the one who brings a small smile inside.
If several faces appear, choose just one for today. The others can come back another time.
[pause 2s]
[reassuring] And if no one comes right away, that's fine. You can think of someone who did you a simple kindness. A stranger who helped with a heavy suitcase, a pharmacist who took the time to listen.
[pause 3s]

[soft, intimate] Now try to see this person in detail. Their face. The color of their eyes. The shape of their smile, or that little crease between their eyebrows when they're thinking.
What are they wearing, in your memory? Where are they?
[pause 2s]
Maybe in a kitchen, on a park bench, behind a desk, on the other end of the phone.
And their voice. The sound of it, the way they say your name. A phrase they often use.
[pause 2s]
Let this presence settle near you, as if they had just sat down in the room. Notice what that already changes, simply imagining them here.
[pause 3s]

[warm] Now remember one particular moment when this person really mattered. One specific day.
Maybe they listened when things weren't okay. Maybe they believed in you before you believed in yourself. Maybe they did something quiet and kind. Dropped off some soup, gave you a ride, sent a message at exactly the right time.
[pause 2s]
Bring that moment back as concretely as you can. Where were you? What time of day was it? What did they say, or do?
[pause 3s]
And what did you feel, in that moment? Relief, maybe. Surprise. The feeling of mattering to someone.
[pause 2s]
[gentle] You can also ask yourself what this person passed on to you. A way of laughing at small troubles. Pride in doing things well. The idea that you were allowed to make mistakes. Perhaps even today, without realizing it, you carry a little of them in the way you are.
[pause 4s]

[soft, calm] Now bring your attention to your chest. Is there something there? A warmth, a slight opening, a kind of fullness?
[pause 3s]
Gratitude is often felt in the upper body. Sometimes around the heart, sometimes in the throat, sometimes just as a smile at the corner of your mouth.
If you feel something, let it take up a little more room. Like a light being turned up slowly on a dimmer switch.
[pause 2s]
[reassuring] And if you don't feel anything in particular, that's completely fine. Thinking of them with care is already a lot. The feeling may come another time.
Sometimes an unexpected emotion rises, too. Nostalgia, or sadness, if this person is far away, or no longer here. That's normal. Meet it gently, and if it's too much, come back to your breath or open your eyes.
[pause 3s]

[warm, intimate] Now, silently, say thank you to them. A real thank you, in your own words.
You might say what you've never said out loud. Thank you for being there that day. Thank you for your patience. Thank you for believing in me.
[pause 3s]
If the words don't come, you can simply send them a look, a nod, the warmth you're feeling. They would understand.
Imagine they can hear you. See their face as they receive your words. Maybe they smile, or look surprised, a little moved.
[pause 2s]
If you like, rest a hand on your chest as you thank them. As if to keep this moment close.
[pause 4s]

[soft, unhurried] Stay with this warmth for a few more breaths. Notice how simply thinking about someone can change the atmosphere inside you.
[pause 4s]
You're part of a web of connections. People have supported you. And you too, without always knowing it, have mattered to others.
[calm, clear] Research suggests that regularly bringing this kind of gratitude to mind can help us feel more connected to others. And connection is one of our best buffers against stress.
[pause 2s]
[soft] Gently, let the image fade. Come back to your breath, to your body resting here, to the sounds around you.
Move your hands and feet a little. And when the moment feels right, open your eyes.
[pause 2s]
[warm, slowing down] One last idea to take with you. What if you told them today? A message of a few words is enough. You might be surprised by the difference it makes, on both sides.
