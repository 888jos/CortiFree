# sleep-story-night-train-25 · The night train (EN)
[pause 1.5s]
[warm, calm, unhurried] Take a moment to settle in. Lie down however you like, pull the duvet over your shoulders, and let your head find the hollow of the pillow.
[pause 2s]
Tonight's story is a journey. A long night journey by train, from Paris all the way to Italy.
You have nothing to prepare, no ticket to look for, no suitcase to carry. Everything is ready.
[soft, reassuring] And you don't need to reach the end of the journey. Most night travellers fall asleep long before the border. If you drift off along the way, the train will carry on without you, quietly, until morning.
[pause 4s]

[storytelling, calm] It's a winter evening in Paris.
It's cold outside, a dry, clear cold. The pavements gleam under the street lamps. Your breath makes small white clouds in front of your mouth.
You reach the Gare de Lyon a little before nine. You have a small bag over your shoulder, not very heavy. Just what you need for one night and a few days.
You push open the big glass door and step into the hall.
[pause 2s]
It's warmer in here. The hall is vast, with its high, dark glass roof above your head and its big round clocks. Sounds echo under the roof. Footsteps, wheeled suitcases, a distant announcement, the coo of a pigeon perched on a beam.
At this hour, there aren't many people left. The commuter trains have gone. A few travellers remain, like you, leaving for the night. They walk without hurrying. Nobody runs.
You pass a newspaper kiosk pulling down its metal shutter. A bakery, where an assistant is slipping the last croissants into a paper bag.
[pause 2s]
You stop for a moment beside a piano, left in the middle of the hall for travellers. Someone is playing very softly, a slow melody, a few notes that rise into the glass roof and get lost there. You listen for a while. Then you go on your way.
On the big departures board, the white letters flip over one by one with a small clatter. Your train is there, at the top of the list. Night train. Departing at twenty one twelve. Platform M.
You go down to the platform.
[pause 3s]

The train is already there. It's waiting for you, long and dark, under the orange glow of the lamps. Its carriages are midnight blue, with a thin gold line beneath the windows. There are so many of them that you can't see where it ends.
Along the platform, a few staff in uniform wait at the door of each carriage. They hold lists, and they have torches clipped to their belts.
The air smells of cold metal, and faintly of coffee from the platform kiosk.
[pause 2s]
You walk along the train, reading the carriage numbers. The windows glow with a soft yellow light. Behind some of them, you glimpse people settling in. A woman hanging up her coat. An old man opening a book. A child in pyjamas, nose pressed to the glass, giving you a little wave.
You wave back. The child smiles, then disappears behind the curtain, no doubt sent off to bed by a patient parent.
[pause 2s]
Here's your carriage. The attendant checks your ticket, stamps it, and hands it back with a small nod. Have a good journey, he says.
You climb the three metal steps. Inside, it's warm. A narrow corridor with blue carpet runs alongside the compartments. The walls are varnished wood, and brass wall lights give off a golden glow.
You walk down the corridor. The floor creaks a little under your feet. Your cabin is the third one.
You slide the door open. It rolls gently on its runner and closes behind you with a soft, muffled click.
[pause 3s]

[soft, storytelling] The cabin is small, but it has everything. Everything has its place, as on a boat.
There's a berth, already made up. White sheets, pulled tight, folded over a blue wool blanket. A thick, plump pillow. At the foot of the berth, a little ladder, and above, another berth, empty tonight. You have the cabin to yourself.
Under the window, there's a tiny fold down table, and on it, a bottle of water and a little pouch with a flannel and a bar of soap.
In one corner, a steel washbasin, with a mirror and a small tap. Above it, a net for your things. And hooks to hang your coat.
[pause 2s]
Everything is small, clever, well thought out. A handle to hold beside the berth. A little round mirror. A blue night light down by the floor, to help you find your slippers in the dark. You feel as if you're in a den, or a ship's cabin. A place made for shelter.
You put your bag in the net. You take off your coat and your scarf and hang them up. You take off your shoes and tuck them under the berth, side by side.
Then you sit on the edge of the berth.
[pause 2s]
The sheets are cool and a little stiff, like hotel sheets. They smell of clean laundry. The blanket is soft under your hand, thick and slightly fuzzy, the kind of blanket that has kept a great many travellers warm on a great many winter nights.
Above the pillow, there's a small reading lamp with a frosted glass shade. You switch it on. A round, warm light settles on the pillow. Then you switch off the ceiling light.
[pause 2s]
Now the cabin is lit only by that little lamp. It's like a nest. A small world of your own, warm and golden, with the big cold world on the other side of the glass.
You lay your hand flat against the window. The glass is cold and smooth, and through it you can feel the calm chill of the winter night. Then you tuck your hand back under the blanket, where it warms up again straight away.
[pause 3s]

Through the window, you can see the platform. The orange lamps. A porter going by with a luggage trolley. A clock showing ten past nine.
In the corridor, a few doors are still sliding. Voices wishing each other goodnight. Then, little by little, the quiet returns.
A whistle, somewhere in the distance.
The train doors close, all together, with a sigh of air.
[pause 2s]
[soft, slow] And very gently, without a jolt, the train begins to move.
At first you can hardly feel it. The platform simply slides past the window, as if it were the one moving. The lamps go by, one after another, faster and faster. Then the end of the platform. Then the night.
The wheels begin their song.
Da-dum. Da-dum. Da-dum.
Slowly at first, rolling over the points with small, soft bumps. Then more and more steadily.
Da-dum. Da-dum.
[pause 5s]

[storytelling, calm] The train leaves the station and crosses the city.
Through the window, Paris passes by, all lit up. Buildings with glowing windows, where you glimpse kitchens, living rooms, people having dinner in front of the television. Wet streets, with red traffic lights reflected on the tarmac. A bridge, and under it the Seine, flowing black and slow, with golden reflections.
The train isn't going very fast yet. It takes its time, as if it wanted to say goodbye.
You stay sitting by the window, watching.
[pause 2s]
The buildings grow lower. Then come houses with little gardens. Warehouses. An empty car park lit by a single lamp. A suburban station, deserted, where the train passes without stopping, its name flashing by too quickly to read.
It's nearly Christmas. In some windows, fairy lights are blinking, red, green, gold. On a balcony, a small decorated tree. In a garden, a light up reindeer, slightly crooked, glowing all alone in the cold.
Then the lights grow fewer. Further apart. One here. Another over there, further off.
And at last, the city is behind you.
[pause 2s]
Da-dum. Da-dum. Da-dum.
The train has reached its cruising speed. It speeds through the night now, steady, sure of its way. You feel a faint, very gentle vibration rising through the berth. And now and then, a slight sway from side to side as the track curves.
[pause 3s]

There's a soft knock at the door. Two small taps.
It's the carriage attendant, a grey haired man in a blue jacket, with a tired smile. He's holding a tray.
Good evening, he says quietly. A herbal tea for the night? Lemon verbena, or lime blossom?
You choose the verbena. He sets a steaming cup on your little table, with a paper napkin. He asks what time you'd like your breakfast. You tell him, not too early. He nods, writes something in his notebook, and wishes you goodnight.
The door closes with its soft, muffled click.
[pause 2s]
You take the cup in both hands. It's hot. The steam smells of lemon and dried grass. You drink in small sips, watching the night go by. Each sip goes down warm and settles in your belly.
On the little table, the cup leaves a small ring of mist. The verbena bag still floats at the bottom, like a tiny boat resting on a golden pond.
[pause 3s]

It's time to get ready for the night.
You stand up and run a little water into the small basin. It's lukewarm. You wipe your face with the flannel. You brush your teeth, holding the edge with one hand because of the gentle sway.
You put on your pyjamas. Soft cotton pyjamas, a little worn, the ones you always take when you travel.
You draw the little curtain across the window, only halfway. You still want to be able to look out from your pillow.
Then you slip under the sheets.
[pause 2s]
They're cool at first. You shiver a little. Then the wool blanket does its work, and very soon warmth gathers around you.
You lie on your side, facing the window. The pillow is thick and soft. You pull the blanket up to your chin.
The little lamp still lights one corner of the pillow. You leave it on, for now.
Da-dum. Da-dum.
[pause 3s]
[soft] You listen to the small sounds of the carriage. The faint creak of the wooden panels as the train takes a bend. A curtain ring tinkling against its rail. A door sliding at the far end of the corridor, then closing. Someone coughing softly in the next compartment, then nothing.
These are the sounds of a house on wheels. A house full of sleepers, crossing the night without any fuss.
[pause 3s]

[storytelling, unhurried] Outside, it's the countryside. A winter countryside, deep in the night.
You can make out huge, flat fields covered in frost. A row of bare trees, black against a sky that's slightly less black. A lone farmhouse with a single lit window. Then nothing. Then another farmhouse.
The sky is clear. A nearly full moon has risen, and it's following the train. Every time you look, it's there, in the same place above the fields. As if it were travelling with you.
Under the moon, the frost glimmers faintly on the fields, with a bluish sheen.
[pause 2s]
The train runs alongside a canal. The water is frozen at the edges, and the ice shines like glass. A barge is moored there, perfectly still, a thread of smoke rising from its little chimney. Someone is asleep inside, warm, rocked by nothing at all.
Further on, a towpath stretches along the water under the bare trees. A bicycle leans against a gate, covered in frost, left there until spring. The train passes, and the canal stays behind, smooth and quiet.
Da-dum. Da-dum. Da-dum.
[pause 3s]
The train passes through a sleeping village. You see, very quickly, a church tower, a square with a fountain, a street of closed shutters. A single street lamp on a corner. No one outside. Everyone is asleep.
Except, perhaps, the baker. At the end of a lane, a small square window is lit, misted with steam. Behind it you can make out an oven, trays, hands covered in flour. Tomorrow morning, the village will wake to the smell of warm bread. But for now, it sleeps.
Then the fields return. The trees. The frost. The moon.
[pause 3s]

A little later, the train crosses a long stone viaduct. Below it lies a lake. A great still lake, black as ink, with the whole moon reflected in it, round and perfect.
The train passes over the lake slowly, arch after arch. You see its reflection gliding across the water, a line of small golden windows moving through the dark, like a necklace of lights.
Nothing moves on the water. Not a ripple, not a bird. Only the moon, and the little golden windows of the train, sliding across it.
On the shore, a few rowing boats are pulled up on the pebbles. A wooden jetty reaches out into the water. Everything is frozen in the cold, and in the silence.
Then the viaduct ends, and the trees come back.
[pause 3s]

Every so often, the train slows down.
It pulls into a small station.
The platform is deserted. There's an empty bench, a round clock showing a little after eleven, a small lamp above the door of the station building. The station's name is written on a blue sign in white letters. A name you don't know. A name you'll forget straight away.
The train stops, with a long sigh of brakes.
[pause 2s]
And for a while, everything is still. The silence is complete. All you can hear, somewhere in the carriage, is the soft hum of the ventilation.
On the platform, a railwayman walks slowly by with a lantern. He looks along the train, from end to end. Then he raises his hand.
And the train sets off again. Gently. Without a jolt.
The little station slides away behind, with its empty bench and its clock. It grows smaller. It disappears.
Da-dum. Da-dum.
[pause 3s]

[soft, slow] You switch off the little lamp.
Now the cabin is dark. Only moonlight comes in through the half open curtain, drawing a pale rectangle on the blanket.
Your eyes get used to the dark. You can see the landscape even better. The fields. The woods. A river that gleams for a moment in the moonlight, then hides behind poplars.
On the blanket, the rectangle of moonlight shifts slowly as the track curves this way and that. It slides over your knees, then over your hand, then back again. You watch it without moving, the way you might watch a cat wander across a bed.
Da-dum. Da-dum. Da-dum.
[pause 3s]
Another small station. The train slows, stops. An empty platform, an empty bench, a lamp. A name on a blue sign.
The silence. The stillness.
Then the train moves off again, gently.
Da-dum. Da-dum.
And another one. A platform. A lamp. A name. The silence.
Then the rolling starts again.
[pause 3s]
At one point, during a stop, another train passes on the next track. A goods train, very long, very slow. Its wagons pass your window one after another. A grey tanker. A dark red wooden wagon. A wagon loaded with tree trunks. Another. And another.
They go by with a low, steady, almost sleepy sound. You watch them, without counting. There are so many. Then the last wagon passes, with a little red light blinking at the back. And the next track is empty and dark again.
You've stopped counting the stations. They all look alike. Each one is a small pause in the night, a moment of calm, before the train goes on its way.
[pause 3s]

[storytelling, slower] Little by little, the landscape changes.
The ground begins to rise. The flat fields give way to hills, gentle at first, then steeper. The train slows a little on the climbs. You feel the rise in your body, a slight tilt of the berth.
There are forests now. Great forests of fir trees, dark and dense, lining the track.
And then, on the branches of the firs, you notice something white.
Snow.
[pause 2s]
Only a little at first, in patches along the track. Then more and more. The banks are white. The roofs of the houses are white. The fir trees wear heavy coats of snow on their branches.
The train is entering the mountains.
The air in the cabin seems to change too. Quieter. Softer. As if the snow outside were muffling every sound in the world, and leaving only the gentle rolling of the wheels.
Da-dum. Da-dum. Da-dum.
[pause 3s]
The landscape under the moon has a silent beauty.
Everything is white and blue. The snowy slopes glow softly in the moonlight. The shadows of the firs are dark blue on the snow. In the distance, the peaks stand out against the sky, immense, motionless, covered in glittering ice.
The train follows a narrow valley. Below, a mountain stream runs between snow covered rocks. You can't hear it through the glass. But you can see the water shining, black and silver, between the white stones.
On the other side of the valley, a small mountain village. Wooden chalets with roofs heavy with snow. A church with a pointed spire. Not a single light. The whole village is asleep, buried in snow, like an animal hibernating.
Da-dum. Da-dum.
[pause 3s]
At the edge of a forest, under the moon, a roe deer lifts its head. It watches the train go by without moving, its big ears turned toward the sound. Then it goes back to calmly nibbling the grass poking through the snow.
A little further on, a lone chalet with a single lit window. Someone up there is still awake. Perhaps to put another log on the stove. Perhaps for a glass of water. Then that window goes dark too.
Da-dum. Da-dum.
[pause 3s]

The train enters a tunnel.
Everything goes black. The sound of the wheels changes, deeper, more enveloping. A soft, steady rumble resonating all around the carriage. It's like being inside the mountain.
You close your eyes for a moment. You listen. The rumble wraps around you like a thick wool scarf, and for a moment there's nothing else in the world but that low, kind sound.
Then the train comes out of the tunnel. And the snow and the moon return all at once, dazzling in their softness.
Da-dum. Da-dum. Da-dum.
[pause 3s]
The train climbs slowly, in long curves, toward the pass. It crosses a stone bridge over a ravine. Far below, you can see the valley you've just left, with the thin dark line of the stream.
It begins to snow.
A few flakes at first drift past the window. They dance in the light spilling from the carriages. Then they grow thicker, closer together. They fall softly, silently, on the firs, on the rocks, on the roof of the train.
[pause 2s]
[soft, slower] You watch the snowflakes from the warmth of your berth.
Some of them land on the glass. You can see their shape for an instant before they melt. Tiny stars of ice, each one different, with six branches as fine as lace. Then each becomes a drop of water, sliding slowly down the pane.
Outside, it's very cold. But in here, under the wool blanket, it's warm. Your feet are warm. Your hands are warm. Your body is heavy and relaxed, rocked by the motion of the train.
Da-dum. Da-dum.
[pause 3s]

At the top of the pass, the train stops at a station a little larger than the others. It's the border station. The platform is covered in fresh snow, and flakes are falling through the light of the lamps.
Here, the drivers change. Far off, you hear calm voices, doors opening and closing. A woman's voice announces something very softly, in Italian, then in French. You don't try to understand. The words are soft and round, like a song.
Then everything falls quiet again. And the train sets off, and begins to descend, slowly, down the other side of the mountain.
Da-dum. Da-dum.
[pause 3s]
[slower, softer] On this side, the valleys are narrower and deeper. Villages cling to the slopes, with houses of grey stone and roofs of heavy slate slabs covered in snow. There are square bell towers, little humpback bridges over the streams, low walls tracing terraces under the snow.
Everything is asleep here too. A single lamp glows over a square, above a frozen fountain. Around it, the snow lies smooth and untouched. Not a single footprint. Nobody has crossed the square since the snow began to fall.
[pause 2s]
The train winds gently down, bend after bend. You feel your body slide, just slightly, toward the foot of the berth, and then back.
In the corridor, every light is out now, except the little blue night light down by the floor. The whole carriage seems to breathe in the same rhythm. The wooden panels barely creak, like an old ship letting itself be carried by the swell.
Da-dum. Da-dum.
[pause 4s]

[slow, soothing] The rolling of the wheels has become a lullaby.
Always the same rhythm. Always the same song. Da-dum. Da-dum. Like a heart beating slowly. Like a hand gently patting a child's back to send them to sleep.
The berth sways, barely. From right to left. From left to right. Like a cradle.
[pause 3s]
Without deciding to, your breathing has fallen in with the train. One in breath over a few beats of the wheels. One out breath, a little longer, over the next few. You're not counting. It just happened, the way you fall into step with someone walking beside you.
Da-dum. Da-dum.
[pause 4s]
You don't need to look outside anymore. You know the snow is falling. You know the mountain is there, vast and calm. You know the train knows the way.
There's nothing for you to steer, nothing to check, nothing to remember. The driver, right at the front, is keeping watch for you. The attendant, at the end of the corridor, is keeping watch for you. Everyone on this train can sleep.
Da-dum. Da-dum.
