[calm, clear] Before we begin, you might notice something. As soon as the body comes up, thoughts often arrive all by themselves. Too much of this. Not enough of that. I should. If only.
You don't have to silence them. You don't have to believe them either. You can just recognize them, like people passing in the street. Oh, there's the judgment about my stomach. Oh, there's the comparison.
Then, without arguing with them, come back to sensation. Not to the image of your body, but to what it feels, from the inside.
Each time a judgment pulls you back to the mirror, that's okay. Just return to what you can feel. Warmth, weight, contact.

[soft, unhurried] Let's start with your feet. Feel their contact with the floor, the bed, or the fabric of your socks. Maybe warmth, coolness, a slight tingling.
Wriggle your toes a little, if you like, just to feel them more clearly.
[pause 2s]
These feet have carried you for years. They took your first steps. They took you to school, to work, to appointments that mattered. They've walked on sand, on wet pavements, perhaps down hospital corridors.
They did all that without asking for thanks. So, silently, simply tell them... thank you for carrying me.
[pause 4s]
Move gently up to your legs, your knees, your hips. All this architecture that lets you get up in the morning, climb the stairs, run for a bus.
If part of your legs hurts, or doesn't work the way it used to, you can still greet it. Thank you for everything you've done, and for what you still do.
You can rest a hand on your thigh as you say it, as a small gesture of respect.
[pause 3s]
[soft] Now move to your hands. Feel them, resting. The palms, the fingertips, the small sensations living there.
Think of everything they do each day. They type on a keyboard, open doors, chop vegetables, hold a phone, write your name. They stroke a cat, hold a child's hand, comfort someone who's crying.
And your arms. They've carried shopping bags, moving boxes, maybe a sleeping baby. They've let you hold people close.
[warm] Thank you, hands. Thank you, arms. For everything you do that I so rarely notice.
If your hands are stiff or sore today, you can thank them anyway, and let them rest completely.
[pause 2s]
Can you feel a little warmth in your palms? Or simply their weight?
[pause 2.5s]
This is how gratitude for the body begins. By actually feeling it, rather than looking at it from the outside.
[pause 2s]

[soft, intimate] Now bring your attention to the center of your chest. Somewhere underneath, your heart is beating. You may not feel it directly, and that's okay. You can rest a hand there if you like, to feel a little closer.
This heart has been beating since before you were born. Around a hundred thousand times a day. It has never stopped to rest. It beat through your calmest nights and your hardest days. It's beating right now, for you, asking nothing in return.
And your breath. These lungs filling and emptying, thousands of times a day. Even when you're angry with your body, it keeps on breathing for you.

[warm] Take a moment to recognize this loyalty. Thank you, heart. Thank you, breath.

[gentle] Now, if you feel able, choose an area of your body you have a more difficult relationship with. Somewhere you judge, or hide, or that hurts.
If that feels too charged today, you can stay with your heart or your hands. You have nothing to prove.
[pause 2s]
If you do choose an area, simply rest your attention on it, delicately. The way you'd approach a slightly timid animal. Not too fast.
Notice the emotions that come. Embarrassment, sadness, sometimes anger. They often have a long history. Let them be here, without judging yourself for feeling them.
[pause 3s]
[soft, warm] Then, if you can, send this area a little kindness. You can rest a hand on it. Or imagine a warmth wrapping around it, like a soft, warm blanket.
Breathe gently toward it. As if each breath in brought a little more room, and each breath out let a little tension go.

And tell it, in your own way, I'm sorry I've been so hard on you. Or, you're part of me. Or simply, I'm here.

[reassuring] If strong emotions have come up, that's completely normal. The body holds a lot. You can stop at any moment, open your eyes, come back to your breathing. And if your relationship with your body has been painful for a long time, talking to a professional can really help.

[calm, unhurried] Now take a moment to feel your body as a whole. From your feet to your head. Not a perfect body. A living body, breathing, feeling, keeping you company.
This body has changed, and it will keep changing. It has been through years, scars, transformations. And it has never stopped being yours.
[pause 3s]
Notice whether there's a little more gentleness than at the start. Or perhaps not. Both are okay.
You won't keep this softer way of seeing all the time. Old judgments will come back. But each time you thank a part of yourself, even briefly, you're training a different habit.
