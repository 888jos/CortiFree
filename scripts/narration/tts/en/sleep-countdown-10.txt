# sleep-countdown-10 · Count down to sleep (EN)
[pause 1.5s]
[soft, calm, unhurried] Tonight, we're going to count. Not sheep. Breaths.
From fifty down to one, one number on each out-breath.
It's a very simple task, almost boring. And that's exactly what a mind needs when it still wants to work.
We give it a small job. Enough to keep it busy. Not enough to wake it up.
A bit like handing a rattle to a child who isn't ready to sleep yet. They hold it, they look at it, and without noticing, their eyes begin to close.
[pause 2s]

[soft] Before we start, settle in for the night.
Lie down in the position you usually fall asleep in. Pull the duvet up to wherever you like it. Find the hollow in the pillow, the one that already knows the shape of your head.
Let your arms rest wherever they're comfortable. If anything bothers you, a crease in the sheet, a strand of hair, a leg at an awkward angle, take a moment to sort it out now.
After that, you won't need to move. The bed will take care of the rest.
[pause 4s]

[gentle, reassuring] There's one rule, just one. If you lose count, it doesn't matter at all.
If you can't remember whether you were on thirty-two or twenty-eight, pick whichever number you like and carry on from there.
If your breath doesn't line up with my voice, that doesn't matter either. Your breath is right. Follow it, and let my voice trail along behind, like a quiet sound in the background.
And if you fall asleep before the end, that's perfect. Few people make it all the way down.
It isn't a contest. Nobody's checking. There's no right way to count, only yours.
[pause 2s]

[soft, slow] To begin, three breaths, a little slower than usual.
Breathe in gently through your nose, feeling the cool air coming in...
[pause 3s]
and let the air flow out for a long time, warm now, through your nose or your mouth.
[pause 6s]
Again. Breathe in, feeling your belly rise under the duvet...
[pause 3s]
and breathe out, feeling it fall, very softly, like a balloon slowly emptying.
[pause 6s]
One last time. Breathe in...
[pause 3s]
and breathe out, letting the full weight of your body drop into the mattress.
[pause 6s]
[soft, soothing] Now let your breath return to its natural rhythm. Stop steering it. It knows what to do. It does this every night without you.
On each out-breath, I'll say a number. You can repeat it in your head with me, like an echo.
And between the numbers, I'll invite you to let go of one small part of your body.
Here we go.

[pause 3s]

[pause 3s]

[pause 3s]
Forty-five. Your chest softens. It doesn't need to hold itself up anymore. It barely rises at all.
[pause 3s]
Forty-four. Your belly is soft. It rises and falls by itself, like a slow wave rolling up the sand.
[pause 3s]

[pause 3s]
Forty-two. Your hips sink a little deeper. Your thighs grow heavy, warm and still.
[pause 3s]
Forty-one. Your calves, your ankles, your feet let go, down at the bottom of the bed. Even your toes are resting now.
[pause 3s]
Forty. Your whole body is resting. From head to toe, there's nothing left to carry.

[softer, slower] Now we'll leave a little more space.

No need to be precise. If you skip a number, or count the same one twice, let it be. The numbers are there to carry you, not to keep an eye on you.

Thirty-three. Your body is as heavy as a sack of sand set down on the floor. Once it's down, it doesn't move. It simply takes the shape of whatever is beneath it.

Twenty-five.
