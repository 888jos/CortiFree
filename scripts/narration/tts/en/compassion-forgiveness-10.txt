# compassion-forgiveness-10 · Letting go of a grudge (EN)

[warm, calm] A grudge is a bit like a stone in your shoe. At first, you feel it with every step. Then you get used to it, and you limp a little without noticing. You forget that you could take it out.
Over the next ten minutes, you'll look at a grudge you've been carrying, and see whether you can lighten the load a little. For you. Not for the other person.
[pause 2s]
[clear, reassuring] One important note before we begin. Choose a light or moderate grudge. A hurtful remark from a colleague, a friend who didn't keep their promise, a family member who broke your trust. Not a situation involving violence, and not a trauma. Wounds like those deserve to be explored gently, with the support of a professional.
And nothing obliges you to forgive. Not today, not ever. Here, you go at your own pace, and you can stop at any time.

[soft, unhurried] Settle into a steady position. Feet on the floor, back supported, hands resting on your thighs or your belly.
If you like, close your eyes. Otherwise, keep a soft gaze, resting downward.
Breathe in through your nose, deeply but without forcing...

Now let your breath find its own rhythm. Rest your attention on your belly, rising slightly as you breathe in, and softening as you breathe out.

[calm, grounded] Feel the weight of your body. Your hips on the seat. Your feet on the floor. You're here, safe, in the present moment. What happened belongs to the past. All you're going to do now is look at it.
If at any point what comes up feels too strong, come back to this anchor. Your feet, your belly breathing. It will always be there.
Let's try it right now. Press your feet gently into the floor... then let go. That's your safe place for the whole session.
[pause 3s]
[calm, intimate] Now let the situation come to mind. The person, the moment, the words or actions that hurt you.
You don't need to relive it all in detail. One image is enough. A fragment of the scene, like a blurry photo.
And notice what happens in your body as you think about it. A grudge often has a physical signature. A clenched jaw. Heat in the chest. Fists that want to close. A knot in the stomach, pulling tighter.
[pause 2s]
You can name what you feel. Anger. Sadness. Disappointment. Humiliation, perhaps. Every emotion has the right to be here.
Let these emotions exist without feeding them. You don't need to replay the scene to feel them. If it gets intense, take one breath down into your belly, then look again.
[pause 3s]
[warm, gentle] Now acknowledge the hurt. Not to dwell on it, but to do it justice.
Say to yourself silently... what happened hurt me. I had every right to be hurt. My reaction makes sense.
[pause 2s]
Often, we hold on to a grudge because it feels as if no one has ever acknowledged what we went through. So start there. You acknowledge it.
Perhaps what hurt was a need that wasn't respected. The need for respect, for being listened to, for being taken into account. That need was legitimate. It still is.
Place a hand on your heart if it helps, and let that acknowledgment reach you.
[pause 3s]
[calm, clear] Now look honestly at what this grudge is costing you. You.
How many times have you gone back over that scene? In the shower, on the train, as you're falling asleep? How many imaginary conversations have you had with this person, searching for the perfect comeback?
[pause 2s]
And in your body, every time you think about it, the tension comes back. Your heart speeds up, your muscles tighten. As if the event were happening again, over and over.
Notice, too, whether this grudge spills over into other parts of your life. A greater wariness of other people. An irritability that flares up without warning. A subject you avoid, a place you no longer go.
Meanwhile, the other person probably feels none of this. You're the one carrying the weight. Like holding a bag of stones at arm's length, hoping it will feel heavy to someone else.
[pause 2s]
[gentle] Just notice that, without blaming yourself. Holding a grudge is a very human way of protecting yourself. It may have served you, for a while.
You can thank that protection, silently. Thank you for looking out for me. And gently ask yourself... do I still need to hold it this tightly?
[pause 3s]
[warm, clear] So what is forgiveness, really? Not what we often think.
Forgiving isn't excusing. What happened wasn't acceptable, and it still isn't.
Forgiving isn't forgetting. You can keep the memory, and the lesson that comes with it.
Forgiving doesn't necessarily mean reconciling. You can forgive someone and decide not to see them again. You can forgive and keep your boundaries, firmly.
Forgiving doesn't mean forcing yourself to feel affection, either. You can stay neutral toward this person. You can even go on not liking them.
[pause 1.5s]
Forgiving means putting the weight down. Deciding that this story won't take up so much room inside you anymore. Taking back the energy it's been using up.
It's a gift you give yourself, far more than one you give them.
Sometimes it helps to remember that the other person was also acting with their own limits, their fears, their tiredness. That doesn't excuse them. It simply makes them human, like you.
[soft] Take a breath with that idea. Not to change your mind about what happened. Just to see whether the fist inside you can loosen, even a notch.
[pause 4s]
[calm, gentle] Now see whether you can take one tiny step in that direction. Not a grand gesture. Just an opening.
You can choose a phrase, if one of them speaks to you. I choose to put this weight down, a little. Or, I don't condone what you did, but I no longer want to carry it. Or, I'm freeing myself from this story, at my own pace.
If no phrase feels right, simply keep the intention. The wish, however timid, to feel freer one day.
And if you sense it isn't the right time, that the anger still needs to exist, that's completely fine too. You can say, not yet. And that in itself is a form of honesty with yourself.
[pause 2s]
[soft, unhurried] Imagine setting the bag of stones down on the ground, beside you. It's still there, and you can pick it up again if you want. But for now, your arms can rest.
Feel your arms, your shoulders, your hands, as if they really had just let go of something heavy. Let them soften, in rhythm with your breath.
[pause 5s]
[calm, intimate] Now, one last step, a more delicate one. In some situations, we also carry part of the responsibility. A reaction that was too sharp, a silence, a boundary we didn't manage to set.
If that's the case for you, if part of this story belongs to you, look at it with the same gentleness.
You did what you could, with what you knew, with what you were feeling at the time. You can tell yourself... I forgive myself for my part, too. I'm learning.
And if no part of it is yours, which happens too, you can simply thank yourself for taking the time to look at all this.
[gentle, reassuring] If strong emotions come up, tears, sharp anger, let them pass through like a wave. That's normal. Breathe, come back to your feet, open your eyes if you need to.
[pause 3s]
[soft] Now stay for a few moments without doing anything. Notice your body. Your jaw, your chest, your belly. Is there a little more space than at the beginning?
[pause 3s]
If there's a little space, breathe into it. A breath in that goes a little further. A breath out that sets down a little more.
[pause 4s]
[warm] And if nothing has changed, that's normal too. Letting go of a grudge is a process, not a switch. It happens in layers, like peeling an onion. You may need to come back to it several times. Each time, the weight gets a little lighter.
The anger may come back tomorrow. That isn't going backwards. It's just another layer showing itself.
On that day, you can simply come back to your feet, to your breath, and repeat the phrase that spoke to you today. No need to start from scratch.

[calm] Gently bring your attention back to the room. The sounds, the temperature of the air, the feel of your clothes against your skin.
Move your fingers and feet a little. Roll your shoulders, once or twice.
And when the moment is right for you, open your eyes.
