# focus-decision-8 · Making a decision (EN)
[pause 1.5s]
[warm, calm] You have a choice to make, and maybe you've been going around in circles for a while. Pros, cons, back to the pros. The more you think about it, the less clear it gets.
That's normal. When a decision weighs on us, the mind speeds up and hunts for the perfect answer. It compares, it anticipates, it imagines the worst. And often it ends up exhausted, without having decided anything.
[pause 2s]
[warm, clear] Today we'll do it differently. First, slow down. Then listen to three sources of information. Your head, your body, and what matters to you.
One important note. You won't need to decide by the end of this session. The goal is to see more clearly. The decision will come when it comes.
[pause 2s]
[soft, unhurried] Settle into a position where you feel steady. Back supported, feet on the floor, hands resting.
Close your eyes, or gently lower your gaze.
For a few minutes, the decision can wait. There's nothing you need to solve right now.
[pause 2s]
[calm] Let's start by slowing your breath. You'll breathe in through your nose for about four seconds, without forcing, then breathe out slowly for about six.
Breathe in...
[pause 3.5s]
[slow] and out, slowly.
[pause 5.5s]
Again. In...
[pause 3.5s]
and out, as if you were slowly letting the air out of a balloon.
[pause 5.5s]
[gentle] Once more, at your own pace. If it feels too long, shorten it. And if you feel any discomfort, simply return to your natural breathing.
[pause 6s]
[soft] On the out-breath, let your belly soften, and your jaw too. A slow breath tells your body there's no immediate danger.
Now let your breathing go back to normal. Has something already slowed down a little? Your heart, your thoughts, that sense of urgency?
[pause 3s]
[calm] Urgency makes it feel as if you have to decide right now, this minute. But most decisions can wait a few calm minutes. You're giving yourself that time.
[pause 2s]
[clear] Now name your decision in one sentence. Simple and clear. For example, should I take this new job? Or, should I move, or should I stay?
If there are more than two options, pick the two main ones for this exercise. We'll call them option A and option B.
Say your sentence silently. Then give each option a short name. A word or two.
[pause 3s]
Start with your head, briefly. Let the main arguments run past, one or two for each option, without debating them. You already know them by heart.
Then set them down beside you, like files on a table. They're still available, but they're not the only information you have.
[pause 2s]
[calm, unhurried] Let's begin with option A. Imagine you've chosen it. It's done. The decision is made, and you're living with it.
Picture yourself a few weeks from now. An ordinary morning. Where are you? What do you do when you get up? Who are you spending your day with?
Make the scene concrete. The places, the faces, the rhythm of your days. As if you were really there.
[pause 3s]
[soft, intimate] Now bring your attention to your body. Don't analyze, just observe. What happens when you step into this choice?
Maybe an opening in your chest, a sense of space, of lightness. Or maybe a knot in your stomach, a tight throat, shoulders tensing up.
Maybe a mix of both. Excitement tinged with fear, for example. That's common when a choice helps us grow.
Simply notice. And if you like, name one or two words to yourself. Light, tight, warm, heavy.
If nothing clear shows up, stay in the scene for another breath or two. Sensations are sometimes quiet, like a whisper.
[pause 3s]
Your body doesn't give you the answer on its own. But it offers information your head doesn't always see.
[calm] Now let that image dissolve. Come back to your breath for a few breaths, like washing your hands between two experiences.
Breathe in...
[pause 3s]
and as you breathe out, let option A drift away. You're not losing it, just setting it aside.
[pause 4s]
[calm, clear] Now on to option B. This time, imagine this is the one you chose. Not the other. This one.
Picture yourself in the same way, a few weeks from now. An ordinary morning. Where are you? What are you doing? Who's around you?
Take the time to step into this life, with as much detail as the first. If a thought keeps nagging, yes, but, note it without following it. It will be useful later.
[pause 3s]
[soft] And again, listen to your body. What changes? Is your breathing fuller, or shorter? Is there tension, release, relief?
Gently compare it with what you felt for option A. Without jumping to conclusions. Just notice the difference, if there is one.
And if both feel the same, or blurry, that's information too. Maybe the two options are equally good, or maybe you need to know more.
[pause 2s]
Come back to your breath for a few moments.
If your mind has drifted back into the arguments, that's normal. It loves doing that. Simply come back to the feeling of the air coming in and going out.
[pause 4s]
[warm, calm] Now, another question, a deeper one. Set aside the pros and cons, and what other people might think.
Ask yourself, what really matters to me, in my life, right now? Not a goal, but a direction. Freedom, maybe. Security. Being close to the people I love. Learning. Creating. Being useful. Being honest with myself.
Let two or three words come. The ones that truly resonate, not the ones that would look good on paper.
[pause 3s]
Now look at your two options in the light of these values. Which one brings you closer to what matters to you? Which one looks more like the person you want to be?
It isn't necessarily the most comfortable option. Sometimes what matters takes courage. And sometimes, on the contrary, the simplest choice is the one that respects you most.
[pause 3s]
[calm, clear] One last exploration. If you feel you can't decide yet, ask yourself what's missing.
A concrete piece of information? A number, a date, an answer from someone?
A conversation with someone you trust?
Simply time, to let things settle?
Or maybe permission to get it wrong. No decision is ever completely safe, and most can be adjusted along the way. Sometimes what's missing is simply accepting that some uncertainty will remain, whatever you choose.
Notice what's missing, if anything. And perhaps one small action to get it.
[pause 3s]
[soft, warm] Now let all of this settle. You've listened to your head, your body, and your values. You've explored the question in a different way than going around in circles.
Maybe one option is becoming clearer. Maybe not yet. Both are fine.
You're under no obligation to decide now. Decisions often ripen in calm, once we stop forcing them. You can let this one find its way, on a walk, or overnight.
You can also set a time to decide. Tomorrow evening, or by the end of the week. It's often a relief to know when, even before you know what.
[pause 2s]
[calm] Take a deeper breath. Feel your body's points of support. Gently move your hands, your shoulders.
[soft, warm, slowing down] Open your eyes when it feels right for you. And if one option felt lighter or truer, write it down somewhere. It's a marker, not a verdict.
[pause 4s]
