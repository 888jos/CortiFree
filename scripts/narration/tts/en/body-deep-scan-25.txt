[warm, calm, unhurried] Hello, and welcome to this deep body scan. You've chosen to take twenty five minutes just for yourself. A Sunday, a day off, a quiet late afternoon... Whenever it is, this time belongs to you.
[pause 2s]
[calm, clear] To begin, find a place where you won't be disturbed. If you can, switch your phone to do not disturb, so nothing interrupts the session.
Lie down on your back, on a mat, a blanket, or your bed. Legs stretched out and slightly apart, feet falling naturally out to the sides. Arms by your sides, a little away from your body, palms facing up or down, whichever you prefer.
[pause 2s]
If this position isn't comfortable for you, you can bend your knees, slide a cushion under your legs, or do the whole practice sitting in an armchair. What matters is that your body feels well supported.
A blanket can help too. When we stay still, we often cool down a little.

[soft] Close your eyes, if that feels good. Or keep them half open, your gaze resting low, not fixed on anything.
[pause 2s]
[warm, clear] Before we go further, an intention. This scan isn't about reaching a particular state. Not perfect relaxation, not an empty mind. It's about introducing yourself to your body, just as it is right now, moment by moment, with curiosity and gentleness.
You'll probably notice sensations that are pleasant, unpleasant, or neutral. They all have their place. And you'll also notice your mind wandering, sometimes often. That's what minds do. Each time you realize it, you can simply come back, without judging yourself. That coming back is the practice itself.
[pause 2s]
This approach comes from mindfulness based stress reduction programs, developed more than forty years ago. There, the body scan is practiced slowly and regularly, as a way of learning to live in the body rather than rushing through it without noticing.
[reassuring] If you fall asleep, that isn't a failure either. Maybe your body needed the rest. But the invitation is to stay awake, attentive to what's here, for as long as you can.
And if any part of your body is painful or injured, you're always free to choose your distance. You can observe it from afar, very gently, or simply move on to the next area.
