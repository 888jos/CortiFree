# morning-slow-weekend-10 · Slow weekend morning (EN)
[pause 1.5s]
[warm, unhurried] It's the weekend. Or a day off, a morning with no alarm and no appointments.
Today, there's no rush. Nothing to do, nowhere to be. For these ten minutes, you have permission to accomplish absolutely nothing.
[pause 2s]
You might still be in bed, in an armchair, by a window, out on a balcony. Choose wherever your body wants to be. And settle in as if for a long while.
If someone is still asleep beside you, or the house is waking up around you, that's fine. You can listen quietly. This moment is still yours.
[pause 2s]
[soft] Start by noticing where you are. The surface holding you up. The temperature of the air. What you're wearing. Pajamas, a blanket, an old sweater.
There's no ideal posture this morning. If you want to pull your knees up, lean back against a cushion, let your head tilt to one side, go ahead.
Let your body find its most comfortable position, the one you'd choose if nobody were watching.
[pause 3s]

[warm, clear] Now, a curious observation. Even though nothing is urgent, part of you may still be running.
Listen from the inside. Have your thoughts already started planning? Groceries, laundry, messages to send, all the things you should do this weekend to make the most of it?
[pause 2s]
Is your body a little ahead of itself? A slight tightness in your chest, a clenched jaw, an itch to get up and do something?
[pause 2s]
That's very common. After a busy week, the body holds on to its momentum, like a train that keeps rolling after the engine's been switched off. Alert mode doesn't shut down all at once, just because it's Saturday.
You don't have to chase it away. Just notice it. Oh, look. I'm still running.
[gentle] You might even notice, with a bit of humor, all the little voices saying you should get up, tidy up, get things done. They're used to running the week. This morning, they can sit next to you, without taking the wheel.
There's nothing wrong with you for still feeling the pull. It's just the rhythm of the week, taking a little while to wind down.
[pause 2s]

[soft, slower] So, let's slow down. Very gradually.
First, the breath. No technique here. Just let each out-breath last a tiny bit longer, like a lazy sigh.
Breathe in...
[pause 3s]
and let the air drift out without hurrying, all the way to the end.
[pause 6s]
Again. The in-breath comes on its own...
[pause 3s]
and the out-breath takes its time.
[pause 5s]
And at the end of the out-breath, there's sometimes a small moment of stillness, before the next in-breath arrives. You don't need to make it happen. Just notice it, if it's there.
Place a hand on your belly if you like, and feel it rise and fall, slowly. Like a wave stretching up the sand, then drawing back without any hurry.
[pause 5s]

[calm, unhurried] Now, your movements. If you move, move at half your usual speed.
If you're holding a mug, bring it to your lips as if in slow motion. If you change position, do it as if you were underwater.
Try it with your hand now. Lift it very slowly, just a few inches, and set it back down.
[pause 4s]
Notice everything you felt during that simple movement. The lightness, the weight, the air on your skin.
At weekends, we often fill our free time just as quickly as everything else. Slowing your movements is a way of telling your body, today, we have time. Really.
This slowness might feel strange at first. Maybe even a bit uncomfortable. That's a sign you're stepping out of autopilot.
If the urge to hurry comes back, notice it, and slow down just a little more. Not out of discipline. Out of curiosity.
[pause 3s]

[soft, warm] And now, let yourself savor what's here. The sensations of the morning, one at a time.
The light, first. If your eyes are open, look at it. How does it come into the room? Through the window, between the curtains, as a beam or a soft veil?
[pause 2s]
Notice its color. In the morning, it's often softer, more golden, or a pale, peaceful gray. Watch how it rests on things, the wall, the floor. The shadows it draws.
Maybe a speck of dust is dancing in a sunbeam. Maybe a reflection is trembling on the ceiling. Tiny shows that play out every morning, often with no one watching.
If your eyes are closed, feel the light through your eyelids. A tint, a brightness.
[pause 4s]
Then the warmth. Where is your body nice and warm? Under the blanket, around a mug, where the sun touches your skin?
[pause 2s]
Let yourself enjoy that warmth, simply. Like a cat settling into a patch of sunlight. It has nothing else to do. Neither do you.
Notice the contrast, too. One part warm, another cooler. Your nose a little chilly, your feet tucked under the covers. Those small differences make the warmth even more pleasant.
[pause 3s]
And the sounds. Listen to the morning. At weekends, it often sounds different.
Maybe fewer cars. Birds more noticeable. Neighbors laughing, a child playing, a market setting up, church bells in the distance. The coffee machine gurgling, the cat jumping off the sofa.
You don't need to name them. Let them come to you, and go again. The near ones, the far ones, and the silence in between.
Choose one sound, and follow it until it fades. Listen right to the end, the moment it melts into everything else.
[pause 5s]
Sounds make a lovely anchor on a slow morning. They arrive without any effort, and they're always new.
[pause 2s]
And maybe a smell. Coffee, toast, fresh air drifting in through a half-open window. Or just the familiar smell of home.
Breathe in gently, without searching. Let the smell come to you, rather than going looking for it.
[pause 4s]

[softer, quieter] Now I'll guide a little less. There's nothing in particular to notice anymore.
Let your attention open up, like a window flung wide. Everything can come in. Sounds, sensations, light, passing thoughts.
Nothing to hold on to, nothing to push away. Just be here, in the middle of it all.
You could imagine sitting on a riverbank. Everything that passes, sounds, thoughts, sensations, drifts by on the water. You watch, and let it flow.
If you catch yourself planning, smile at the habit, and come back to the morning.
You can let your gaze rest somewhere, without fixing on anything. Or close your eyes. Your body is heavy, your breath finds its own way.
[pause 8s]
One breath... and you stay right here.
[pause 6s]
Notice that this moment doesn't need anything more. No goal, no progress to make. Just the morning, and you in it.
All is well. There's nowhere else you need to be.
If a thought carries you off, it's nothing. You notice, you come back. And the morning is still here, waiting for you.
Maybe the light has already shifted a little since we began. Maybe a new sound has appeared. The morning moves slowly around you, and all you have to do is let it.
[pause 8s]

[gentle, warm] Gently, bring a little attention back to yourself. And ask yourself a question, without rushing for an answer.
What would really do me good today?
[pause 3s]
Not what would be productive. Not what you should do. What would actually do you good.
Maybe a long walk. A bath. A book. Seeing a friend. Cooking without watching the clock. Or nothing at all, which is a perfectly good answer.
You can also ask what your body needs. To move, to rest, to eat something delicious, to be outside? And what does your heart need? Quiet, laughter, company?
Listen to what comes. Sometimes it's a specific wish, sometimes just a feeling. More air, more calm, more connection.
If nothing comes, that's fine. The question can keep working quietly in the background while you have breakfast or take a shower.
[pause 4s]
[warm] Hold that wish lightly. It's not another task to tick off. It's an invitation.
It doesn't matter if the day doesn't go exactly the way you picture it. The point isn't to get your weekend right. It's to stay a little closer to yourself.
And if, later on, you feel the pace picking up again for no reason, you can come back to what you did this morning. A lazy out-breath. A slow-motion gesture. One sound you really listen to.
[pause 2s]
[soft] Before you move, stay here a moment longer. Notice what it feels like to have taken this time. Even if nothing special happened.
Maybe that's exactly the point. That nothing happened.
[pause 3s]
You've just given your body something rare. Time without a goal. It often holds on to that, a kind of ease that lingers for a while.
[gentle] When you feel like it, and not before, start to move. Slowly. Stretch, if you're in the mood.
Arms overhead, a long yawn, your whole body lengthening.
[pause 3s]
[warm, grounded] And try to give yourself mornings like this more often. Even ten minutes, on a Tuesday, before the house wakes up.
For now, step into your day at the pace of this morning. There's no race to win today.
