# body-yoga-nidra-15 · Stress-relief yoga nidra (EN)
[pause 1.5s]
[warm, calm] Welcome to this yoga nidra. It's a pause for deep recovery, like a nap, except that part of you stays listening. In a quarter of an hour, you'll get up rested and ready to pick up your day again.
This format is sometimes called non-sleep deep rest. Your body settles almost as it would in sleep, while a small part of you stays awake and listens. It's the nap you can't always take, offered to yourself in another way.
[pause 2s]
[soft, unhurried] Lie down on your back, on a mat, a bed or a sofa. Legs stretched out and slightly apart, arms a little away from your body, palms facing up if that's comfortable.
If your lower back pulls, slide a cushion under your knees. If you tend to get cold, cover yourself with a blanket. The body cools down as it rests.
[pause 3s]
Make any last adjustments. Move your shoulders, your head, your pelvis a little, until you find a position you could stay in for a long time without moving.
Then close your eyes. From now on, the idea is to stay as still as you can. If you need to move, do it slowly, then come back to rest.
[pause 2s]
[calm, reassuring] The only instruction in yoga nidra is to keep listening to my voice. No need to concentrate hard. Let the words come to you. If you drift off for a moment, that's fine. You'll pick up the thread again further on.
[pause 2s]

[calm, unhurried] Become aware of the sounds around you. The most distant ones first. A car, voices, the wind, perhaps. Let your listening reach far, beyond the walls, without looking for anything in particular.
[pause 3s]
Then the closer sounds, in the room. A humming fridge, a ticking clock, the faint rustle of your clothes. And finally, the sound of your own breathing, very close.
[pause 3s]
Let them all be there, without holding on to any of them. They pass by, like clouds. No sound is a disturbance. Each one simply becomes part of the background.

[soft] Now feel your body lying down. The points of contact with the floor. The back of your head, your shoulder blades, your elbows, the backs of your hands, your pelvis, your calves, your heels.
Feel the weight of each of these points. The floor holds you completely. There's nothing to hold back, nothing to hold up.
[slow] Say to yourself, silently... I am lying down, I am resting, I am listening.
[pause 4s]
[warm, calm] Now comes the moment for your sankalpa. It's an intention. A short, positive phrase, said in the present tense, as if it were already true.
You may already have one. If not, here are a few for inspiration. I am calm and present. I am allowed to rest. I move through my days with more ease.
Choose one, or let your own appear. Don't look for the perfect phrase. Take the first one that feels right.

Let your sankalpa settle. We'll come back to it at the end.

[soft, unhurried] Feel your body as one single presence, from feet to head. Feel the space it takes up in the room, its shape, its outline, completely still.
[pause 4s]
Now become aware of your natural breathing. Don't change it. Watch it the way you'd watch waves from the shore, asking nothing of them.
Feel the air come in through your nostrils, a little cooler. And go out, a little warmer.
Feel your belly rising and falling, all on its own.
Each in-breath brings a little freshness. Each out-breath invites you to settle a little more deeply.
[pause 4s]
Now follow the path of the breath. It comes in through the nostrils, down through the throat, gently fills the chest, then the belly. And it travels back the other way. Belly, chest, throat, nostrils. A quiet round trip, again and again.

[calm, clear] Now we'll count the breaths backward, from eleven down to one. Silently, without changing the breath. I'll start with you, then you'll carry on alone.

[calm, unhurried] Now we'll explore opposite sensations. Let them arise from memory, without forcing. If they don't really come, simply imagine them.
Awaken the feeling of heaviness. Your body is becoming heavy. Legs heavy, as if filled with sand. Arms heavy, resting. Head heavy, sinking into the pillow. Your whole body weighs down, as it does after a long day of walking.
Eyelids heavy. Shoulders heavy. Your back sinking into the floor, as if into warm sand. Everything is heavy, and everything is held.
[pause 4s]
[soft] And now, lightness. Your body is becoming light, very light. Like a cork floating on water. Arms light, legs light. As if the floor were barely holding you anymore.
Hands so light they could float away. Chest light, like a feather carried by the breath. Your whole body drifting, barely touching down.
[pause 4s]
Heaviness... and lightness. Let the two sensations come in turn, without preferring one. Heavy as a stone... light as a soap bubble.
[pause 4s]
[warm] Now awaken the feeling of warmth. Remember your hands around a mug of hot chocolate in winter. Or the sun on your skin, late on a summer afternoon. Warmth spreading through your whole body.
It starts in your belly, glows through your chest, flows down your arms to your hands, slips down your legs to your feet. A soft, enveloping warmth.
[pause 4s]
[soft] And now, coolness. Your feet in a river in springtime. The crisp air of a February morning on your face as you step outside. Your whole body becoming cool.
A clean coolness on your cheeks, your forehead, the backs of your hands. Like slipping between cool sheets on a summer night.
[pause 4s]
Warmth... and coolness. Your body knows how to move from one to the other. Now rest in a temperature that feels just right.
[pause 4s]
[calm] One last pair, closer to your everyday life. Remember a hectic moment, without choosing the worst one. A packed train platform, a phone that won't stop ringing, a morning where everything piles up. Just notice, for a moment, how your body remembers it. Maybe a shoulder creeping up, a breath growing shorter.
[pause 3s]
[soft, soothing] And now, let that image drift away. Remember a moment of calm. A Sunday morning with no alarm, a bench in the sun, the quiet of a house where everyone is still asleep. Let that calm settle into your body, and fill all the space.
[pause 4s]
Your body knows how to do both. It knows how to rev up, and it knows how to come back to calm. For now, it's resting in the calm.

[calm, clear] This yoga nidra is coming to an end. We're going to come back gradually.
If you dozed off at times, or don't remember everything, that's perfectly fine. Your body still benefited from the rest, even when your attention wandered away.

Become aware of your breathing. Let it deepen a little, become more present.
