# anxiety-leaves-stream-8 · Leaves on a stream (EN)

[warm, calm] Welcome. Take a moment to really arrive, right where you are.
Settle into a position where your body can stay still for a few minutes. Sitting, with your back upright but not stiff, or lying down if you prefer.
Let your eyes close, or lower your gaze toward the floor, slightly unfocused.
[pause 2s]
[gentle] There's nothing to get right in these few minutes. Just be here, sitting, and watching.
Breathe normally. Simply notice the air coming in, and the air going out.
Maybe your breathing is fast, maybe slow. There's nothing to correct. Let it do whatever it wants.
[pause 2s]
Feel the places where your body is supported. The backs of your thighs, your back against the chair, your hands resting. Each out-breath settles you in a little more.
[pause 4s]

[clear, warm] Today, I'd like us to practice something simple, and precious. Looking at your thoughts, instead of looking through them.
Usually, when a thought like, I'm going to mess this up, shows up, we take it as reality. We're inside it, like wearing glasses we've forgotten we have on.
This exercise comes from an approach called acceptance and commitment therapy. It invites you to take those glasses off and set them on the table for a few moments, so you can look at them.
The goal isn't to chase thoughts away, or to make them more pleasant. It's to change your relationship with them. Watching them pass, instead of being swept along.
[pause 2s]

[storytelling, soft] Imagine you're sitting beside a small stream. Choose whatever place comes to you. A country brook, a mountain river, the edge of a canal in the city.
What's beneath you? Grass, a flat stone, a wooden bench?
[pause 2s]
Feel the temperature of the air on your skin. Maybe a little sunshine, maybe cool shade.
Listen to the water. It flows at its own pace, not too fast, not too slow. It makes a small, steady sound against the pebbles.
[pause 4s]
And on this water, leaves are floating by. Every so often, a leaf drifts in from the left, glides past you, and moves off to the right, until it disappears around a bend in the stream.
All kinds of leaves. Green, yellow, russet, large or tiny. Watch one or two float past, without doing anything.
[pause 5s]

[calm, clear] Now gently turn your attention to what's happening in your mind.
Each time a thought appears, take it and place it on a leaf. Then let the leaf carry it away.
If the thought is made of words, you can imagine those words written on the leaf. I need to call the mechanic back. Onto a leaf. If it's an image, place the image on the leaf, like a small photograph.
Every thought gets its own leaf. Worries, shopping lists, memories, brilliant ideas, judgments, questions.
You don't sort them. You don't keep the good ones or chase away the bad ones. Everything goes onto the water in the same way.
[pause 5s]
[gentle] Let each leaf go at its own pace. You don't need to push the current to make it go faster. And you don't need to hold a leaf back to look at it longer.
Some thoughts will come back. The same worry, five leaves later. That's perfectly fine. Place it down again, without irritation. The stream never gets tired.
If several thoughts arrive at once, put them all on one big leaf, or on several small ones. It's up to you.
And if there are no thoughts for a while, simply watch the water flow. The quiet stretches are part of the stream too.
[pause 4s]

[calm, reassuring] At some point, and it's almost certain, you'll realize you're no longer by the stream. A thought hooked you, you followed it, and now you're preparing tomorrow's meeting or replaying a conversation.
This is exactly the heart of the exercise. The moment you notice is a small moment of waking up.
Without scolding yourself, come back to the water's edge. Take the thought that pulled you away, and place it on a leaf too.
[pause 4s]
[gentle] There are also quiet thoughts, ones we don't even recognize as thoughts. I can't do this. This doesn't work for me. This is a bit silly.
They're thoughts like any others. They deserve their leaf. Watch them float away.
[pause 2s]
Sometimes a thought comes back with real force, sticky, insistent. What if I can't pay the bills this month? Notice how it tugs at you. You can even give it a bigger, heavier leaf. It still floats. It just takes a little longer to drift away.
Notice, too, the thoughts that comment on the exercise. Am I doing this right? My stream isn't realistic enough. One leaf for each.
And if an emotion shows up, impatience, boredom, a touch of sadness, you can name it, and place it on the water too.
The more you practice this, the more natural it becomes to tell yourself, oh, that's a thought. Not a truth, not an order. Just a thought.
[pause 3s]

[softer, unhurried] Now carry on at your own pace. I'll guide you less. One thought, one leaf. The water flowing.
A thought arrives. You notice it. You place it on a leaf. And you watch it drift away, at the pace of the water.
[pause 8s]
If you've wandered off, no problem at all. Come back to the bank, one leaf, and the current.
If you're having trouble seeing the leaves, that's fine. Some people imagine bubbles rising instead, or clouds crossing the sky. The image doesn't matter much. What matters is the gesture. Notice, place, let go.
[pause 5s]
[calm, grounded] Notice that while the leaves pass, you stay on the bank. You're the one watching, not the leaf.
Thoughts come and go. You're here before, during, and after each one of them.
The bank doesn't leave with the current. It stays right where it is, steady, while the water flows by.
[pause 5s]
Maybe the stream is very crowded right now, with leaves everywhere. Or maybe there are long stretches with nothing at all. Either way, just watch the water.
If the current seems to stop, or a leaf gets stuck, notice that. It's often a sign that a small thought is here, the leaf isn't moving. Place that thought on another leaf.
[pause 6s]

[soft, slowing down] Gently, let the image of the stream fade. The leaves carry on their journey without you, and that's just fine.
Come back to your breath. To the places where you're supported. To the weight of your hands.
[pause 2s]
Become aware of the room around you, and the sounds that are here.
Feel the air coming in through your nose, a little cooler, and flowing back out a little warmer.
[pause 3s]
[warm] Has anything changed? Maybe a little more space between you and whatever passes through you. As if your thoughts were stuck to you a little less tightly.
[pause 2s]
You may have had lots of thoughts, and been carried off often. That doesn't mean you failed. Every return to the bank was the exercise.
[gentle] Gently move your fingers, your feet. Stretch if your body feels like it. And open your eyes, in your own time.
[pause 2s]
[warm, clear] Today, when a thought gets loud, remember this little phrase. I notice I'm having the thought that... It's a leaf. You can let it drift.
