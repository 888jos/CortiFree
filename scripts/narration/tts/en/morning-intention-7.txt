# morning-intention-7 · Set your intention (EN)
[pause 1.5s]
[warm, calm] Good morning. Today, we're going to choose a word. Just one. The word that will help you stay yourself, whatever the day has in store.
[pause 2s]
[soft, unhurried] Sit upright, tall without being stiff. You might picture your spine as a stack of coins, balanced one on top of the other.
Rest your hands on your thighs, palms down, or open toward the sky, whichever you prefer.
Let your eyes close, or lower your gaze to the floor, about an arm's length in front of you.
[pause 2s]
Think of this as a small threshold between the night and the day. Before the messages, before the obligations, a moment to choose your direction.
[pause 2s]
[calm] Start by listening to your breath, without changing it. Where do you feel it most clearly this morning?
[pause 2s]
Maybe at the rim of your nostrils, with the cool air coming in. Maybe in your chest as it rises. Maybe in your belly.
Choose that spot, and stay with it for a few breaths.
[pause 4s]
[soft] Feel the small resting moment that comes on its own at the end of the out-breath. No need to stretch it. Just notice it.
If thoughts drift by, the shopping list, an email, yesterday's conversation, let them pass. Come back to the place where you feel your breath. Coming back is the practice.
Let your body settle as well. Feet on the floor, weight on the seat, hands heavy on your thighs. For now, there's nothing else to do but sit here and breathe.
[pause 3s]
[warm, clear] Now I'd like you to ask yourself a question. Not what do I have to do today. You already know that, and your to-do list has it covered.
The question is more like this. How do I want to be, today?
In the way I speak to people. The way I work. The way I respond when something doesn't go to plan.
[pause 2s]
Or put another way. Tonight, as you get into bed, what would you like to be able to say about how you lived this day?
Let the question float. Don't hunt for the answer with your head. Wait for it to come.
This isn't one more goal to hit. It's more like a compass. A gentle direction you can come back to when the day pulls you off course.
[pause 3s]
[soft] Maybe a word has already appeared. If so, welcome it, even if it surprises you.
If not, here are a few suggestions. Listen, and notice which one resonates.
[slow] Patience.
[pause 1.5s]
Presence.
[pause 1.5s]
Courage.
[pause 1.5s]
Gentleness.
[pause 1.5s]
Clarity.
[pause 1.5s]
Lightness.
[pause 1.5s]
Trust.
[pause 2s]
[warm] There's no right or wrong word. What matters is the one that fits what you need today, not the one you think you ought to pick.
And if none of these speak to you, another word may come. Openness. Simplicity. Listening. Or even an image.
You may find yourself torn between two. Choose the one that feels a little kinder when you say it, the one that eases you rather than the one that piles on pressure.
[pause 3s]
[calm, intimate] Do you have your word? Say it to yourself once, slowly.
[pause 3s]
Behind every word, there's often a value. Something that matters to you. If you chose patience, maybe it's because you care about the quality of your relationships. If it's courage, maybe you want to move forward on something important.
Why this word, this morning? What does it protect, or what does it make possible?
[pause 3s]
[soft] Now look for this word in your body. What does patience, or trust, or lightness feel like, physically?
Maybe your shoulders drop a little. Your chest opens. Your jaw unclenches. Your breathing becomes slower, or fuller.
If nothing clear shows up, simply imagine you're already living this word. How would you be sitting? How would you be breathing?
Let your body take the shape of your word. Adjust your posture slightly, if you feel like it.
Notice where the sensation is clearest, and remember that spot. That's where your word lives.
[pause 3s]
[calm, clear] Now think of one specific moment in your day when this word could be useful. Just one.
Maybe a meeting that's likely to drag on, if your word is patience. A conversation you've been putting off, if it's courage. Dinner with your kids or the people you love, if it's presence.
See that moment. The place, the time, the people around you.
[pause 2s]
Then imagine your word walking into the scene with you. It doesn't change anyone else. It changes something small in you. A second's pause before you reply, a softer look, a step forward.
If something difficult comes up in the scene, see yourself coming back to your word, the way you'd reach for the handrail on a staircase. One breath is enough.
Watch how the moment unfolds, with your word by your side. How do you feel at the end?
[pause 3s]
[warm] You didn't need to become someone else. Your word simply helped you stay a little more yourself.
[pause 2s]
[soft, unhurried] Come back to your breath. Let's anchor your word over three out-breaths.
Breathe in easily...
[pause 3s]
and as you breathe out, say your word silently.
[pause 5s]
Breathe in...
[pause 3s]
and on the out-breath, your word again. Let it sink down with the air.
[pause 5s]
One last time. Breathe in...
[pause 3s]
and your word, all the way through the out-breath.
[pause 5s]
[soft, warm] Stay a moment with what's here. The word, the feeling in your body, the quiet of the morning.
[pause 3s]
[warm, reassuring] You don't have to live this word all day long. You'll forget it, that's a given. What matters is coming back to it now and then.
And this word isn't there to judge you. If tonight you feel you lacked patience or courage, that's not a failure. Tomorrow, you can choose the same word, or another one.
[pause 2s]
[warm, clear] To help you remember, give yourself a discreet reminder. Write the word on a sticky note and put it on your screen or your mirror. Make it your phone wallpaper. Or link it to something you do often, like opening a door or drinking water.
Each time you come across that reminder, one breath, and your word.
These small reminders matter. Without them, the morning's word gets lost in the noise of the day. With them, it comes back.
[pause 2s]
[calm] Gently, come back to the room. Move your fingers and toes. Sit up a little taller, and stretch your neck to one side, then the other.
[pause 2s]
[soft, warm, slowing down] Whenever you're ready, open your eyes. Your word is chosen. It's just waiting for you.
