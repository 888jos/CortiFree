[warm, calm] Welcome to this body scan. For the next ten minutes, you're simply going to visit your body, one area at a time, from your toes to the top of your head.

[soft, unhurried] Choose your position. Lying on your back, arms by your sides, legs uncrossed. Or sitting, your back upright and supported, feet flat on the floor, hands resting on your thighs.
Take a few seconds to adjust whatever needs adjusting. A cushion under your knees, a shoulder to shift, a waistband to loosen.

If you'd like, close your eyes. Otherwise, let your gaze rest on a spot on the floor or the ceiling, without staring.
Take a breath a little fuller than the others...

[almost a sigh] and let it go.

[soft] Then another, feeling your body lean a little more into whatever is holding it. Your back, your pelvis, your heels settling down.
[pause 4s]
[calm, clear] Before we begin, an intention. In a body scan, you're not trying to relax at all costs, or to change anything. You observe. You notice what's here, like a curious explorer taking in a landscape without judging it.
You may feel warmth, coolness, pressure, tingling, an itch, heaviness. Or nothing at all. Feeling nothing is an observation too. Whatever you notice is just right.
[gentle] And if your mind wanders off, as it almost certainly will, notice that, without scolding yourself, and come back to whichever part of the body we've reached.
If some part of your body is painful, you can observe it from a distance, gently, or move straight on to the next area. You decide how close to get.
[pause 3s]
[soft] Now let your breathing find its own rhythm. It doesn't need to be slow or deep. It's just as it is.
Simply notice where you feel it most clearly. Maybe at your nostrils, maybe in your chest, maybe in your belly. This breath will keep you company for the whole journey.
[pause 4s]
[calm, unhurried] Bring your attention down to your feet. First your toes. The big toe, then the others, all the way to the little one.
Can you feel them without moving them?
[pause 3s]
Maybe they're touching each other. Maybe you can feel the fabric of your socks, or the air on your skin.
Then the soles of your feet. The padded part beneath your toes, the arch, the heel. If you're sitting, notice the pressure of the floor. If you're lying down, the weight of your heels on the mattress or the mat.
The tops of your feet now, with their fine tendons just under the skin. And your ankles, with their small round bones on either side.
[pause 2s]
[soft, slow] Imagine breathing all the way down into your feet. As you breathe in, your attention travels down to them...
[pause 3s]
As you breathe out, they settle a little more, heavy and still.
[pause 5s]
[calm] Your attention moves up into your legs. Your calves at the back, often a little denser. Your shins at the front, right under the skin.
Your knees. The front, the kneecap. The sides. And the soft hollow behind each knee, a place we often forget about. It may feel a little warmer, a little softer than the rest.
Let your knees release, as if they no longer had to hold your legs together. The joints can open ever so slightly with each out-breath.
[pause 3s]
Then your thighs, the biggest muscles in your body. Feel their weight. The backs of your thighs against the chair or the floor. Maybe the temperature under the fabric of your clothes.
[pause 2s]
Take a moment to compare your left leg and your right leg. Does one seem heavier, warmer, more present than the other?
[pause 3s]
There's no right answer. It's just a way of sharpening your attention.
[gentle] Simply observe. If you notice some tension, you can breathe with it, without demanding that it leave. Breathe in as you look at it, breathe out as you give it a little more room.
[pause 5s]
[calm] Now your pelvis. Your hips, on each side. Your seat, carrying much of your weight. Feel the points of contact, maybe one side pressing down a little more than the other.
Your pelvis is like a bowl that gathers the weight of your upper body. Let it grow heavy, widen, and rest completely on whatever is holding it.
[pause 3s]
[soft] Your belly, now. Notice how it moves with the breath, rising slightly, falling again. Notice too whether it feels tight or relaxed, warm or neutral, whether it's gurgling a little. All of that is part of the landscape.
The belly is often one of the first places stress settles. You don't have to force it to relax. Simply looking at it kindly already gives it a little room.
[pause 4s]
Then your lower back. The area that holds up your posture all day long. Feel the area around your kidneys, the muscles on either side of your spine.
Maybe stiffness, tiredness, warmth. Or a pleasant sense of rest. You don't need to do anything with what you find. You notice it, that's all.
[pause 5s]
[calm, unhurried] Moving up to your chest. Feel your ribcage open a little as you breathe in, and close as you breathe out. Your ribs spreading apart, then drawing together.
[pause 3s]
[soft, intimate] If you can sense it, notice your heartbeat. No need to go looking for it. If it's there, it's there. This heart has been beating for you all along, day and night, without you ever having to think about it.
[pause 4s]
[calm] Then your upper back. The space between your shoulder blades, and the shoulder blades themselves. Feel them against the back of the chair or the floor.
And your shoulders. Where are they right now? High, low, rolled forward?
[pause 3s]
No correcting, just noting. If they decide on their own to drop a little, let them.
[soft, slow] Imagine each in-breath gently filling the space between your shoulder blades, and each out-breath letting your shoulders slide down, a little heavier, a little wider.
A lot of tension quietly builds up here. You're giving it a moment's rest.
[pause 5s]
[gentle] If you feel drowsiness creeping in, that's common when the body settles. You can open your eyes slightly, or take a fuller breath, to stay present.
[pause 2s]
[calm] Your attention now travels down both arms at once. Your upper arms. Your elbows, with their bony points. Your forearms, the skin a little thinner on the inside.
Your wrists. Then your hands. The backs of your hands, your palms. Each finger, from thumb to little finger. Your fingertips, often very sensitive. Maybe a pulse, a buzzing, some warmth.
[pause 3s]
[soft] Let your hands become completely idle. All day long they hold, type, and carry. For now, they have nothing to do.
[pause 5s]
[calm] Move up to your neck. The front, your throat, where the air passes through. The sides. And the back of your neck, which has been holding up your head since this morning.
[pause 2s]
Your face, now. Your jaw. Are your teeth touching, or not?
[pause 2s]
Your mouth, your lips. Your tongue, resting in your mouth.
[soft] If your teeth are touching, let a small gap open between them. Your jaw can grow heavy, as if sinking gently toward your throat. Feel the ease spreading in front of your ears, across your temples.
[pause 3s]
Your cheeks. Your nose, and the air moving through your nostrils, a little cooler on the in-breath, a little warmer on the out-breath.
Your eyes, beneath your eyelids. The tiny muscles around your eyes, which work so hard in front of screens. Your eyebrows. Your forehead, broad and flat.
[slower] Let your forehead widen and smooth out, like a sheet of paper unfolded flat on a table.
[pause 3s]
Then the sides of your head, your ears. The back of your skull, resting or balanced. And finally, the top of your head. Maybe a light sensation, tingling, air, or nothing in particular.
[pause 5s]
[calm, warm] Now widen your attention to take in your whole body at once. From your toes to the top of your head. As if you were stepping back to look at the whole landscape you've just travelled through.
One body, breathing. With its tense areas, its calm areas, its neutral areas. Everything welcomed in the same way.
[pause 3s]
[soft, slow] Feel the breath move through this whole body. On the in-breath, like a wave rising from your feet to your head...
[pause 4s]
On the out-breath, flowing back down to your toes.

[warm, gentle] Notice how this body feels now, compared to the start of the session. Not to judge whether it's better or worse. Just to see.

You've taken ten minutes to listen to it, without asking anything of it. That's a rare thing in a day.

Slowly move your fingers and toes. If you feel like it, stretch your arms, gently roll your head.

Become aware of the sounds around you. The room, the street, the silence.
