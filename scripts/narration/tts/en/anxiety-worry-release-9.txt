# anxiety-worry-release-9 · Set your worries down (EN)

[warm, calm] Some days, your head feels like an overstuffed bag. You've crammed in worries about work, money, health, family, and you carry it everywhere, even to bed.
This moment is here to open that bag, look at what's inside, and sort through it. Keep what you can act on. Set the rest down, at least for a while.

[soft] Get comfortable, sitting or lying down. Let your hands rest wherever they feel good.
You can close your eyes, or let your gaze rest on the floor.
[reassuring] If you're rushed or restless, that's completely fine. You don't need to be calm to start sorting.

[calm] Now let your breath find its own rhythm. Feel the weight of your body, the contact with the seat or the mattress. For the next few minutes, you have nothing else to do but sort, calmly.
[pause 2s]

[clear] This sorting draws on a very old idea, found in the Stoic philosophers as well as in today's therapies. Telling apart what depends on you from what doesn't. It sounds simple, and yet it can lighten the load a lot.
Let your current worries come up. Not to solve them right now. Just to take them out of the bag and set them in front of you.
A first one may show up straight away. Give it a short name. The overdue report. The doctor's appointment. My daughter's grades.
[pause 2s]
Once it's here, notice where it shows up in your body. A weight on your chest, a clenched jaw, a knotted stomach. Each worry often has its own little corner.
[pause 3s]
Then a second. And maybe a third. Big or small, it doesn't matter. A bill, an argument, a loved one's health, the state of the world.
Three or four is enough for today. You don't need to empty the whole bag.
[pause 3s]

[calm, unhurried] Now imagine two circles drawn on the ground in front of you, as if in chalk.
A small circle in the middle. This is what depends on you. Your actions, your words, your choices, the way you take care of yourself.
And around it, a large circle. What doesn't depend on you. Other people's decisions, the past, the distant future, the weather, the economy, what people think.
That large circle is often the one that fills our nights. And it's exactly the one we have the least control over.
[pause 2s]
Take a moment to see these two circles clearly. Their color, their size, the surface they're drawn on.
You can even step into the center of the small circle. Feel your feet inside it. This is your ground, the place where your hands can actually do something.
[pause 3s]

[clear] Pick up your first worry. Hold it in your hands, like an object. And ask yourself one simple question. Is there something I can concretely do about this?
[pause 2.5s]
If yes, place it in the small circle. If not, place it in the large one.
Often, a worry has a bit of both. The doctor's appointment, for example. The result doesn't depend on you, but booking the appointment and preparing your questions does.
In that case, split it in two, and place each piece in its own circle.
A difficult bill, for example. The amount is fixed, but calling to ask for a payment plan is up to you. A loved one's health. You can't control it, but you can call them tonight and tell them you're thinking of them.
[pause 3s]
[calm] Do the same with the second worry. Then the third. Take your time. There are no wrong answers, only your honesty with yourself.
[pause 3s]
Notice what happens when you place a worry in the large circle. Sometimes a small relief, as if you were finally admitting it was never your job. Sometimes resistance. If I stop thinking about it, something bad will happen. And yet, thinking about something is not the same as controlling it.
[gentle] If you're unsure about a worry, set it on the line between the two circles, and come back to it later. The sorting doesn't need to be perfect.
[pause 2s]

[warm, clear] Now look at the small circle. This is where your strength lies.
Choose just one worry in this circle. And ask yourself, what would the next small action be? Not the whole problem. Just the first step.
Sending that email. Jotting down a few ideas on a piece of paper. Calling a friend for advice. Booking a time slot. Sometimes the most useful action is simply going to bed at a reasonable hour.
[pause 2s]
Once you've found it, picture yourself doing it. When, where, how. Feel what it's like to go from I'm worried, to I'm handling it.
If you can, pick a specific moment. Tomorrow at ten, before you open your inbox. The more concrete the step, the more easily your mind can let go of it at night.
[pause 3s]

[calm, unhurried] Now turn toward the large circle. Everything in it, you may have been carrying for days, even weeks. And yet carrying it hasn't changed it.
Let's set it down. Imagine a place for that. A big wooden box by a fireplace, a rowboat tied up at the edge of a lake, a trunk in an attic. Choose whatever speaks to you.
[pause 2s]
[soft] Take the first worry from the large circle. Feel its weight in your hands. Then gently place it there.
You're not throwing it away. You're not pretending it doesn't exist. You're simply recognizing that it isn't yours to hold, right now.
If you chose the box, hear the soft knock of wood as the worry touches the bottom. If it's the rowboat, watch it drift out a little on the still water, held by its rope.
[pause 3s]
And the next one. Set it down. Maybe you notice a slight easing in your arms, your shoulders, your chest.
[pause 2s]
[gentle] If a worry resists, if your hands don't want to let go, that's okay. Set it halfway down. Or tell it, I'll pick you up later, if I need to.
And if it comes knocking again tomorrow, that will be normal. You can set it down again, as many times as it takes.
[pause 2s]
[soft] Once everything is set down, gently close the lid. Or let the rowboat rock at the end of its rope. Your worries are there, sheltered. They don't need you in order to exist.
Take a moment to feel your empty hands. Open them, if you like, palms facing up. They can rest now too.

[calm, soothing] Now come back to your breath. Notice the space around you, now that your hands are lighter.
