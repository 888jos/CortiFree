# morning-gratitude-6 · Morning gratitude (EN)
[pause 1.5s]
[warm, gentle] Hello, and good morning. Settle in just as you are, sitting on your bed, on a chair, or still lying down if you'd rather.
Let your hands rest wherever they're comfortable. Close your eyes, or simply let your gaze drift down toward the floor.
[pause 2s]
[soft] Take one breath that's a little fuller than the others...
[pause 3s]
And as you breathe out, let your shoulders drop a notch.
[pause 4s]
Now let your breathing do whatever it wants. Calm, or a bit quick, it doesn't matter. Just follow it for a few moments.
The air coming in, slightly cool in your nostrils. The air going out, a little warmer.
If thoughts are already drifting in, the day, the time, your list of things to do, let them pass through. You'll get back to them later. For now, just the breath.
[pause 3s]

[warm, clear] Before we begin, a word about why.
Your attention has a natural habit. It spots what's wrong first. The problem to fix, the delay, the criticism. That helps keep you safe, but first thing in the morning, it can tint the whole day gray.
Gratitude is a way of training attention. It doesn't deny what's hard. It simply notices, as well, what's holding you up, all the things that so often go unnoticed.
This morning, I'd like us to look for three thank-yous. Not vague ones. Precise, detailed thank-yous that you can really feel.
[pause 2s]

[gentle] The first one is very simple. Something material, something everyday, that you used this morning or during the night.
Your bed, for example. The mattress that held you up for hours, the duvet that kept you warm.
Or the hot water from the tap. All it takes is turning a handle, and it flows, at just the right temperature.
Choose your thing. Now make it precise. Its texture, its smell, the sound it makes. The exact moment you enjoyed it.
[pause 2s]
Imagine this morning without it, just for a second. A hard bed, a cold shower. Suddenly this small, ordinary thing takes on a different place.
Think of all the people who worked so it could be here. The ones who made it, shipped it, installed it.
[soft, warm] And thank it, silently. A simple thank-you, like the one you'd give someone holding a door open for you.
[pause 3s]

[warm] The second thank-you goes to a person. Someone who matters to you, or someone who simply helped you recently. A loved one, a colleague, the person at the bakery who smiled at you.
Let their face appear. Their eyes, their expression, the way they hold themselves.
If several faces come to mind, choose just one for today. The others will have their turn another morning.
[pause 2s]
Remember a specific moment with them. Something they said, a gesture, a message that arrived at just the right time.
What does this person bring you? Laughter, calm, confidence, a helping hand?
[pause 2s]
Thank them silently, as if they were standing in front of you. You can even imagine their reaction as they hear it.
You don't need to tell them for this thank-you to count. Simply thinking it already shifts the way you see them, and your day, a little.
[pause 3s]

[gentle, intimate] The third thank-you is for you. That may feel strange, and it's often the hardest one. Take your time.
It could be a quality. Your patience, your sense of humor, your curiosity, the way you look after others.
Or your body. Your legs that carry you all day long. Your hands that know how to do a thousand things. Your heart, which has been beating since your very first day, without you ever having to think about it.
Choose just one thing. And thank it, the way you'd thank a loyal friend.
[pause 2s]
Think of a recent moment when it helped you. Yesterday, maybe. One specific situation where this quality, or this body, was there for you.
If a little voice protests or plays it down, that's okay. Notice it, and come back to your thank-you.
[pause 3s]

[soft, warm] Now let these three thank-yous come together. The simple thing. The person. What's in you.
Place a hand on the center of your chest, if you like.
[pause 2s]
What's happening there? Maybe a gentle warmth. A sense of openness, of softness. Maybe a smile finding its way onto your face.
[pause 2s]
Or maybe nothing special, and that's just as good. Gratitude doesn't need to be dramatic to count.
[calm] Research suggests that practicing gratitude regularly may help people feel a little more at ease. But this morning, there's no result to chase. Just this moment.
[soft, unhurried] Breathe with whatever is here. Breathe in, and let your chest open a little...
[pause 3s]
Breathe out, and let that feeling spread toward your shoulders, toward your belly.
[pause 5s]

[soft] Stay here for a few more breaths. Nothing to add, nothing to look for.
The breath carries on, simply. Any warmth in your chest can stay there on its own, without you holding on to it.
[pause 5s]
[warm, clear] Before you get up, choose one of these three thank-yous to carry with you. The one that touches you most this morning.
You can come back to it at some point today. On the train, at your desk, standing in line. It only takes a few seconds.
And if you feel like it, you could even say it out loud. Send a quick message to the person you thought of, for instance.
[pause 2s]
[gentle, encouraging] Gently, come back to the room. Move your fingers, stretch your back a little.
Open your eyes, and look around you. Maybe a fourth thank-you is already waiting for you somewhere.
[pause 4s]
