# compassion-loneliness-8 · Lonely tonight (EN)

[calm, clear] First, something important. Loneliness hurts, and that's no accident. The need for connection is as basic as the need to eat or sleep. When it isn't met, the body registers it as an alarm. What you're going through isn't a flaw. It's a human signal, and a completely legitimate one.

[soft, unhurried] Settle in wherever you are. On the sofa, in bed, against a cushion. If you have a blanket or a hot drink nearby, bring them closer. Your comfort matters.
Let your eyes close, or keep a soft light somewhere in view.

[calm] Notice where your body is in contact with things. The weight of your legs. Your back leaning against something. The texture of the fabric under your fingers. Tonight, something is holding you up, quite literally.
[pause 2s]
Listen to the sounds as well. A car in the distance, the hum of the fridge, maybe rain. The world is still there, all around you.
Let those small sounds keep you company for a moment. You don't have to do anything with them.

[gentle, intimate] Now, if you're willing, look for where loneliness lives in your body. It often has a shape. An emptiness in the chest. A tight throat. A coldness, or a heaviness in the arms.
There's no right or wrong way for loneliness to feel. Whatever you find is welcome.
[pause 3s]
Don't try to make it go away. Just rest your attention on it, gently. As if you were sitting down next to someone who's sad, without saying a word.
You can place a hand there. Feel the warmth of your palm. You're the one offering this touch, and it counts.
[pause 3s]
[soft, slow] Breathe toward that place. Imagine that with each breath in, a little space opens up around the feeling. Like opening a window in a room that's been closed for too long.
[pause 5s]
[gentle] You can tell yourself softly, I feel lonely tonight, and it's hard. Use whatever words feel like yours.
Saying it plainly often softens it a little. You're no longer pretending, and no longer fighting it.

[calm, storytelling] Now widen your view a little. Imagine your street, your neighborhood, your city, seen from above, at night. Thousands of lit windows.
Some dark, some glowing blue from a screen, some warm and yellow.
[pause 2s]
Behind many of those windows, someone is feeling just what you're feeling right now. A student far from home, eating a bowl of pasta in front of a laptop. An elderly man watching television without really seeing it. A young mother rocking her baby at two in the morning, feeling invisible. Someone who's just moved to a city where they don't know a soul.
[pause 2s]
[warm] None of these people know each other. And yet tonight, they share something with you. Loneliness, strangely enough, is one of the most widely shared experiences in the world.
And it says nothing about your worth. It only says that a need is waiting to be met. Even people surrounded by others feel lonely on certain evenings.
You're not the only one feeling this. Let that idea settle.
[pause 4s]

[warm, intimate] Let's send them something. Not a message, not a call. A simple wish, from your heart.
Think of all those lit windows, and silently tell them, may you feel less alone tonight. May someone be thinking of you. May you find a little warmth.
[pause 3s]
And now turn that same wish toward yourself, since you're one of those windows too. May the loneliness weigh a little less tonight. May I find warmth, inside me and around me. May I know that I matter.
[pause 3s]
[reassuring] If these words sound strange, that's normal. Just hold on to the intention, like a candle being lit.
You might picture that small flame passing from window to window, right across the city.
This flame doesn't rest on you alone. Every window that receives it keeps it going in turn. And perhaps, somewhere, someone is thinking of you in the very same way.
[pause 4s]

[warm, soft] Now let a memory of connection come back. A moment when you felt a real bond with someone. It doesn't have to be a big moment. A fit of giggles with a colleague. A noisy family dinner. A long conversation on a train platform. A knowing look shared with a stranger.
[pause 3s]
Relive that moment in detail. Where were you? What were the sounds, the smells, the light? Who was there, and what was being said?
[pause 3s]
And above all, find what you were feeling. That warmth, that lightness, that sense of belonging.
Where does that warmth sit in your body? Maybe in the very same place as the emptiness from earlier. Let it settle there for a moment.
[pause 3s]
Your body remembers this feeling. It's capable of feeling it. And it will feel it again.
Let yourself rest in it for a few breaths, the way you'd lean against someone you trust.
[pause 5s]
[gentle] If this memory also brings some sadness, because that person is far away, or that time has passed, let it be here. Sadness and warmth can live side by side.
[pause 3s]

[calm, clear] Before we finish, think of one tiny act of connection you could do tomorrow. Nothing spectacular.
Send a message to someone you've been thinking about. Really say hello to the person at the bakery, looking them in the eye. Invite a colleague for a coffee. Call a grandparent. Sign up for that activity you've been tempted by for ages.
Choose one. Picture yourself tomorrow, doing it. It isn't much, and that's often how connections start again.
[pause 3s]
If that feels like too much tonight, simply keep the idea in mind. That's already a beginning.
Studies on social connection suggest that it's often these small, everyday interactions, repeated over time, that build a sense of belonging.

Stay a moment longer with your hand resting on you, and your breath coming and going. Notice how you feel now. Without needing the loneliness to be gone.

[warm, reassuring] If it's still here, that's okay. Tonight, you didn't leave it on its own. You kept it company.
You can tell yourself, if it feels right, I'm staying with myself tonight.

[calm] And if this feeling of loneliness comes back often, or weighs very heavily, talking to a health professional can be a real source of support.

[soft] Gently, come back to the sounds of the room, the contact of your body. Move your hands a little.
And whenever you like, open your eyes, or let yourself drift toward sleep if it's time.

[warm, slowing down] Tonight, thousands of people believe, just like you, that they're alone. You're not set apart from anyone. And somewhere, someone will be glad to hear from you tomorrow.
